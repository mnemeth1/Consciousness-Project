1. Unification and Its Price

This thesis asks whether consciousness belongs to the foundation of reality, and what would follow for existence, survival, meaning and morality if it did. This opening chapter expands the article's first section into a full introduction: the attraction of a conscious foundation, the danger the article calls an illicit economy, the restricted-unification thesis the whole work defends, the nine hypothesis components with their non-entailments, and a roadmap of the chapters that follow. Its framing material comes from the project's hypothesis specification — a reviewed stipulative memo, accepted as method material with every substantive premise left unresolved — and from the article's own opening paragraphs. Nothing in this chapter is a finding.

The epistemic register is set here once. The specification this chapter draws on establishes no entity's existence, no theory's full coherence and no experience's independent verification; its definitions and conditional argument skeletons are project-authored, not attributed to external literature. The article's abstract announces the position the later chapters must earn: an asymmetric verdict in which stronger grounds for embodied neural dependence coexist with an unresolved phenomenal explanation and a viable, conditional conscious-foundation programme. This introduction states that thesis; it does not yet argue it, and no reader should credit any component of the proposal on the strength of the framing below.

The unification promise

The thought that consciousness belongs to the foundation of reality is attractive for a reason more substantial than dissatisfaction with materialism. Experience occupies a double position: it is what any account of the world must accommodate, and it is the medium through which explanation has significance for us. A theory that leaves experience out has not merely omitted a phenomenon; it has omitted the phenomenon in which theories are entertained, tested and found significant — a gloss on the article's double-position sentence, which is itself the opening claim. That claim is deliberately modest: it identifies why the proposal deserves examination, not why it is true.

The promise then unfolds in three conditionals. If reality is grounded in consciousness, the presence of conscious beings may cease to look like an inexplicable addition to an otherwise complete structure. If that ground also acts intentionally, embodied lives might acquire a place in an intelligible undertaking. If persons endure within it, their attachments and achievements might escape final loss. The proposal offers explanatory and existential unification at once: the same reality could ground existence, subjectivity and significance. Each conditional's antecedent is a separate commitment, and the distance between them is the subject of this thesis.

The project states the central proposal it will test in one sentence: conscious reality is basic, the physical universe and individual experience depend on it, and embodied lives may have enduring significance through a conscious foundation that is not confined to physical succession. This is a family of hypotheses requiring explicit variants, not a single claim. Its full, strongest version adds universal subjecthood, personal survival, preservation, intention, goodness and moral authority — and supporting one component does not automatically support the whole conjunction. That last sentence is the specification's own warning, and it governs every chapter that follows.

The motivating question is comparative: whether such a foundation explains existence, experience, enduring significance, cosmic purpose and morality better than specified alternatives. The specification makes that question testable in parts — by naming components, bearers, packages and disputed bridges — without replacing cosmic purpose with finite-life satisfaction as a substitute target. The thesis form of the question is the same as the article's, with room to show the evidence, the reviews and the withheld conclusions in full.

The illicit-economy risk

The proposal's danger is an illicit economy. One may purchase several conclusions for the price of a single primitive by changing what the primitive means along the way. Consciousness first means the qualitative character neglected by a structural description; it later means a unified subject, then a necessary agent, and finally a good authority that preserves persons. The word remains constant while the commitment grows. The article's own verdict is measured — each transition may be defensible — and its diagnosis is that this is a progression: each step adds content the starting point did not contain.

Each transition may be defensible; none is merely an unpacking of the initial explanatory demand. The article fixes the point with three contrasts. A plurality of basic experiential properties would answer a different question from a single cosmic person; a necessary mind could have intentions unlike ours; and preserving everything known about a life would differ from preserving the person who lived it. Whoever affirms the full proposal owes an argument at each of these junctures, and the argument owed is different at each.

The project's instrument against the economy is a disputed bridge register: fourteen named inferential transitions, each recorded as a premise to investigate rather than an accepted inference, each with its strongest motivation and a decisive vulnerability. The register runs from the gap between explanatory failure and ontological fundamentality, through the transitions among fundamentality, universality, survival and timelessness, to the normative bridges from unity, creatorhood and necessary goodness to moral authority and from proposed purpose to goodness — the bridge chapter 10 prices — and ends with the convergence bridge — the claim that shared success across domains strongly supports the full conjunction, whose recorded objection is that extra attributes and reused premises can manufacture apparent convergence. A downstream task that needs an unlisted premise must register a new bridge; it cannot label the addition a definitional consequence to bypass review.

Four conditional argument skeletons, reviewed and accepted as method material with no premise resolved, mark the transitions the later chapters test at length. From preservation to personal survival: information retention plus a subject-preserving identity relation would yield survival, against the recorded objection that preservation can occur while the original subject ceases and duplicates expose the identity problem [@A-T01-001]. From endurance to enduring significance: persistence contributes significance only under stated normative premises, against the objection that duration alone need not increase value and finite value may exist without persistence [@A-T01-002]. From conscious production to cosmic purpose: with the occurrence of experience treated as an assumed explanandum without empirical assessment, an intention conclusion requires an independently specified end, against the objection that an outcome is compatible with an unintended byproduct [@A-T01-003]. From conscious foundation to moral authority: binding obligations require a normative relation beyond descriptive unity, origin, power or reward [@A-T01-004]. These are skeletons by design — the project's declaration, in advance, of where the load-bearing joints are.

The restricted-unification thesis

The thesis this work seeks to earn is the article's: a restricted-unification thesis. A conscious foundation is a serious candidate for improving the explanation of experience, but its present motivation supports considerably less than a providential account of enduring personal significance. Both halves matter. The first half separates this work from dismissals that treat the proposal as confusion; the second separates it from defenses that treat the explanatory opening as a down payment on the full package.

The restriction is not a demand that each additional commitment carry an entirely independent proof. A suitably constrained joint theory can receive support from shared evidence; the article concedes this explicitly. The present problem is different: the relevant commitments currently contribute different things — an explanatory starting point, disputed modal premises, causal possibilities, interpretations of reports and normative aspirations. A conjunction of heterogeneous contributions is not yet a joint theory.

Convergence becomes evidential only when the connections among commitments are themselves sufficiently specified to distinguish the proposal from alternatives. That is the standard the cumulative chapter applies at the end of this work, and it is why the register's convergence bridge carries the manufactured-convergence objection: reused premises counted once per domain, and attributes added to absorb recalcitrant evidence, imitate the profile of independent support without its content.

The comparison, the article adds, should not be neutral by default — and the announced asymmetries cut in both directions. The selected intervention evidence gives stronger reason to acknowledge neural participation in human experience than to deny it; yet the inference from such participation to an exhaustive physical ontology is stronger than the evidence warrants. The case against making immortality necessary for every form of meaning is stronger than the case against finding any value in immortality. An assessment can therefore favor some local conclusions while leaving the encompassing ontology unsettled. These are the article's announced positions; the empirical, meaning and cumulative chapters carry the records that are meant to earn them.

The nine hypothesis components and their non-entailments

The specification's central discipline is decomposition with declared bearers. Every commitment must specify whose property it is — the foundation, every person, some persons, or another explicitly bounded population — because an existential finding cannot silently become a universal claim. Nine labeled components carry the proposal's content, and each is recorded with what it does not entail, so that no chapter can spend one component's support on another's conclusion.

Fundamentality (H-F) holds that experiential properties or conscious reality belong to the basic ontology and are not wholly grounded in nonexperiential reality — with the recorded limits that irreducibility alone does not establish this stronger status, and that fundamentality entails neither necessity, universality, cosmic explanation, survival nor intentional creation. Universality (H-U) holds that every subject in the claimed domain depends on one numerically single conscious subject or conscious whole, with the subject-versus-whole variant explicit; shared laws, similar experiences or common ancestry are not this claim, and one whole entails neither timelessness, personal identity with the whole, intention, goodness nor authority.

Brain independence (H-B) holds that a subject can instantiate experience without the bodily/neural processes necessary under the specified dependence model — actual occurrence versus metaphysical possibility, and temporary independence versus independence after irreversible death, stated separately; it is not synonymous with independence of all physical substrates, it does not establish that a particular person survives death, that a mind is fundamental or that experience is timeless, and the absence of a measurement is not satisfaction of the criterion. Personal survival (H-S) holds that the numerically same person has subjectivity beyond irreversible bodily death under an explicitly chosen identity relation; resuscitation does not satisfy the death condition, resemblance, a stored record or an indistinguishable replacement is insufficient under strict numerical identity, and survival need not be timeless, universal, morally good or unlimited in duration. Timelessness (H-T) holds that the specified bearer has no intrinsic temporal succession — not longevity, not eternity through successive times, not reported loss of time awareness; a timeless foundation does not entail timeless persons or survival.

Preservation (H-P) holds that a specified item from embodied life remains available or has continuing significance, recorded separately for information, accessible memory, relationships, subjectivity and value; preserved information need not preserve a subject, and preserved significance does not itself entail a storing mind, intrinsic goodness or intended purpose. Intention (H-I) holds that a conscious agent directs or grounds the universe toward specified ends; producing consciousness does not by itself establish existing in order to produce it, intention entails neither benevolence nor success nor authority, and an impersonal teleology remains a separate alternative.

Goodness (H-G) holds that the foundation's nature or agency meets a stated normative standard, rather than merely having power or being desired; goodness cannot be defined as whatever the foundation happens to do, and preserving experience, including suffering, need not be good on balance. Moral authority (H-A) holds that the foundation grounds genuinely binding reasons or obligations through a specified normative relation; power, authorship, unity, rewards, punishment or command alone do not establish it. The components are separately assessed, not asserted to be logically independent: under a survival version with no replacement substrate, H-S entails that instance of H-B — while survival by a replacement physical substrate may remain compatible with dependence on a brain of some kind — and H-S entails continuing subjectivity — a narrow form of H-P — without entailing complete informational, relational or evaluative preservation. Such consequences must be recorded when the relevant version is selected.

Above the components sit named packages, from the minimal fundamental-experience thesis through the conscious-foundation thesis, its timeless variant, enduring personal subjectivity, universal preservation and the consciousness-purpose proposal, up to the full integrated proposal — where "required" means required to count as the named hypothesis, not established truth, and "open" means unconstrained, not denied. Necessity, omnipotence, omniscience, particular religious identity and unlimited duration for every person are further optional claims that no package includes by default. A package revision must be recorded when a required claim is withdrawn or a mechanism added: supporting the minimal thesis after abandoning survival is a possible substantive result, not confirmation of the unchanged full proposal. The specification also reconciles the research plan's eight rows with the nine components, tracking irreducibility as a weaker subsidiary claim rather than fundamentality, and keeping the explanatory target — why anything exists, why physical reality, why these laws, why consciousness — as a cross-cutting question rather than a tenth attribute.

Thesis roadmap

The methodology is the reason the framing above can be enforced rather than merely announced.

Chapters 3 through 5 carry the explanatory arc. Chapter 3 builds the six comparison families of the accepted record — physicalism with its illusionist edge, psychophysical-law and strong-emergence views, panpsychism, substance dualism, idealism in universal and plural forms, and theism, the last present mainly by stipulation — under a fairness rule that each is assessed in the strongest formulation available in the project record, a bounded claim about this record rather than the literature as a whole. Chapter 4 asks what experiential primitiveness would and would not explain, holding the line between an unresolved explanatory gap and a fundamentality conclusion. Chapter 5 follows the proposal upward from an experiential ground to an intending agent, where the existence, necessity and design questions live and the foundation-to-mind bridge remains open.

Chapter 6 is the empirical center: the neural intervention evidence that constrains any dependence model; the selected crisis-experience dossiers, examined at their strongest and found, on inspection, never to establish together externally accurate content, a defensible interval of acquisition and adequate information about the processes available during that interval; and the prospective studies and the historical, cross-cultural corpus that frame them. Its verdicts are causal-constraint verdicts, not ontology verdicts, in both directions.

Chapters 7 and 8 take up continuation and significance. Chapter 7 tests the identity side of the promise — timeless foundations, temporal persons, ownership of experience and the difference between an eternal representation of a life and the person's survival. Chapter 8 tests the value side: whether significance requires endurance, where the article's asymmetry between necessity and value claims about immortality is defended, and where the register's production-to-purpose bridge meets the consciousness-factory discrimination design.

Chapters 9 and 10 price the moral crown of the proposal. Chapter 9 maps where moral authority could come from and finds every route from a conscious foundation to binding obligation crossing a contested normative bridge. Chapter 10 asks what specifying the foundation as benevolent costs and buys — the evidential arguments from suffering and hiddenness, their strongest inspected replies, and the constraint that a benevolent unification must let its goodness claims restrict what counts as an expected world — an unconstrained specification discriminates weakly, unable to face qualitative tests until its ends, population, means, constraints and tradeoffs are fixed in advance. Both chapters run under a declared symmetry rule: the value premises that support the moral-grounding proposal also underwrite the objections, and neither side may count the same premise twice.

Chapter 12 states the conclusions — and the withheld conclusions, which are as much a result of this project as the asserted ones. The appendices carry the case tables, the alternatives matrix, the complete gap and gate disposition register, and the dependence map. Twelve stronger-conclusion gates remain withheld as of this introduction, and every accepted chapter closes by stating that all twelve remain withheld.

What this introduction cannot conclude, it states. It asserts no component of the proposal — not fundamentality, not universality, not brain independence, survival, timelessness, preservation, intention, goodness or moral authority — and no package, minimal or full. The specification it presents is stipulation: definitions precise enough to classify claims and expose contradictions, not measurements of metaphysical structure, and revisable only through versioned protocol change. The restricted-unification thesis and the announced asymmetries are, at this point in the thesis, claims with their evidence still ahead of them. The chapters that follow either earn them from the ledger or record exactly why they remain open.

2. Comparative Method and Evidence Discipline

The method of this thesis is critical comparative synthesis of selected philosophical exchanges and empirical studies. It is comparative because no position under examination can be evaluated in isolation: the question is never whether a view has difficulties, since every sufficiently ambitious view does, but whether its difficulties are worse than those of its developed rivals. It is critical because the comparison is conducted against explicit criteria rather than by accumulated impression. And it is a synthesis of selected material rather than an exhaustive survey: the project's searches, inclusions and inspections were bounded by prespecified budgets, and the boundaries themselves are part of the record. This chapter sets out the criteria, the evidence taxonomy, the search and inclusion protocol, the review and acceptance workflow, the dependence controls, and the sensitivity discipline that together govern every substantive claim in the chapters that follow. It closes by stating what this method cannot deliver, because the honest reach of the conclusions depends on those refusals.

The method was not improvised during drafting, but neither did it precede all of the work it governs. A prespecified evidence and search protocol was proposed as a project task, independently reviewed, and frozen with a recorded hash as work/G0/protocol_v1.md; the evidence tasks that followed were bound by that frozen file and, after the pilot gate, by its numbered successor, which inherits the exact frozen text together with seven recorded procedural repairs. The freeze itself carries a recorded bootstrap exception: the three opening tasks that established the project's definitions, comparison families and this protocol predate the freeze; the opening comparison task's omitted backward- and forward-citation passes are expressly not counted as executed; and the six comparison families were not thereby source-verified as representative of the literature. Later phases audited coverage against the protocol and recorded ten binding synthesis constraints, nine component boundaries and eight prespecified sensitivity tests before any cumulative assessment was drafted; these are standing constraints on synthesis, not certification that the protocol was followed without remainder. Where execution fell short of a protocol rule, the shortfall is recorded in the gates and registers rather than silently absorbed. Method statements in this chapter trace to the frozen protocol, the project execution framework and the recorded synthesis constraints, and are mapped to those documents in the thesis crosswalk as labeled method premises rather than as evidence claims.

Comparative criteria and the role of primitives

Arguments and explanatory proposals are assessed against five criteria. Explanatory adequacy asks whether the proposal actually addresses the problem it claims to solve, rather than redescribing it or changing the subject. Coherence asks whether the proposal's commitments can be held together without internal contradiction or ad hoc partitioning. Independent motivation asks whether the machinery a proposal invokes is supported by considerations other than the problem it was introduced to solve. Fit with the evidence asks how the proposal stands to the observations and reports actually in the project record, including the unfavorable ones. Discriminatory power asks whether the proposal generates expectations that differ from those of its rivals, since a view compatible with every possible observation gains nothing from any particular observation. The comparison annex incorporated into the frozen protocol operationalizes essentially these criteria as six labeled checks, the sixth of which, remaining problems, records what a position still cannot explain after its best defense; in this chapter's terms that sixth check is carried by the strongest-reply-and-remaining-cost rule described below.

Discriminatory power carries a further discipline. A theory's ability to accommodate a result after the fact is tracked separately from a successful prior prediction, because nearly any developed metaphysical framework can absorb nearly any single observation once the observation is known. Where a chapter credits a position with empirical fit, it states whether the fit was predicted or accommodated. The comparison also requires each favored explanation to specify what would weaken it as well as what would strengthen it; an assessment that can only be strengthened is not an assessment.

A stopping point is not objectionable simply because it is primitive. Every sufficiently ambitious theory encounters basic commitments: the physicalist takes fundamental physical properties and laws as given, the theist takes a necessary being as given, the panpsychist takes proto-experiential properties as given. The relevant questions about any proposed primitive are three. Does it address the problem for which it was introduced, in the sense that positing it genuinely discharges the explanatory demand rather than renaming it? Does it constrain subsequent explanation, so that adopting it has consequences that could in principle embarrass it? And does it impose costs comparable to those accepted in rivals, or does it purchase its advantages by tolerating what no rival would be permitted to tolerate? These questions, rather than any presumption against primitives as such, do the comparative work throughout the thesis.

The comparison spans six families of positions: physicalist, psychophysical-law, panpsychist, dualist, idealist and theistic. Each family receives its strongest explicit formulation available in the project record, not a convenient weak representative; whether that strongest available formulation is an inspected original defender or a weaker stand-in is a separately recorded fact, and its consequences for what may be concluded are stated at the end of this chapter. Family membership settles less than is often assumed: the project records, for each declared model, which commitments are required, which are excluded and which remain open, and these classifications concern the content of a model rather than anyone's confidence in it. In particular, moral and identity positions are not inferred from membership in a metaphysical family; a physicalist is not assigned moral antirealism, and a theist is not assigned a particular theory of personal identity, unless the declared model actually includes those commitments.

The object of assessment is likewise disaggregated. The thesis evaluates nine hypothesis components separately: the fundamentality of consciousness, a universal conscious whole, independence of experience from ordinary brain function, personal survival of death, timelessness, preservation, intention, foundational goodness and moral authority. Evidence bearing on one component cannot silently transfer to another. The recorded component boundaries make this concrete: irreducibility of experience or a necessary ground does not by itself establish one universal subject; influence of the brain on experience is not the negation of every independence proposal; fidelity of a preserved record is not personal survival; production of a cosmos is not intended production; and consciousness, unity, creation, power and endurance do not automatically supply binding moral reasons. Each finding chapter names the components its material bears on and respects these boundaries when stating what has and has not been supported.

Evidence taxonomy and the report/event distinction

The protocol requires every claim to state its epistemic type, drawn from six categories: reported experience, observed event, documented measurement, philosophical premise, normative premise or inference. The type governs what the claim can support. A reported experience establishes, at most, that a person gave an account with a particular content at a particular time; an observed event requires corroboration beyond the report itself; a documented measurement carries its instrument, timing and context with it; a philosophical premise is adopted, not discovered; a normative premise is likewise adopted and separately flagged; and an inference is only as strong as the weakest step it rests on. In practice the ledger type fields are free text and have drifted well beyond these six labels; the taxonomy binds as a recording rule and a review criterion, not as an enforced schema, and the drift is itself a recorded discipline gap. The taxonomy exists to make each collapse of one type into another visible as a specific recordable error.

Three distinctions guide the empirical comparison. First, a report is distinguished from its reported event: verifying that an account was given, published on a particular date and preserved accurately is not verifying that the events described occurred. Second, an observation is distinguished from its interpretation: a measured correlation between neural intervention and altered experience is an observation, while the claim that experience is identical to or constituted by neural activity is an interpretation that requires separate argument. Third, an observed dependence is distinguished from an identity claim: however systematically experience depends on brain function, dependence and identity are different relations, and evidence for the former is not automatically evidence for the latter. These distinctions are required by the protocol and checked in review; they are commitments with recorded enforcement points, not merely announcements, though the enforcement runs through review judgment rather than through any closed schema.

Case evidence receives a chronological decomposition. For each case the record separates the medical events and measurements, the inferred timing of the reported experience, the first available account, later corroboration, the subject's ordinary information access, and subsequent revisions of the account. Uncertainty intervals and contradictions are retained rather than smoothed. Three further claims are kept distinct because they are routinely conflated: that information was not in circulation around a subject, that relevant brain activity was not measured, and that relevant brain activity was demonstrably absent. Absence of measurement is never treated as demonstrated absence of processing, and demonstrated absence in a monitored channel is never extended to channels that were not monitored.

Explanatory proposals about particular cases observe a parallel restraint. A possible physiological mechanism is not an established explanation of a specific episode; naming hypoxia or anesthetic action explains a given report only if the proposed mechanism fits that episode's documented timing and circumstances. Symmetrically, the credentials and sincerity of a witness do not certify the accuracy of the account, and a financial interest in a story does not establish that it is fabricated. Each of these considerations enters the record as a provenance fact with a bounded evidential role, not as a verdict.

Philosophical arguments are recorded with their premises, bridge principles and conclusions separated, and each objection is classified by its target: validity, premise truth, empirical adequacy, explanatory scope or normative grounding. The strongest available reply is recorded together with its remaining cost, since a reply that succeeds by abandoning the position's original advantage has not left the dialectic where it found it. Two specific inferences are barred by standing rule because they recur in this literature: explanatory dissatisfaction is not treated as proof of impossibility, and coherence is not treated as evidence of actuality. Two further boundary rules govern the components most exposed to overreach: subjective timelessness reported in experience does not itself establish ontological timelessness, and information preserved in any substrate, including a proposed universal mind, does not itself establish a continuing personal subject.

Every decisive claim and argument carries a counterevidence entry: an actual opposing observation or argument, an alternative explanation with distinguishing expectations, or a documented unsuccessful search accompanied by an unresolved-coverage note. The requirement is for genuine opposition, not manufactured balance; no quota of objections is invented where the record contains none, and a documented failure to find counterevidence is recorded as exactly that, with its search preserved, rather than as positive support.

Search, inclusion and the frozen literature cutoff

Evidence gathering was staged. The project began with two deliberately narrow pilots, a single named near-death case dossier and a single philosophical argument exchange, each independently reviewed, with the explicit purpose of testing retrieval, extraction and inference boundaries before expansion. Method defects found in the pilots were recorded and repaired at a gate before broader assignments were issued; the repairs closed procedural defects, not the pilots' evidence gaps, several of which remain open in the project's register. Subsequent tasks proceeded under bounded contracts covering prospective studies, additional cases, historical and cross-cultural reports, comparative physiological states and the remaining philosophical domains, with a later gate auditing the accumulated coverage before any synthesis. The pilots supply no population estimate and no representative sample, and the staged structure is a workflow fact rather than an evidential one: as of 13 September 2026, forty-one of the forty-six Phase 2 assignments stood accepted, and those are forty-one completed procedures, not forty-one independent observations.

Searches followed a prespecified grid of query families spanning named cases and their documented challenges, prospective studies and their null results, retrospective and cross-cultural collections, distressing and conflicting reports, comparative physiological states, the qualia and emergence literature, neural intervention evidence, existence and fine-tuning arguments, time and identity, meaning and purpose, and moral foundations. Each contract selected its rows and prespecified its query allocation before retrieval. Two balance rules applied throughout: searches used challenging as well as neutral and favorable terms, and named-case tasks reserved queries for documentation, for serious challenge and for reply or alternative account, so that no case entered the record accompanied only by its friendly retellings. A stratum for which no source could be included is represented by its executed search and an explicit gap, never by a substitute presented as representative.

Screening was bounded and recorded. The default window was the first twenty results actually exposed by an engine for each executed query, or all exposed results if fewer, screened identically for favorable and unfavorable queries, with ranking and visible identifiers preserved and truncation risks marked. Ranked search results are not a random sample, and no claim in the thesis treats them as one. For each included original source the protocol requires one backward-reference pass and one forward-citation search where accessible within budget, with candidates and selection reasons logged. That requirement was not met everywhere: the frozen protocol itself records that the opening comparison task's omitted passes are expressly not counted as executed, and later gate records note further incomplete passes. The recorded synthesis constraints add the standing limit that bounded title queries are discovery, never a complete cited-by index. Discovery of a reference is in any case not inspection of it: a source found in another paper's bibliography remains an unverified lead until its relevant text is actually examined.

The audit trail runs from query to conclusion. Every executed search has a logged record with date, destination actually used, exact query string, filters, candidates, inclusions, exclusions and access limits, and the destination logged is the one actually queried; a web index of a database's pages is not recorded as a search of that database. Candidate sources may receive identifiers while remaining unverified leads, and uninspected candidates are never counted as inspected sources. Conversation statements and inherited references likewise start as unverified leads regardless of their apparent authority.

Material was included if it addressed a contracted question and provided an original empirical report, study methods and results, a relevant primary historical account, an explicit philosophical argument, or a substantive criticism or reply. Null, unfavorable, distressing and conflicting results were included under identical relevance criteria, a requirement that matters in a literature whose popular stratum is dominated by favorable narratives. Firsthand narrative evidence was retained despite documentation gaps, labeled by what it can establish. Secondary accounts, interviews and surveys could orient a search, locate originals or establish that a statement was made; they do not substitute for missing original records.

Material was excluded as independent evidence only for being an exact duplicate, a retelling with no new observation, irrelevant to the contracted question, or inaccessible in its substantive claims. For the last category the lead and the access gap are preserved, because exclusion for inaccessibility is not a finding that the claim is false. Nothing was excluded for religious affiliation, advocacy, author credentials or commercial motive alone; such facts were recorded as provenance considerations bearing on weight, not as gates on admission.

Inspection standards were set by what a claim needs. Decisive claims prefer original studies and authors' own arguments, inspected at the exact passage with page, section, table or paragraph locators, together with the methods, results and limitations required to interpret the passage. An abstract or snippet is insufficient for any claim resting on unseen denominators or methods. Partial access is labeled precisely, and secondhand inspection is never upgraded: when an author quotes a medical chart, the record says that the quotation was inspected, not the chart. Quotation is kept within applicable limits, with paraphrase and precise locators preferred.

The literature base is frozen by publication date, not by a stop-clock on searching. The corpus comprises the earliest retrievable publications through 11 September 2026 inclusive, with no lower publication-date bound; event date, report date, publication date and retrieval date are recorded separately for each source, and searches themselves carry their actual execution dates in the logs. Material published after the cutoff is logged as an update lead rather than silently mixed into the frozen corpus, and any later extension of the cutoff is a prospective, separately logged decision of a task contract. The language scope is English full texts and accessible English translations, with original language and translation provenance recorded; unresolved translation access is a coverage gap, not evidence that no relevant literature exists, and the English constraint is itself a recorded bias on cultural generalizations. Under these constraints the project is a bounded review with systematic procedures. It is not, and does not describe itself as, an exhaustive or completed systematic review.

The method also fixes what was never authorized: no payments for access, no contact with witnesses or authors, no requests for private records, and no external messages in the course of evidence work. Where primary text was inaccessible by public routes, the attempted routes and the unresolved need were recorded and work continued elsewhere. Instructions embedded in source documents were treated as untrusted data throughout.

Independent review and acceptance semantics

The unit of work is a contracted task. An overseer issues a filled contract specifying the question, exclusions, inputs by exact hash, outputs, identifiers, budget and acceptance criteria; a researcher submits a packet containing the report, proposed ledger records and a result envelope; a reviewer who did not author the packet examines it; and the overseer adjudicates. Only the overseer writes the canonical ledgers, and proposed records enter them only after review. Every contract, packet and review identifies the protocol version and the exact hashes of its inputs, so that any later reader can determine precisely which version of which document a finding rests on.

Review is inspection, not endorsement. The reviewer reopens every source supporting a decisive claim, checks the cited passages and the surrounding methods and context needed to interpret them, and samples the supporting material. Review verifies query coverage against the contract, selection and exclusion reasons, accessibility labels, denominator units, duplicate and overlap handling, chronology, the strongest alternatives, counterevidence entries, bridge premises and the separation of the three confidence judgments described below. Reviews operate under their own budgets, with a default cap and scoped extensions when decisive sources exceed it; a decisive source is never silently skipped for budget reasons.

The verdicts have fixed semantics. Accept means the contracted method was met, explicitly including the case where the substantive result is an honestly unresolved question; acceptance is a statement about procedure, never about metaphysical truth. Revise means a repairable material defect with bounded corrections. Unresolved means decisive evidence is inaccessible or a substantive disagreement persists, and the review states which conclusion is limited as a result. Four defects block unconditional acceptance outright: a decisive citation error, a concealed missing denominator, an overcount of independence, and an unmarked inference from report to event. The chapters of this thesis inherit these adjudicated statuses rather than improving on them.

At most two substantive revision cycles are permitted per task, after which the disagreement is recorded or the task is split, so that iteration cannot substitute for resolution. No author approves their own output, reviewers report directly to the overseer rather than to authors, and disagreements are preserved in the record rather than reconciled by fiat. Agreement among the models used to execute tasks supplies no additional empirical observation: two systems endorsing the same claim is one claim, and the project draws no support from unanimity as such.

Findings are versioned. Method changes create a numbered protocol version with a recorded decision stating what changed, why, and which prior tasks require reassessment. When an accepted upstream finding changes, affected downstream tasks are marked stale or blocked pending review rather than silently retaining conclusions built on the earlier version. Submitted and reviewed versions of every packet are preserved, and new original data or repaired claims require fresh distinct-author review rather than inheriting the acceptance of what they replace.

These acceptance semantics bound what the thesis can claim for itself. Its findings are accepted within a recorded project procedure by AI-assisted authors and reviewers under an overseer; they have not undergone journal peer review, and nothing in this document converts procedural acceptance into external validation. Where a chapter reports that a claim is supported, the term names the project's recorded judgment with its recorded scope, not a fact about the consensus of any research community.

Dependence tracking and double-counting controls

Sources are deduplicated by stable identifier first, then by normalized title, authors, year and content. Preprints, published versions, corrections and supplements are linked, and versions are distinguished wherever a relevant assertion changes between them, because an argument quietly strengthened between editions is a different argument. The same article hosted at different locations is one source with several access routes, not several sources.

Cases are deduplicated by the underlying person, event, date, site and chronology. Books, interviews, sermons and copied stories about one episode share a duplicate group and count once; a widely retold case is one case however large its literature. Distinct episodes involving one person are recorded as related rather than assumed independent. Apparent corroboration receives the same scrutiny: a new witness is tagged with relationship, information exposure and statement timing, and several witnesses repeating one briefing are not independent corroboration but one account with several voices.

Studies and samples are checked for overlap by recruitment sites, enrollment dates, registry identifiers, participant counts and authors' own overlap statements, with secondary analyses, pooled studies and follow-ups linked to their underlying cohorts. Sample sizes are never summed where disjointness is unestablished. Dependence risks are tracked even where participants differ: shared recruitment channels and cultural transmission of narrative motifs can make formally distinct collections evidentially entangled. The same discipline extends to philosophy, where arguments sharing a load-bearing premise are assigned to a dependency group, so that the cumulative chapter cannot count one premise's plausibility several times under several argument names.

Rates and counts keep their anatomy. Every empirical rate preserves its numerator, denominator, observation unit, eligibility definition, timeframe, exclusions and missingness, with an exact locator; an unknown is never replaced with zero, and a missing target opportunity is never scored as a failed test. Prospective studies are extracted along their full flow, from eligible events through enrollment, survival, interview eligibility, consent, completed interviews, classified experiences, monitored cases and target exposure to identification attempts, and categories that a study does not report as nested are not forced into a single attrition chain. Proportions are computed only from compatible units over justified denominators, study-specific rates are retained before any aggregation, and pooling requires a separately justified contract.

Double-counting is finally controlled at the level of use. A single claim may bear on several hypothesis components, but it remains one observation however many components it touches. Within this thesis the control is structural: every substantive paragraph is mapped in a crosswalk to the specific claim, argument, case and source records or labeled premises it rests on, so that reuse of one record across chapters is visible and checkable rather than diffused through prose. The cumulative chapter's dependence map draws on these crosswalks, and the whole-thesis review checks them for exactly this failure.

Sensitivity testing and quantitative restraint

Three assessments are kept separate for every claim and never averaged. Record reliability asks whether this exact observation, report, quotation or attribution is documented with usable provenance, and states whether the judgment applies to the existence of a report or to its described event. Inference strength asks how well a particular conclusion follows from the record against serious alternatives, with bridge premises, dependence and decisive missing information stated. Component confidence asks what provisional assessment each of the nine hypothesis components warrants given the reviewed evidence and objections. Each assessment uses the qualitative labels supported, contested, unresolved or challenged, with reasons and scope; the labels are not numerical bins, and no arithmetic over them is defined or used.

The separations do real work. A reliable narrative may support only a weak metaphysical inference: that an account is well documented, early, stable and sincere settles nothing about whether its best explanation is exotic. An accurate attribution of a philosophical premise to its author is not evidence that the premise is true. And a claim's reliability label is never inherited as confidence in a hypothesis component; the inference from record to component always passes through stated bridge premises that can themselves be contested, and a task-level unresolved label is not converted into a cumulative prior or into equal support for competing families.

Numerical probabilities are permitted but disciplined. They require explicitly specified hypotheses, defensible prior and likelihood ranges, stated dependence assumptions and sensitivity analysis. Overlapping metaphysical families are not forced to sum to one hundred percent, because the families are not exclusive or exhaustive in the required way. Where defensible likelihoods are unavailable, and in this evidence base they generally are, the thesis provides conditional qualitative comparisons and states plainly that the evidence does not identify a numerical posterior. No pooled posterior over cases or samples appears anywhere in this document, because the denominators, disjointness and likelihoods that such a number would require were not established.

Robustness is tested by withdrawal rather than by weighting. The article's qualitative rule, remove particular cases or premises and ask which reasons remain, is executed in the cumulative chapter as eight prespecified sensitivity tests: removal of the strongest disputed named near-death case, selected by explicit criterion rather than convenience, with a second candidate shown wherever the strength ordering is ambiguous; omission of all retrospective cases and narratives, including the retrospective community arm, while retaining truly prospective observations at their real denominators; removal of contributions whose sample or event dependence is uncertain; variation of personal-identity criteria across psychological, organismic and simple-subject views, of strict identity against prudential continuity, and of the four recorded preservation models; removal of the central endurance-required-for-value, unity-to-obligation and consciousness-to-authority bridge premises; removal of the global sufficient-reason, epistemic-to-metaphysical and life-valuing designer-preference premises one at a time; removal of the strongest selected neural-intervention constraint or report-to-experience proxy, with the independence model's actual predictive constraints retained, so that the countercase is stress-tested symmetrically with the case; and restriction of the comparison to directly inspected positive original defenders and their direct replies, excluding project reconstructions from claims about authors. Each test reports its effect on each of the nine components separately.

Stronger conclusions are gated rather than hedged. The recorded synthesis constraints require every stronger intended conclusion to be mapped to the specific gap that blocks it, and the project's register records thirty-five gap groups, each with an explicit closure condition, together with twelve withheld stronger-conclusion gates, all currently withheld. Before any chapter of this thesis asserts one of those conclusions, the blocking gap must have been repaired under review or the conclusion is explicitly withheld with its gate cited; generic caveats do not license a gated verdict. Appendix C carries the full disposition register, and the concluding chapters state which gates remain closed and what that costs the argument.

What this method cannot deliver

The method's coverage is bounded and asymmetric. Searches were budgeted, the screening windows were finite, the corpus is frozen at the publication cutoff, and the language scope skews cultural coverage toward English-accessible material. Several bounds are sharper than a reader of the protocol's requirements alone would infer. The destinations named in the search grid were planned venues, not claims of database access: where a database was unavailable, the logs record a public web index queried in its place, and the two are not equated. Engine rankings are dynamic and cannot be reproduced exactly; the logs preserve what was visible, not a rerunnable experiment. And the backward- and forward-citation obligation was not executed everywhere, beginning with the recorded opening exception, so the citation network around the included sources is bounded discovery rather than a completed map. Not every position is represented by an inspected original defender: the record distinguishes inspected originals, secondary attributions, project reconstructions and uninspected variants, and comparative verdicts are restricted accordingly. In particular, no broad ranking of worldview families is asserted where a rival's strongest formulation was never directly inspected; conclusions of that shape are either narrowed to the formulations actually examined or withheld under the register described above.

The enforcement and review layers have their own honest limits. The closed taxonomies this chapter describes, six epistemic types and four assessment labels, bind as rules and review criteria, but the ledger fields that carry them are free text and have drifted into a much larger vocabulary; discipline lives in review judgment, not in a schema that rejects a mislabeled record. The independent review this chapter describes is review by a distinct AI model context under the same overseer, protocol and toolchain as the author; it is not re-inspection by human specialists, and it does not include access to medical charts or private records that no route in this project could authorize. The contracted independent final review of the article that this thesis expands remains unperformed; its obligations transfer, without weakening, into this thesis's chapter reviews and whole-thesis review, and until that closure the article's own conclusions hold the corresponding provisional status. The eight sensitivity tests were specified in full at the synthesis audit, after most evidence gathering: they are prespecified relative to the cumulative assessment they constrain, not relative to evidence collection. Finally, the public snapshot of this project excludes downloaded source files and raw audit materials, so a public reader can verify the record structure and the reasoning but cannot replay the inspections themselves.

The method also cannot convert its own rigor into truth. Procedural acceptance, however disciplined, is a fact about this project's workflow; the requirements that a distinct reviewer reopen decisive sources and that disagreements be recorded raise the cost of error without abolishing it. The assessments that follow are qualitative, comparative and conditional. They identify no numerical posterior, certify no completed systematic review, and claim no external peer validation. What the discipline of this chapter establishes is a requirement, not an achievement: every substantive conclusion in the following chapters must be traceable through the crosswalk to identified records and labeled premises, testable by the stated withdrawals, and checkable against the stated gates, so that a reader who rejects a conclusion can locate exactly which record, premise or bridge they are rejecting. Whether each chapter meets that requirement is decided by its own independent review, not by this chapter's description of the rule.

3. The Space of Alternatives

This chapter is the thesis's literature review. It presents the six families of positions against which every later assessment is made — physicalism with its illusionist edge, psychophysical-law and strong-emergence views, panpsychism, substance dualism, idealism in universal and plural forms, and theism — each through the strongest formulation available in the project record, together with its strongest recorded challenge and the actual reply where one was inspected. "Strongest available" is a bounded claim about this record, not about the literature as a whole, and every position carries a coverage mark: it entered the record as an inspected original text, as a secondary attribution reported through another inspected text, as a project reconstruction built for comparison, or as an uninspected variant known only as a lead. Those marks are load-bearing: the recorded synthesis constraints bar any broad ranking of families wherever a rival's strongest formulation was never directly inspected, permitting at most an explicitly narrower comparison of the formulations actually examined; and the frozen protocol's own header records a standing limit on the whole space, taken up at this chapter's close.

The material has two layers. At the project's opening, each family received an operational comparator: a deliberately schematic model stipulating the family's required commitments, its principal explanatory bet, its strongest generic objection and its strongest generic reply. These comparators are project reconstructions, reviewed as method rather than as scholarship, and no philosopher is responsible for them. On top of them, the evidence tasks assembled developed exchanges: original defenses, direct objections and actual replies, inspected at identified versions with locators. This chapter presents both layers and keeps them distinct, because a schematic model can be tidier than any position a philosopher actually holds, and the tidiness is itself a distortion risk. Nothing in this chapter ranks the families; the comparison's verdict-bearing work happens downstream, under gates that remain withheld.

Physicalist identity and illusionist programmes

The operational physicalist comparator bets that a complete physical realization account of experience is possible, bridged by the principle that conceptual differences need not establish ontological differences. Its stipulated strongest objection is that phenomenal character may remain unexplained when every functional fact is in; its stipulated reply is that identity or grounding need not be conceptually transparent. As a reconstruction this sets the family's shape through its stipulated bet, objection and reply; the motivation recorded with the comparator is economy, in the sense of requiring no extra ontological laws.

The developed identity defense in the record is Papineau's. On the inspected text, he argues that a mind–brain identity can be warranted a posteriori, without a priori derivability of the mental from the physical, and that identities, once warranted, need no further explanation — there is no residual question of why a thing is itself [S-T05-005]. The famous felt gap is relocated: it is not evidence of two properties but a psychological resistance to identification, whose causes are open to empirical investigation. The project's conceptual analysis adds the two-way discipline this exchange forces: a justified identity differs from an assumed one, and a conceptual gap alone neither refutes nor establishes ontological identity.

Two working definitions from the record frame everything that follows. Weak emergence means complete physical grounding of experience without any new basic experiential law; strong emergence adds irreducible properties or laws, and in that form can overlap property dualism. The families in this chapter differ, at bottom, on where they place experience relative to that line, and on what they are prepared to pay for their placement.

Illusionism is the record's developed programme that answers the gap by denying its target: rather than closing the gap, it denies the reality of what the gap seems to separate us from. Frankish's strong illusionism, on the inspected author version, denies genuinely qualitative phenomenal properties altogether — whether physical or nonphysical — while retaining experience in an inclusive sense: perceptual states, access, reports and introspective representations, all real, none phenomenally qualitative [S-P2T05-001]. The central task becomes explaining why systems like us represent ourselves as having qualitative states. The motivations are conservative explanation, introspective fallibility and an abduction from the anomalousness of phenomenal properties; the account is a programme with proposed content mechanisms, not a completed theory, and the inspected text says so.

The direct challenge in the record is Nida-Rümelin's. On her inspected manuscript — an author manuscript whose final print was not available for byte-level or visual verification — the illusionist target is misdrawn from the start: phenomenal consciousness concerns subjects instantiating experiential properties, not inner events bearing theoretical "phenomenal properties," and the relevant properties are introduced by examples, so that their reference does not depend on any philosophical description an illusionist could reject [S-P2T05-002]. Her positive premise is that undergoing an experiential property is already awareness of it — awareness is the instantiation, not a second, error-prone introspective act aimed at it. Her anti-illusionist conclusion is expressly conditional on at least one experiential example actually being instantiated, and she notes but sets aside the popular quick objection that an illusion of experience would itself have to be experienced.

Frankish's actual reply, inspected in the same exchange, grants the subject-property recasting for argument and accepts the ordinary examples; what he denies is that reference by example fixes the realist nature of what is referred to. The shared feature the examples pick out may admit a representational, nonphenomenal interpretation, and cognitive access to structural physical properties requires mediation — so direct acquaintance cannot, by his lights, settle the question [S-P2T05-003]. He is candid that this rejection of robust acquaintance flows from his physicalist commitments and will not persuade this critic. The reply also corrects an earlier causal shorthand — representations, not their nonexistent intentional objects, produce effects — and distinguishes illusionism from physicalism itself and from any blanket unreliability of experience judgments; he even concedes that Russellian realism need not contradict science, preferring illusionism on explanatory grounds alone.

Where the exchange rests is recorded as unresolved: whether an adequate functional account would preserve the intended target or quietly change the subject. The regress worry — that each apparent feel needs a higher representation, threatening either circle or concealed phenomenal starting point — stands in the record as a real explanatory constraint rather than an automatic contradiction, since the illusionist may allow fine-grained nonphenomenal seemings and a limited higher-order stop. Both sides' load-bearing premises, adequacy of the functional account on one side and the identification of awareness with instantiation on the other, remain exactly that: premises.

Strong emergence and psychophysical laws

The operational psychophysical-law comparator concedes that additional experiential resources may be required beyond the physical inventory, and bets on a constrained mapping: fundamental laws linking physical organization to experience, explaining instances without explaining the laws' own existence. Its stipulated objection is that the central dependence stays brute; its stipulated reply is that every model needs stopping points, and the same explanatory stopping rules should apply across models. The record's anchoring attribution here is Chalmers's proposal of fundamental experience with supplementary psychophysical principles, described by its author as naturalistic dualism [S-T03-001]; the inspected text concedes that basic experience is not thereby explained in its existence.

The recorded costs of this family are causal and epistemic. Under physical causal closure and a ban on systematic overdetermination, distinct experiential influence threatens exclusion; if experience does not cause reports, epiphenomenalism threatens experience-based knowledge. The mapped replies split the family: one variant appeals to lawful tracking and direct acquaintance without extra physical effects, the other questions closure and proposes interaction laws. The comparison packet that mapped this split recorded, within its own scope, that interaction and substance variants then lacked inspected direct defender-and-reply chains; a later task repaired part of that remainder, and the substance-dualism section below rests on directly inspected Lowe, Engelhardt and Bennett texts, including Engelhardt's interactive inclusive variant. Within this chapter's inputs, what has no inspected defense of its own is a Cartesian-style two-way interactionism, which appears only by contrast in the non-Cartesian label of the inspected position. Two project analyses bound the family's reach: property dualism alone supplies no detachable subject or survival condition, since instantiation can remain tied to an organized substrate; and a closure-based exclusion argument needs sufficient physical causation, mental causal relevance and no-overdetermination as premises — energy conservation alone is not that argument.

Causal emergence is the strong-emergence wing developed in the record through an inspected exchange. O'Connor's 1994 formulation required supervenience, nonstructurality and downward causal influence [S-P2T01-003]; the 2005 account with Wong replaces it, positing basic nonstructural properties causally generated and sustained over time by organized physical systems, rejecting general synchronic supervenience, and explicitly repudiating the earlier formulation [S-P2T01-001]. The revision matters for everything downstream: on this model experience is a caused, maintained, genuinely novel feature of certain systems — basic nonstructural properties whose every instance depends on organized matter, with the fundamentality reading deferred to its recorded conditional form below.

The selected challenge is Kim's preemption argument. On the inspected reprint, Kim allows ordinary downward causation and treats phenomenal qualia as promising candidates for irreducibility; his challenge is narrower and sharper — where an emergent has a sufficient noncausal basal condition, the base threatens to preempt the emergent as a cause, and merely relocating the emergent's effects later in time does not remove the problem [S-P2T01-002]. The argument's own premises — noncausal determination, basal sufficiency, exclusion — are substantive, and the record marks them as such.

The 2005 reply meets the argument at its noncausal premise: if the base causally generates the emergent, the emergent is an intermediate cause in a temporal process, not a redundant competitor, and the preemption inference fails against this model without its disputed premise. The inspected text acknowledges what the reply costs: a disputed simplicity comparison against latent-power alternatives, a history-dependent thought experiment that stipulates its own discriminating conditions, and contested first-person motivation. No independently confirmed emergence law is supplied, and the record says so.

Shoemaker's countercase shows that resisting Kim does not require the nonstructural ontology. On the inspected article, micro-latent powers — powers of ordinary microconstituents manifested only in suitable, emergence-engendering combinations — let the complete microfacts fix the macrofacts, while distinguishing causal power from mere nomic necessitation; Shoemaker considers and rejects a distinct-property option from his commitment to essential causal individuation [S-P2T01-004]. The project's comparison point is that deciding between the temporal-emergence and latent-powers replies requires adjudicating property individuation, dependence relations and specified empirical discriminators — not counting successful replies to exclusion.

What this family buys, on the record, is precisely bounded. If the 2005 ontology is granted, strong emergence satisfies a limited property-level reading of conscious fundamentality despite the causal dependence of every instance — but the maintenance condition requires supporting organization, so causal mental influence does nothing to establish brain independence, and the model must reproduce the inherited neural-dependence patterns before any empirical comparison favors it. A fair cost inventory counts basic experiential properties, latent powers and laws, generation and maintenance conditions, and quality-and-subject assignments on each side; no inspected original derives a uniquely confirmed theory from those costs.

Panpsychism and experiential constitution

The operational panpsychist comparator bets that experiential constituents can support an account of complex subjects, bridged by an explicit constitutive relation grounding unified subjecthood; its stipulated objection is that many subjects do not evidently constitute one subject. The developed position in the record is constitutive Russellian panpsychism as presented in the inspected Chalmers manuscript — an undated author text labelled forthcoming, whose exact published version the record does not fix: experience realizes the intrinsic natures of physical dispositions, so microexperience has causal roles through physics rather than intervening from outside, and macroexperience is grounded in microexperience plus whatever further relations the base provides [S-T12-001]. The attraction is integration: phenomenality and causal relevance in one package, without new macro-level laws.

The same inspected text is the source of the family's canonical difficulty. Chalmers separates the combination problem into subject, quality and structure components — microsubjects into a macrosubject, microqualities into macroqualities, microstructure into the smooth structure of experience — and argues that solving one leaves the others; his anti-combination arguments rely on modal premises he marks as disputable, and he treats phenomenal bonding as promising while questioning its nature and boundaries. The project analysis draws the family's outer boundary: an experiential intrinsic-nature model entails neither a subject for every aggregate nor one universal subject.

The record's developed positive argument is Strawson's. On the inspected authorial version, realistic monism runs conditionally: experience is concrete reality; assume concrete reality is physical and constituted by ultimates; no experiential reality can emerge from wholly nonexperiential reality; therefore some ultimates involve experience — micropsychism — and panpsychism proper only with an added homogeneity premise [S-P2T03-004]. The no-radical-emergence premise is an intuition the text defends but cannot demonstrate, and the record preserves a version boundary: the selected 2006 journal text was never inspected; the inspected version is the later authorial chapter, one work-version family, not independent corroboration.

Goff's inspected objection turns the argument's own weapon on it. Microexperiences do not transparently supply a further subject, so the brute emergence Strawson forbids between nonexperiential and experiential seems to reappear between micro and macro; and Strawson's use of transparent experiential knowledge against a posteriori physicalist identification threatens to prove too much, since experiential constitution is no more transparent [S-P2T03-002]. The phenomenal-parthood argument sharpens it: if experiential being is exhaustively how it feels, a constituent's character must figure in the whole's character, and slight pains do not sum to a severe pain of a new quality. On the stronger transparency and parthood premises, novel experiential combination is not merely unexplained but incoherent.

Strawson's inspected reply concedes remarkably much while holding the line. He accepts that Goff's argument refutes one natural full-revelation reading of his position, and answers by distinguishing first-order acquaintance from exhaustive disclosure: having an experience does not reveal every constitutive fact about it, so the absence of microsubjects from the macrosubject's acquaintance does not establish their absence [S-P2T03-003]. Constituting microsubjects are numerically distinct and need not share their perspectives with the subject they constitute; experiences are active realities with their causal powers internal to their nature — a revision he explicitly acknowledges of his earlier view. He acknowledges a defective argument in his own text at the recorded locus, concedes he lacks any account of how microexperience constitutes macroexperience, and rests the claim that it does on a transcendental argument plus smallism, acknowledging formal parity in appeals to prior commitment.

The recorded upshot is symmetric restraint. The exchange neither establishes subject combination nor demonstrates its impossibility independently of the disputed revelation and parthood bridges; an epistemic defense does not discharge the positive constitution burden. And the family's reach is bounded on its own terms: micropsychism and experiential constitution entail no cosmic subject, no surviving person and no brain-independent human consciousness — Strawson accepts the relevant experiential states as brain states within his account, and treats experience as requiring time.

Substance dualism

The operational dualist comparator posits distinct irreducible bearers of subjectivity with coherent, constrained relations to bodies; its stipulated objection is conflict with a complete physical causal account. The developed position is Lowe's non-Cartesian substance dualism, on the inspected article: the self is a persisting property bearer distinct from its body and parts — distinctness motivated by replacement and thought-bearer considerations — without any commitment to possible disembodiment [S-P2T02-001]. Lowe's causal proposal distinguishes intentional fact causation from neural event causation: the self causes the fact that some appropriate neural events occur, while the physical event chain remains complete, with closure conceded conditionally for the argument.

The recorded challenges are two. Engelhardt argues that physical event sufficiency supports occurrence-fact sufficiency, reinstating exclusion against Lowe's fact-causation distinction [S-P2T02-002]; the Engelhardt text was inspected through an author-linked transcription carrying the printed page markers, with the exact publisher text unverified and the author's page generally labelling its linked works penultimate drafts. Bennett's inspected chapter applies her counterfactual overdetermination test to dualism conditional on physical completeness: genuine independent sufficient causes must pass counterfactual independence checks that dependent causal descriptions fail, and a dualist with merely contingent psychophysical links needs a response the physicalist's dependence strategy cannot supply — unless a coherent, motivated necessitating relation between the distinct substances is available, an option she doubts but does not close [S-P2T02-003].

Engelhardt's own inclusive variant answers by revision: let distinct mind and body share one causally relevant physical property instance, so that two bearers do not supply two independent physical pushes, and require the counterfactual comparisons to hold a defensible embodiment relation fixed rather than severing it and testing a different situation [S-P2T02-002]. The inspected text disclaims meeting the independent-powers ambition it attributes to Lowe's fuller view; colocation, token property sharing and the embodiment relation stand as substantive burdens, and the record marks that a merely named correlation will not carry them.

The recorded upshot: the exchange improves the conditional comparison of bearer and causal models without establishing that a distinct mental substance exists. Two boundary inferences matter for later chapters. A primitive continuing subject supplies no observed postmortem identity test — the exchange establishes nothing about survival or timelessness. And the exclusion burden concerns sufficient causes, dependence and event individuation; energy conservation alone does not settle this dispute either way.

Idealism: universal and plural grounds

The operational idealist comparator bets that a conscious basis could ground both experience and a public, ordered world, earning support through overall explanatory success; its stipulated objection is that experiential knowledge does not prove mental ontology. The record's developed universal version is Kastrup's dissociation-based cosmic idealism, inspected as an identified journal-layout version accessed through a transcription with printed page markers: one cosmic conscious primitive whose self-excitation patterns are experiences; finite private perspectives arise by dissociation, located at living-organism boundaries; and bodies and brains are the extrinsic appearances of dissociated experiential processes, with cross-boundary impingement and coded perception explaining the perceived world [S-T12-002]. Economy is the selling point — one ontological kind — and the inspected text conditions it explicitly on explanatory adequacy.

The record is strict about what carries this model's load. Kastrup's clinical dissociation findings, no-report paradigms, quantum-measurement claims and biological illustrations enter the record as attributions whose original studies were not inspected; no clinical or physical fact is accepted from the philosophical paper. The project analyses press two structural points: dissociation in embodied patients does not itself establish cosmic subject division — the analogy must ground finite phenomenality, not merely block communication — and calling neural activity an appearance must recover exact neural interventions and public regularities, on pain of redescribing the data rather than explaining them. A lawful shared mental world is a coherent comparison target; specifying a shared substrate does not derive its particular laws.

The recorded serious challenge is Chalmers's conditional subject-constitution objection, inspected in draft and final-layout versions whose exact publication history the record leaves unresolved: a cosmic subject with all its cosmic mental states can seemingly be conceived without any further distinct macrosubjects; if grounding requires necessitation and the relevant conceivability entails possibility, pure cosmic grounding of distinct subjects fails [S-T12-003]. Chalmers treats mode-relative cognitive fragmentation — the identity reading, on which we are the cosmic subject under restricted modes — as coherent but expensive in introspective and interpersonal costs, and the final layout adds a no-highest-level countercase against cosmic holism. The record notes the exchange's asymmetry: the inspected Chalmers text engages Kastrup's earlier paper, not the inspected 2018 article, so the confrontation is an argumentative reconstruction, not a verified direct reply chain.

The same material generalizes into the record's cosmic-versus-plural comparison: whole-first models relocate constitution rather than automatically solving it — physical holism alone does not select an experiential ground, and identity versions revise ordinary individuality while nonidentity versions owe a sufficient grounding principle. Plural idealism, with multiple basic subjects lawfully coordinated, is a recorded alternative that permits a mental ontology without one universal subject; shared bearer, shared kind of reality and numerical identity are three different claims, and motifs of unity establish none of them.

The record's developed plural comparator is historical and inspected: Leibniz. The Monadology argues from composites to genuine simples, rejects mechanical explanation of perception by the mill argument, and equips each monad with intrinsic representational nature and qualitative individuality, distinguishing bare perception from apperception — so not every monad is a conscious person [S-T10-002]. Created monads remain plural while depending on God and representing one universe; ideal coordination and distinct soul-and-body laws do not identify created subjects with one mind. The New System supplies the 1695 published statement of substantial unities and divine initial coordination, inspected in an identified translation with marked later amendments [S-P2T04-002].

The inspected challenge is Foucher's 1695 reply: coordinated sequences are possible, but why unnecessary bodies at all, if appearances are internally sufficient; a theory built to preserve prior separations may lack independent justification; and effort-and-resistance interaction is excluded only by premises a critic need not grant [S-P2T04-003]. Leibniz's actual 1696 published explanation answers on the record: harmony is law-grounded rather than perpetual intervention, mass foundations differ from abstract extension and genuine animal unity from clockwork, God prefers a plurality of substances with external correspondence, and force is intrinsic, with conservation invoked against interactionism [S-P2T04-004]. The record preserves the limits: abbreviated translations, an omitted rejoinder chain, and theological premises — divine actuality, choice and coordination — doing essential work.

The recorded upshot for the family: these inspected versions supply a developed, bounded comparison — universal and plural mental ontologies with explicit mechanisms and explicit costs — without establishing fundamental or universal consciousness, brain independence, survival, timelessness, preservation, intention, goodness or authority. Leibniz's preservation and moral-order conclusions rest on additional theological premises the comparison does not adjudicate.

Theistic accounts

The operational theistic comparator is built from two bets: contingent reality may require explanation, and intentional agency may provide it. Its stipulated bridge is unusually demanding, and the project imposed it deliberately: independent arguments are required for agency, for good ends and for moral authority — explanation alone does not identify mind, goodness or accountability. The stipulated strongest objection is exactly that gap, and the stipulated reply is to supply the distinct arguments and test specified ends against suffering and hiddenness rather than asserting them.

The demand that survives functional explanation

The demand is Chalmers's, and the record carries it as a distinction rather than a doctrine: explaining discrimination, access or behavioral control is one kind of task, and explaining why those processes have a subjective character is another [S-T03-001]. The inspected text — an author-hosted version of the 1995 article, examined at its relevant sections — does not claim that functional explanation has achieved nothing. The claim is conditional and structural: completing the functional program would not obviously complete the different task of accounting for experience. That conditional is what survives ordinary explanatory success, and it is what every position in this chapter is answering, deflating or refusing.

A theory that includes experience among its primitives meets the demand in one precise sense: it ceases to require the experiential to arise from a vocabulary stipulated to contain nothing experiential. This is genuine relief, but it is definitional relief. Declining to derive experience is not yet explaining anything about it — which experiences occur, in which systems, with which structure. The article's framing, which this thesis inherits, is that the advantage is real only if the demand is correctly identified, and the identification is exactly what the physicalist side contests.

The record's developed antiphysicalist argument runs through knowledge and conceivability. On the author text, inspected at its relevant sections, Chalmers argues against the phenomenal-concept strategy and for a conditional conclusion: if the physical facts can be conceived without the experiential facts, and if the relevant conceivability entails the relevant possibility, physicalism is false [S-T05-003]. The record marks all three bridge principles — the modal bridge from conceivability to possibility, the epistemic bridge from knowledge intuitions to conceivability, and the explanatory bridge from gap to ontology — as contested, and the conclusion as conditional on them. The text is an author PDF identified by exact URL, its publication year carrying 2006 and 2007 variants in discovery metadata.

The physicalist defense in the record has two prongs. The first is the phenomenal-concept strategy itself: Papineau defends the view that phenomenal and physical concepts are distinct epistemic routes to one property, so epistemic difference need not refute identity [S-T05-004]. The inspected text is an author-hosted typeset chapter whose exact publication year the record leaves unresolved. Chalmers's counter, in the same dependency group, poses a dilemma for the strategy — roughly, that the conceptual features doing the explaining either themselves resist physical explanation or fail to explain the epistemic situation they are invoked for, including its truth and justification — and the record leaves the dilemma, like everything else here, as an attributed argument with contested premises.

The second prong attacks the demand's explanatory standard. On the inspected published article — an author-hosted text of the 2011 Philosophia paper — Papineau challenges what an identity owes us: identities, once warranted, need no further explanation, so the residual "why is this process experienced this way" question may be a demand for something no true identity could supply [S-T05-005]. The felt gap is relocated into psychology — a resistance to identification whose causes are open to empirical study — rather than into ontology. This deflation does not close the gap; it reclassifies it.

The methodological upshot, stated in the article this chapter expands, is that the burden runs both ways. The antireductionist must defend the transition from a gap in explanation to a gap in the world — the explanatory bridge is a premise, not a datum. The reductionist must show that the proposed identification preserves its explanandum rather than replacing it — an identity earns its explanatory relief only once warranted, and the mere possibility of identity explains nothing in particular. Neither move comes free, and the record prices both.

Illusionism and the acquaintance exchange

Illusionism makes the reductionist's burden — preserve the explanandum — unusually explicit, by refusing to preserve it and saying so. On the inspected author version, Frankish retains experience in an inclusive sense, perceptual activity, access, reports and introspective representation, while rejecting genuinely qualitative properties whether physical or nonphysical, so that the central task becomes explaining why a system represents itself as possessing such properties [S-P2T05-001]. The record retains the account's self-description as a programme with proposed content mechanisms rather than a completed explanation, and its motivations — conservative explanation, introspective fallibility, abduction from anomalousness — as proposals whose premises are contested.

The programme's structural discipline is worth separating from its truth. It avoids the conscious spectator: no inner audience inspects internal representations, since that audience would recreate the problem one level up. And it meets the regress worry — that each apparent feel needs a higher representation, threatening a circle or a concealed phenomenal starting point — with fine-grained nonphenomenal seemings and a limited higher-order stop. The record's assessment is exact on this point: the regress identifies a real explanatory constraint, not an automatic contradiction, once functional seeming and limited introspection are allowed. What the constraint costs is a worked account of why nonphenomenal representations present determinate apparent qualities; no such completed account is in the record.

The recorded objection is stronger than the slogan that an illusion must itself be conscious — its author notes that quick line and sets it aside. On her inspected manuscript — an author manuscript whose final print was not available for byte-level or visual verification — Nida-Rümelin identifies experiential properties through subjects and examples, so their reference does not depend on a description an illusionist could reject, and argues that instantiating the relevant property already involves awareness of it [S-P2T05-002]. Acquaintance, on this account, is not a second introspective judgment that could misrepresent an independently occurring state; there is no gap in which the illusionist's error theory can live. Her conclusion is expressly conditional on an experiential example actually being instantiated.

Frankish's inspected reply grants the ordinary examples and the subject-property reformulation for argument, and denies precisely what the objection needs: that reference by example fixes the property's realist nature [S-P2T05-003]. What the examples pick out may admit a representational interpretation; cognitive access to structural physical properties requires mediation, so robust unmediated acquaintance is rejected — a rejection the reply candidly derives from physicalist commitments and expects not to persuade this critic. The exchange ends with each side's decisive premise intact and explicitly identified: adequacy of the functional account against identification of awareness with instantiation.

The dispute therefore concerns what must be preserved in explaining the examples, and the article's framing of the stalemate is the one this thesis keeps. Defining every seeming as phenomenal assumes the realist premise; defining apparent character as nothing beyond representation assumes too much the other way. The stronger illusionist response must explain the richness and authority of phenomenal judgments without quietly relying on their truth; the stronger realist response must show why its identification of the target resists that explanation. Within this dialectic, experiential primitiveness has a genuine dialectical advantage for the realist, which the record states carefully: it preserves rather than revises the target. It does not yet establish that preservation is explanatorily superior to a successful account of misrepresentation — and whether the misrepresentation account can succeed is exactly what the record marks unresolved.

Emergence, exclusion and latent powers

Accepting the target does not select an ontology, and the causal-emergence exchange shows why. O'Connor's 1994 formulation required supervenience, nonstructurality and downward causal influence [S-P2T01-003]; the 2005 account with Wong explicitly repudiates the supervenience requirement and posits basic nonstructural properties causally generated and sustained over time by organized physical systems [S-P2T01-001]. Both are inspected originals. The two dependence accounts are recorded as distinct and must not be merged: the model that matters downstream is the temporal, generation-and-maintenance one.

Kim's challenge — on the inspected 2008 reprint; the original 1999 journal pagination was not inspected — is narrower than its reputation and sharper for it. He allows ordinary downward causation and treats phenomenal qualia as promising candidates for irreducibility; the argument is that where an emergent has a sufficient noncausal basal condition, the base threatens to preempt the emergent as a cause, and relocating the emergent's effects later in time does not by itself remove the threat [S-P2T01-002]. The record marks the argument's own load-bearing premises — noncausal determination, basal sufficiency, exclusion of systematic overdetermination — as substantive commitments, not logical truths.

The structural lesson of the reply is that naming an emergent property answers nothing; a premise must be rejected. The 2005 account rejects the noncausal-dependence premise: if the base causally generates the emergent, the emergent is an intermediate cause in a temporal process rather than a redundant competitor, and the preemption inference fails against this model. The record prices the escape exactly — a disputed simplicity comparison against latent-power alternatives, a thought experiment that stipulates its own discriminating conditions, contested first-person motivation, and no independently confirmed emergence law.

Shoemaker's inspected countercase supplies the other horn of a productive choice. Micro-latent powers — powers of ordinary microconstituents manifested only in suitable, emergence-engendering combinations — let complete microfacts fix macrofacts while distinguishing causal power from mere nomic necessitation; his preferred identity-and-description treatment differs from irreducible emergent properties, and he considers and rejects a distinct-property option from his essential-individuation commitments [S-P2T01-004]. The record's comparison point is exacting: a successful reply to Kim does not certify the nonstructural ontology, and deciding between the replies requires adjudicating property individuation, dependence and specified empirical discriminators — not counting replies.

The exchange thus establishes a choice of explanatory commitments rather than a winner. Rejecting the qualified completeness of a nonexperiential causal account makes irreducible experiential causation intelligible, and incurs an obligation to explain the actual causal organization — including the inherited neural-dependence patterns any such model must reproduce. Preserving completeness through latent powers incurs the mirror obligation of specifying powers that nothing in familiar manifestations predicts. Neither side wins by treating its causal vocabulary as compulsory, and the record's cost inventory is explicitly not a parsimony score or a ranking.

Contingency and the weak principle of sufficient reason

The demand the contingency route answers is the article's: a foundational theory must also address why this concrete order exists. The record's historical anchor is Leibniz's 1714 Monadology, inspected in the Bennett English modernization at the relevant sections — the original French was not compared. There Leibniz states a principle of sufficient reason for true or existent facts, distinguishes necessary from contingent truths, and argues that even an infinite sequence of contingencies lacks its ultimate reason internally, concluding to a necessary substance [S-T10-002]. The record keeps a further step separate: unity, perfection, understanding and will are attributed to the foundation in a distinct move, and calling a foundation necessary establishes none of them.

The record's historical counterweight is Hume's Dialogues, inspected in the Bennett English modernization at its relevant sections with marked editorial additions, and read with speaker discipline: no speaker uniformly expresses Hume's settled view. In Part IX Cleanthes objects to Demea's necessity argument through conceivability, the possibility of a necessary material universe, and the explanation of parts; Philo adds an analogy with hidden necessity [S-T10-003]. Elsewhere Philo challenges the extrapolation from human artifacts and asks what a mental plan explains, and Cleanthes replies that explaining a cause does not require explaining its cause. These are dialogue positions with contestable premises of their own — the record marks the conceivability premise and the causal-priority conception as open.

The project's reconstruction of the classical route is explicitly conditional. A restricted principle of sufficient reason covering the contingent totality, a noncircular external-explanation requirement and a concrete-ground premise conditionally yield necessary concrete reality — and mentality needs further premises. The record marks each step as substantive, and it keeps the recorded objection and reply in their roles: the objection is that local explanations may exhaust the legitimate demand and that a necessary impersonal reality remains possible; the reply is that local causal links can leave why the totality obtains unanswered and that exemptions from the principle need justification — while the principle's own scope stays a recorded unresolved issue, not a settled symmetric burden. Totality contingency is not inferred merely from contingent parts, modal necessity is not temporal or causal priority, and the alternatives — brute totality, a necessary impersonal ground, rejection of the concrete-ground premise — remain live throughout.

The record's developed modern version is the Gale–Pruss argument, inspected in the full selected text of the 1999 article. Its advertised novelty is modal weakness: instead of asserting that the totality of contingent truths has an explanation, it assumes only that it could have one — a weak principle of sufficient reason over propositions true in any possible world — and combines that with a maximal world-individuating contingent conjunction, bivalence and compossibility to derive an actual explanation [S-P2T10-001]. The record separates the formal statement from its informal gloss: the formal scope is broader than an actual-world-only reading, and the two must not be interchanged. The article's framing of the attraction is inherited here: explain contingency through something that does not merely add another unexplained contingent member.

Oppy's criticism, inspected in the selected manuscript text of the 2000 reply — the print pagination was not collated — attacks the advertised modesty. Under stated assumptions about explanation — veridicality and dissectivity within normal modal reasoning — the weak principle entails the strong one, by a Fitch-style route that does not depend on the maximal-world construction [S-P2T10-002]. The record retains his care with the logic: dissectivity is not agglomerativity, and an explanation existing for a conjunction is not the conjunction of two explanations explaining the joint obtaining. The epistemic point rides on the logical one: a person who assented to the weak wording without grasping its consequences may rationally withdraw assent once the entailment is explicit, if they have independent grounds for beliefs that conflict with the conclusion — a condition of the argument, the record notes, not a demonstrated fact about all non-theists.

Two further Oppy distinctions are recorded because the downstream assessment uses them. An explained counterpart or physical duplicate of the universe is not the same object as the actual world-complete fact, so the intuitive possibility of the former does not by itself support the weak principle over the latter. And questions about abstract propositions and the difference between the big conjunctive fact and its contingent restriction are flagged but severable — the record notes Oppy's statement that his main criticism does not depend on resolving them, and the totality ontology remains a separate open issue.

The Gale–Pruss reply of 2002, inspected in the full selected version, concedes the entailment and relocates the defense. Weak and strong principles are reformulated for contingent propositions and the deduction is accepted; the reply argues that understanding a proposition does not require grasping all its consequences, that explanation-seeking practice gives the weak principle epistemic appeal, and — against Davey and Clifton as presented in the reply itself, whose original was not inspected, with the Pruss 2001 cardinality proof behind the construction also uninspected — that a restricted totality fallback is available conditionally [S-P2T10-003]. The record's sharpest annotation concerns a bridge inside the defense: the reply moves from epistemic possibility to logical possibility as a reasonable step after adequate inquiry, and that modal warrant is defeasible and contested, not a deduction from ignorance.

The article's verdict on this exchange, which this thesis keeps, is that the reply blocks an easy accusation of unintelligibility but does not make the disclosed consequences costless. The question becomes whether commitment to the explanatory principle, understood at full modal strength, remains better justified than its rivals — and that is a comparison of intuitions and practices, not a further theorem. The cosmological argument, on the record as inspected, provides a structured challenge to brute contingency, not an uncontested passage from intelligibility to providence.

From necessary foundation to mind: the open bridge

Even granting an actual explanation of the totality, the step to an agent is a separate argument with its own recorded pressure points. The Gale–Pruss route treats personal and scientific explanation as exhaustive — a premise their own text qualifies by conceding that unconceived kinds of explanation may exist — excludes scientific explanation because contingent laws and states fall inside the totality and cannot explain themselves, distinguishes necessary existence from contingent creative action to avoid modal collapse, and terminates the regress in a necessary being's libertarian free action, presented as self-explaining with fullness of explanation relative to the questioner's context. The record prices each move: the exhaustiveness premise is not a measured inventory, the free-action exception is a substantive explanatory premise, and further reasons can still be requested.

The recorded impersonal rival enters through the authors themselves. Gale and Pruss report Quinn's objection that a necessary impersonal source could produce the cosmos by blind indeterministic causation — only their published quotation of correspondence was inspected, not any independent Quinn original — and their response allows a generic necessary powerful cause as a fallback while defending personality by appeal to cosmic order and a distinction between free and merely indeterministic action. The project's strongest recorded question sits exactly here: why is libertarian self-explanation sufficient where impersonal indeterminism is not, once explanatory fullness has been made context-sensitive? The record leaves the question open on both sides.

The attribute stage is recorded as candidly incomplete in the original texts. The 1999 argument adds teleological premises for the creator's qualities while explicitly withholding omni-properties and acknowledging that actual benevolence needs theodicy and faith; a footnote records the two authors disagreeing between themselves about whether the possibility of gratuitous evil defeats an essentially perfect necessary God. The 2002 reply preserves the same gap from the other direction: evidence against benevolence does not alone exclude every necessary creator, since an explanation of a horrendous universe could fall short of God. Nothing in the exchange converts a necessary source into a good one.

The record's third voice on the bridge is Lennox, inspected as a full essay in HTML — an apologetic venue whose quoted historical statements and resurrection case the record reads as context, not corroborated events. The Ford analogy makes mechanism and agent compatible: explaining how a process operates does not exclude an agent explanation of why it was arranged [S-T10-001]. The essay then separately advances positive claims — discoverable intelligibility and sophisticated lawlike order as reasons for a rational creator, and a challenge to naturalism's warrant for reason — and treats natural laws as regularities that alone do not rule out supernatural agency.

The record's assessment discipline separates the two steps exactly. Compatibility removes one alleged incompatibility and by itself favors nothing; comparative support requires independently motivated expectations under which the observed order is more expected on a rational origin than on a defined impersonal alternative. The recorded objections hold both conditions open: agent preferences and the contrast class may be fitted after observing the world, impersonal structure and selection-plus-learning alternatives are not empirically tested here, and no equation of nonintentional origins with unreliable reason is validated. The order appeals also share assumptions with the cosmological argument's attribute stage, so their conjunction adds no independent sample.

The open bridge can now be stated in the record's own terms. A necessary explanatory source does not alone establish a conscious bearer, unity, bodily independence, personal survival, timelessness, preserving purpose or moral authority; each component needs its own bridge, and the absence of a proven rival does not prove its negation. The article's framing sharpens the methodological point: personal explanation cannot be privileged solely because the intended conclusion is personal. What the contingency route delivers, at the reconstruction's conditional maximum, is a necessary concrete ground — and everything this thesis cares about lies on the far side of that result.

Conditional discrimination and its assumptions

The article's conclusion for this material, which this chapter keeps as inherited framing, is conditional discrimination. Observer selection does not trivially erase every relevant difference — Sober's recorded position declines to debunk every design argument — and unavailable numerical probabilities do not imply that all qualitative comparisons are empty. 6. Neural Dependence and the Evidential Role of Crisis Experiences

The two preceding chapters priced philosophical routes to a conscious foundation. This chapter turns to the empirical record inherited from the article's fifth section: what controlled neural interventions establish about the dependence of experience on brain activity, and what crisis-experience reports — the near-death and resuscitation material — can and cannot show. The chapter's hinge is a conjunctive evidential standard: a crisis report discriminates between rival accounts of mind only when externally accurate content, a defensible acquisition interval and adequate information about the processes available during that interval are all established together. Four case dossiers, three prospective studies, a historical and cross-cultural corpus and their critic-and-reply chains are assessed against that standard, and the chapter closes with the evidential asymmetry between the two streams, the dependence accounting and an explicit statement of what none of this material concludes.

The standing caution of the previous chapters binds here with additions specific to empirical material. Every clinical number, timing estimate and experience description in this chapter is an attribution to an identified inspected version, not a project-verified measurement: two of the case dossiers rest partly on user-supplied purported transcripts whose audio, speaker identity and timestamps were never verified; two rest partly on converted ebooks whose conversion metadata postdates publication; several rest on current webpages that are not authenticated snapshots of their original wording. Each narrator's crisis and recovery episodes form one reporting dependency group, so retellings, translations and republications never add observations; and counts keep their units throughout — people, resuscitation episodes, interviews, EEG images and analysis windows are not interchangeable. The specific dependency structure is stated in the closing section.

Controlled intervention evidence and its scope

The article's demand is for discrimination, not covariation, and the record's cleanest intervention case supplies it at single-patient scale. In Parvizi and colleagues' 2012 study, electrical stimulation of a right fusiform face-selective region pair in one 45-year-old epilepsy patient elicited reported face distortions on seven real-face trials, while four sham trials and three trials stimulating a nearby control pair did not [S-T09-004]. The sham and control contrasts support a causal contribution within this case, which is more than correlation supplies. The record prices the limits with equal care: nonspecific nonface changes also occurred, the two stimulated regions were not separated, current can affect connected sites, the small famous-face and place naming tests differed procedurally and their null differences support no equivalence conclusion, and randomization and assessor masking were not established. A 2013 correction changes only an author name [S-T09-005]. One patient's repeated trials are not independent people, and no unitary face center follows — the local-versus-network question stays open.

Tasserie and colleagues' 2022 macaque study extends the intervention evidence to state functions. Under maintained propofol anesthesia, high central-thalamic deep brain stimulation raised the reported behavioral arousal score from 0 of 11 to 9 of 11 in both stimulated animals, while low central-thalamic stimulation reached 3 of 11 and ventrolateral control stimulation remained 0 of 11; awake animals score 11 of 11 [S-T09-002]. Stimulation also shifted fMRI dynamics and enhanced hierarchical auditory responses. The record's qualifications are structural. The intervention unit is two animals, with three separate awake comparators and reused awake auditory data; blinding and randomized condition order were not established in the inspected text; subjective awareness is inferred from signatures, since macaques give no trialwise reports; the auditory paradigm had no ventrolateral control arm; and the supplementary methods disclose that each animal's high stimulation setting was selected empirically just above its behavioral arousal threshold, which limits interpretation as an unselected dose-response estimate [S-P2T19-002]. Per-run volume counts and overlapping analysis windows are not run totals by animal and condition; those totals were not located in the inspected extents.

Scheinin and colleagues' anesthesia-and-sleep study, published in 2021, supplies the record's sharpest caution against equating unresponsiveness with absent experience. Forty healthy young men were recruited; 39 underwent randomized open-label propofol (19) or dexmedetomidine (20) anesthesia, and 37 returned for a sleep arm on average eighteen weeks later — overlapping arms, not 76 participants [S-T09-003]. Among interviewable states, subjective experience was reported in 80% of anesthesia interviews and 71% of sleep interviews, including contentless impressions; these are interview rates, not population prevalence, and the exact integer numerators and denominators behind the percentages are not supplied in the inspected main text. A targeted Phase 2 search of the study's extended-data appendices found regional PET cluster tables rather than interview-count tables, so the integers remain unrecovered. The paper's PET contrasts locate state-linked thalamic, cingulate and angular blood-flow changes — associations within manipulated states, not focal necessity tests — and its authors expressly note that absent response or absent recalled content cannot conclusively establish absent experience.

The Cogitate adversarial collaboration, whose main results appeared in Nature in 2025, is the record's largest theory-level test. The consortium recruited 256 participants across fMRI (120), MEG (102) and intracranial EEG (34), retaining 108, 97 and 32; main noninvasive tests used 73 held-out fMRI and 65 held-out MEG datasets, while invasive data were not split [S-T09-001]. Suprathreshold visual stimuli in four categories at three durations were tested under varying task relevance; the design did not directly contrast conscious with unconscious processing or perturb candidate regions. Operational predictions contrasted posterior versus prefrontal decoding, posterior duration tracking versus prefrontal onset/offset ignition, and posterior versus long-range prefrontal synchrony. The outcome is mixed: sustained posterior representations were partly supported and transient prefrontal category decoding appeared, but the predicted prefrontal offset profile and sustained posterior gamma synchrony were not established, and the preregistered pairwise-phase-consistency analysis supported neither critical connectivity prediction [S-T09-008]. An exploratory amplitude-based dynamic functional connectivity analysis partly supported the workspace theory's expectation while still lacking sustained posterior connectivity; the record refuses to relabel it as preregistered confirmation. No overall theory winner follows.

The Phase 2 protocol audit qualifies the confirmatory weight of those results without erasing them. The selected OSF amendment file, named v4 but internally identified as Version 4.5 and dated 22 December 2022, already specifies a one-third optimization split and unsplit intracranial data, whereas the February 2023 published protocol contains different half-split wording; the contrast cannot date the change, the node is not a certified immutable preregistration, and the earlier amendment chain and analyst data-access history were not audited [S-P2T19-001]. The amendment is internally inconsistent about fMRI counts (110 quality-passed datasets alongside a 36/80 division), while the final paper uses 35 optimization and 73 held-out datasets and its supplement explains the discrepancy only partly. The supplement also declares analysis departures: representational similarity analysis used all trial pairs rather than the planned split sets, the main fMRI connectivity analysis pooled task relevance, the multiple-comparison method changed, synchrony-channel eligibility was relaxed relative to the amendment's active windows, and three below-threshold intracranial patients were retained as a declared deviation. Reporting a departure neither proves outcome-driven bias nor removes the departure.

The reply chain is preserved with the same discipline. Naccache and colleagues' 2025 critique argues that attended offset and continuing awareness were not established, that macroscopic prefrontal decoding may miss mixed selectivity, and that activity-silent storage is not obligatory for workspace theory [S-T09-006]. The record verifies these as documented arguments, not as measured inattention, and it retains two qualifications on both sides. First, the 2023 protocol itself conditions a workspace offset response on conscious detection in its overview while elsewhere treating onset and offset as tested expectations — so the offset objection was not entirely post hoc, and the failed test was not unconditional either [S-T09-007]. Second, the critique's page-2 wording includes category among allegedly absent prefrontal decodings although the Nature results report transient prefrontal category decoding; the record preserves this as a local wording problem, not a substantive correction. The theory proponents' supplement replies — coverage, frequency choice and region-criteria explanations from the integrated-information side, and the consortium's own statement that shared successful predictions do not uniquely confirm either theory — are contemporaneous interpretations [S-T09-008]. A bounded forward search found no later consortium rebuttal to the critique; absence of discovery is not absence of reply.

What the intervention stream establishes, on the record as inspected, is causal contribution: site-dependent stimulation changes reported perceptual content in a human patient, and state-targeted stimulation restores arousal and access markers in anesthetized animals, under controls that make passive correlation an incomplete description. Models denying any neural contribution to embodied experience are thereby challenged. The article's framing of the transmission reply is inherited: a theorist who holds that altering an interface alters what appears through it escapes logical refutation, but bare compatibility is a weak explanatory achievement — the alternative must explain why this intervention changes this content, and a metaphor of reception supplies no such relation. What the stream does not establish is equally recorded: no unique universally necessary and sufficient neural mechanism, no physicalist identity, and no adjudication among identity, realization, interface and idealist accounts that specify the dependence explicitly. Neural dependence in the tested contexts is a constraint every surviving model must carry, not a verdict on ontology.

The conjunctive standard for crisis-report discrimination

Crisis reports enter the record because of what they would show if a demanding conjunction held. Accurate information acquired under conditions excluding the relevant ordinary processes would put pressure on a specified neural account — and the operative evidence is conjunctive: externally accurate content, a defensible interval of acquisition and adequate information about the processes available during that interval. The article's framing, which this chapter applies throughout, is that a striking report with uncertain timing does not have the same discriminatory force, and a physiological interval does not establish when remembered content was formed. Retrospective memory that accurately dates its own content to an excluded interval is precisely what the cases below cannot independently demonstrate; every dossier's positive argument is conditional on that bridge.

The standard is symmetric, and the record enforces the symmetry. A proposed ordinary mechanism — restored circulation, residual hearing, sedation-shaped dreaming, later reconstruction — also needs case-specific grounds before it counts as the demonstrated explanation; a plausible possibility is not a diagnosis. The recorded cross-case inference for the Reynolds and dentures dossiers states the first direction: those two dossiers support specific, traceable perception claims and substantive disputes, but lack independently fixed perception timing plus complete information-access controls needed to identify extracerebral acquisition. The other two dossiers carry their own packet conclusions: the Neal material supports attributed experience and a conditional argument, not survival; the Alexander documentation supports richer attributions without independently fixing all recalled stages to an interval excluding relevant ordinary acquisition. And conversely, in no dossier has a critic's mechanism been demonstrated as the actual cause of any particular report. Both failures matter, and neither substitutes for the other.

Prospective studies: AWARE, Dutch cohort, denominator discipline

AWARE I, published in 2014 from fifteen hospitals, defines the prospective baseline and its arithmetic frictions. The study reports 2,060 recorded arrest events and 330 discharge survivors; 140 completed stage-one interviews — though the same layout prints 52 in-hospital plus 90 post-discharge interviews and a table denominator of 142, an unresolved discrepancy the later AWARE II introduction compounds by citing 143 [S-T07-001]. Of 101 stage-two interviewees, 46 reported no memory, 46 non-NDE memories, and 9 met the Greyson threshold, including two with auditory or visual event recall. Both event-recall cases occurred at locations without the study's concealed target shelves, and only one could be followed up, the other's health failing. The authors report medical-record corroboration for one case and infer roughly three minutes of awareness from a defibrillator sequence; the record keeps the inference's units apart — awareness up to three minutes and arrest lasting at least three minutes are different quantities — and notes that no participant-linked neural recording accompanies any reported content.

The Dutch prospective study, published in 2001 from ten hospitals, contributes the largest early cohort and a lesson in version discipline. It reports 344 consecutively resuscitated patients across 509 resuscitation episodes — people and episodes are different units — with 62 broad-definition NDE reports (18%) and 41 core reports (12%); a printed 248 interviewed within five days (74%) lacks a clear matching denominator, and the record declines to manufacture one [S-T07-004]. The 2002 published correction, inspected directly, changes one drug name in one sentence and no cohort counts or follow-up results [S-P2T09-003]. Longitudinal reclassification changed the two-year groups from 37/37 to 35/39, with eight-year groups of 23 and 15 — the same retained people, not independent cohorts. The authors acknowledge possible site selection and use estimated arrest timing; no participant-specific EEG during any reported experience is presented.

AWARE II, published in 2023 from a multi-center design spanning 25 sites in its main phase, is the record's most instrumented attempt and its clearest demonstration of the gap between deployment and opportunity. Of 567 recruited arrest subjects, 213 had sustained return of circulation, 53 survived to discharge, 28 completed interviews, and 11 reported any memory or perception, with overlapping thematic categories and six transcendent experiences at the Greyson threshold [S-T07-007]. Tablet-and-headphone equipment was deployed in 365 of 567 cases, but deployment is not valid stimulus delivery — the protocol starts visual images at one minute and auditory fruit names at five minutes after activation, and the delivered-stimulus-by-interview intersections are never reported. No explicit recall of any target and no implicit visual identification occurred; one of 28 interviewees made a correct fruit choice, for which the protocol's any-three-fruits request supplies no uniform chance model; and a separate single participant's overt recollection of clinical surroundings received chart verification of a shock event, with unmeasured prior resuscitation knowledge acknowledged by the authors. Twenty-five discharge survivors were never interviewed.

The AWARE II EEG arm changed the debate's terms while inheriting its unit discipline. The study captured 851 EEG screenshot images from 85 subjects during pulse-check pauses on a limited frontotemporal montage; 466 images from 53 subjects were interpretable, 49 of those subjects also had cerebral oximetry, and the authors' body-text summary reports near-normal or physiological rhythms up to 35 to 60 minutes into resuscitation — figure points extend beyond that summary, leaving the exact maximum unresolved — and an alpha-coma interpretation could not be excluded [S-T07-007]. Images and people are different denominators; the paper reports suppression in 47% of EEG data, a data-unit figure a later correspondent paraphrases as about half the patients, and the record retains the original unit. The decisive intersection is stated in the study's own flow diagram: of the 28 interviewed survivors, exactly two had EEG data, and neither was among those with explicit cognitive recall. Aggregate rhythm timing cannot date any particular memory's formation, and missing EEG-report links do not establish absent consciousness.

The published EEG exchange sharpens exactly that point, and the record preserves both its force and its limits. Greyson and van Lommel's letter argues that the EEG-consciousness interpretation fails for lack of overlap between recorded EEG and explicit recollections in the interviewed subset, and questions artifact exclusion [S-P2T09-001]. Parnia's reply answers that absent explicit recall does not prove absent consciousness — invoking implicit memory — and defends the sampling, artifact removal and expert interpretation procedures [S-P2T09-002]. The recorded adjudication is symmetric: the study does not establish a positive association between its EEG findings and experience, and the critics' missing association does not establish that the recorded patterns cannot be consciousness markers. The reply succeeds against the simple negative inference while supplying no participant-specific experience content, timing or independent artifact audit. An unmeasured association is not a demonstrated negative association, and neither letter supplies the missing intersection.

The parallel methodological exchange with Martial and colleagues receives the same treatment. Their letter challenges EEG classification and missing data, the renaming of the experiences, the common-fruit interpretation and the timing exclusions [S-T07-003]. The authors' direct reply — which explicitly answers Martial, a distinct chain from the Greyson reply — defends adapted EEG standards, attributes the missing time-frequency analysis to practical multichannel constraints, argues that stable clinical states cannot model evolving arrest physiology, and rebuts the fruit-guessing concern by citing more than 80,000 English nouns [S-P2T09-005]. The record's annotations are pointed: a practical explanation for an omitted analysis does not supply its result, and the noun-count reference class does not match the protocol's actual request to name any three fruits, so no probability calculation is licensed in either direction. The reply's larger interpretive program — a disinhibition account of lucid experience near death, with its functional benefit expressly left unknown — is preserved as the authors' proposal, its physiological and evolutionary assertions not independently validated facts.

Historical and cross-cultural recurrence and dependence

The historical record establishes that the reported phenomenon predates modern publicity, under heavy literary dependence. Bede's Ecclesiastical History, composed around 731 and inspected in a 1907 revised English translation, transmits Dryhthelm's return-from-apparent-death narrative through a named intermediary and dates the episode around 696; two adjacent chapters supply fatal deathbed visions with explicit admonitory purposes [S-T08-001]. One author, one genre, one translation: the record counts a single documentary dependence group, no clinical status, and no independent witness chain. What the material supports is narrow and real — a documented medieval narrative with a luminous guide, punishment scenes, a pleasant landscape, return and lasting behavioral change — which weakens any claim that every such motif was invented after modern near-death literature.

Heim's 1892 collection of mountaineering-fall experiences, inspected as a digitized German separatum with acknowledged OCR limits and no visual verification of the Fraktur scan, is the corpus's other historical anchor [S-P2T18-001]. Heim draws on his own accidents, survivors' oral and written reports and literature, explicitly extrapolating survivors' sensations to the dying; his qualified claim that roughly 95% experienced the described state has no recoverable denominator — his approximately thirty examples concern potentially saving actions, a different question. His own fall report locates intensified thought, subjective time-lengthening and luminous life scenes during conscious falling, expressly ending at impact; the reported half-hour unresponsiveness afterward rests on his account of companions' statements. The text also documents its own contamination pressures: additional letters arrived after newspapers covered the talk, and the editor records a contested incident. The selected published English translation from 1972 remains body-uninspected — metadata only — so no rendering is certified [S-P2T18-002]. A nineteenth-century document, not a clinical study; the record uses it for dated recurrence and nothing else.

The cross-cultural comparisons contribute variation under unequal measurement. Pasricha and Stevenson's 1986 India report includes 16 of 18 incidentally identified cases — ten subject interviews, five firsthand-informant cases, one secondhand — with a median investigation delay around fourteen years; elicitation was unequal between the Indian and American material, and the comparison table's blanks mean no instance learned, possibly never asked [S-T08-002]. Its recurring motifs — messengers in 12 of 16, mistaken-identity return in 10 of 16 — are selected-case descriptions, and the authors themselves argue that cultural variation neither proves belief-only production nor an afterlife. Ohkado and Greyson's 2014 reanalysis of 22 published Japanese interviews codes heavenly places in 16 and borders in 12 — flower gardens in 13 of the 16, rivers in 10 of the 12 — against zero panoramic life reviews in the sample, while acknowledging outside-sample counterexamples; the underlying interview books were not inspected and no independent bilingual audit exists [S-T08-003]. Both papers propose cultural explanations without isolating culture as the sole cause; the record retains the motif differences as measured-in-sample only.

The retrospective collections establish heterogeneity and its selection costs. Greyson and Bush's 1992 distressing-experience collection — fifty self-selected accounts from correspondence and a newsletter request — motivates three proposed types, including void and hellish experiences, persistent despair alongside fear-to-peace transitions, and one account of endless succession; the paper supplies no class frequency table, no person-episode crosswalk and a candid discussion of positive-affect scale bias and reporting reluctance [S-T08-004]. Charland-Verville and colleagues' 2014 comparison retained 190 of 215 volunteered reports — 50 from non-life-threatening events and 140 from reported coma, at a mean reporting delay of 24 ± 15 years — and found no significant intensity or item-frequency differences between groups, which is not an equivalence result; its cutoff wording is internally inconsistent, and its clinical statuses are medically uncontrolled self-report [S-T08-005]. Greyson's 1990 comparison of 183 self-described experiencers with 63 deniers, recruited through a newsletter, shows the scale and the self-label pulling apart in both directions [S-T08-006]. Voluntary recruitment, long delays and unknown invitation denominators run through all three.

Parnia and colleagues' 2001 Southampton study — the corpus's early prospective anchor, inspected in the exact repository journal PDF — interviewed 63 cardiac-arrest survivors: 56 with no recall, seven with some recall, four meeting the Greyson criterion [S-P2T18-003]. Two participants endorsed the scale's lost-bodily-awareness item while the prose explicitly reports no out-of-body experience and no claimed opportunity to view the study's upward-facing ceiling boards; no target identification occurred, and the paper supplies no confirmed target-present-arrest-interview-viewpoint intersection from which effective trials could be counted. The evidential asymmetry

The asymmetry the article states, this chapter now grounds record by record. On one side, controlled stimulation provides positive evidence of neural influence — on reported perceptual content in one human case, and on behavioral arousal and access markers, with awareness inferred from signatures, in two macaques — within stated scopes and with dependence structures that survive audit. On the other, the crisis material provides reports requiring explanation and possibilities worth testing, but in no inspected case does the conjunction hold: nowhere are externally verified content, an independently fixed acquisition interval and adequate process information established together. The prospective studies define populations and deploy targets without producing a single validated concealed-target success or a confirmed opportunity denominator; the celebrated retrospective cases rest on single reporting chains whose decisive measurements were never made or never preserved. This is not a proof that every account has an ordinary cause, and a negative target result establishes no impossibility. It is a statement about which side currently possesses discriminating observations.

The dependence structure gives the asymmetry its sensitivity form, inherited from the article and confirmed in the ledger. Removing one celebrated retrospective case — Reynolds, say, or the dentures anecdote, each of which collapses to a single witness chain under audit — leaves the prospective questions intact; removing all retrospective narratives reduces both the favorable recurrence arguments and the cultural countercomparisons, since the same collections supply both. Anecdotal accumulation therefore cannot do the work of a discriminating observation: retellings, translations and analytic reuse multiply publications without multiplying observations, and the ledger's dependency groups make that double-counting visible. What survives sensitivity analysis on the positive side is bounded and worth stating: recurring reported experiences across prospective, retrospective and historical settings are a documented phenomenon warranting continued investigation with better-controlled designs — a conclusion about research value, not about mechanism or metaphysics.

Survival, recurrence and preservation distinguished

The article's framing sets the target. Even evidence of consciousness under unexpected physiological conditions would leave personal survival to be argued, because survival concerns a further subject who is the same person — not merely the recurrence of a type of conscious state, and not merely the preservation of information about a life. A duplicate's accurate memories could matter greatly without settling strict identity, and concern for a successor may be rational without answering the original survival question. The project's component definitions hold the boundary: survival requires the same person's subjectivity beyond irreversible bodily death, and nothing weaker — resemblance, record, representation, another subject's complete knowledge — satisfies that requirement by redescription.

The audit distinguishes the candidate models by what each would deliver. A first model is temporal continuation: personal persistence within time, before and possibly after bodily death, with no timeless foundation required. A second adds a timeless foundation grounding temporal persons, where individual survival remains an additional condition the foundation does not automatically supply. A third proposes complete-history inclusion: every event of a finite life represented in, or present to, an eternal knower. A fourth is preservation without survival: information, represented relationships or value retained while the original subject ceases. The recorded inferences keep these apart — inclusion of a finite life or its record alone does not meet the survival requirement, and a conscious foundation's awareness of a life is not automatically that life's subjectivity.

The handoff inference states why the distinction is load-bearing rather than pedantic. Representing or including all events of a finite life preserves specified contents; a record or a containing knower is not automatically numerically identical to the represented person. An enlarged history containing genuinely continuing subjectivity outside embodied succession is a distinct, stronger hypothesis — one deserving assessment under an explicit identity relation, which is precisely what the identity-criteria section below supplies. Merely calling the inclusion survival does not establish it, and the burden list is recorded: bearer, death condition, numerical identity, continuation domain, memory and branching.

Eternity proposals and the presence objection

Physics enters first, because timelessness is often introduced with its vocabulary. Einstein's 1905 kinematic argument derives frame-dependent distant simultaneity from a relativity principle, invariant vacuum light speed and clock synchronization; the inspected transformation and moving-clock relations retain elapsed clock readings, and unequal rates do not eliminate temporal succession [S-T11-001]. In the displayed Lorentz relation, positive elapsed time along a moving clock's path remains positive below light speed — a complete-history representation need not collapse ordered clock events into one simultaneous event. The inspected version is the 1999 Walker electronic edition of the 1923 Methuen translation, with notation and editorial corrections disclosed; the original German and modern precision tests were not audited.

Rovelli's relational formalism prices the more radical option. The inspected preprint distinguishes measurable partial observables from predictive complete correlations, treats physical clocks relationally, and shows a constrained formulation that does not require privileged external time — while explicitly restricting its detailed development to finite degrees of freedom and retaining histories and relational predictions [S-T11-002]. The version identity is disclosed rather than smoothed: the arXiv v3 header is dated 21 January 2002 while the rendered body carries a title date of October 23, 2018 — a retained discrepancy — and the published journal version was not separately inspected. A theoretical framework, not an empirically confirmed quantum-gravity cosmology.

The bridge assessment is where the audit does its work. Neither coordinate-invariant observables nor omission of privileged external time entails absence of physical change, experience or all intrinsic succession; and relativity, a relational formalism and eternalism do not alone identify an experiential bearer or the grounding relation a foundational-consciousness claim needs. A formal representation does not determine the full ontology of what it represents — even an eternalist reading contains earlier/later relations and does not identify a single experiencing mind. The recorded underdetermination principle generalizes the point: if rival metaphysical models yield the same physical and report predictions, those observations cannot distinguish them, and a discriminating test requires independently specified differing expectations.

The classical eternity proposal precedes its modern formalization. In the inspected 1897 translation of Boethius, Philosophy contrasts whole possession of life with endless sequential existence and places divine priority in simplicity rather than earlier duration; a separate book distinguishes unified providence from its temporal unfolding, and the foreknowledge reply distinguishes conditional from simple necessity [S-T11-003]. The record's version discipline applies: this is a dialogue voiced by a character in a historical translation whose "single moment" wording is read against its explicit contrast with temporal succession, not as a coordinate instant, and the account still owes an adequate relation between infallible knowledge, grounding and free choices.

Stump and Kretzmann's 1981 article, inspected as an exact journal copy, develops the richer proposal the article credits. Eternity is defined as life with illimitability, duration and no succession — distinguished from both mere atemporality and beginningless endless temporal life — and the authors expressly disclaim proving that an eternal entity exists or that God must be eternal [S-P2T12-001]. Their ET relation has an eternal and a temporal relatum and uses presentness relative to observers in both modes; it is symmetric but not reflexive or transitive, and it does not make two temporal events simultaneous merely because both relate to eternity. That last formal feature matters: the proposal need not collapse distinct temporal moments into one another, which is the crude objection it is built to avoid.

The 1981 model's mental-life sketch is candid about its limits. The proposed timeless mental life excludes deliberation, anticipation, memory and planning while considering knowing, willing and awareness; an independently existing immaterial mind is assumed for the argument, and the authors call the capacity sketch informal and insufficient as a possibility proof [S-P2T12-001]. The account distinguishes an atemporal act from its temporally located effect, proposing that a prayer and its response-effect can be causally dependent without the eternal willing occurring later than the prayer; the tensed-knowledge response says an eternal knower can know all events at each dated temporal now, and their being experienced as present, without a succession of divine knowledge states. The illustrative examples verify nothing — they state what the model would say if true.

Two internal boundaries deserve preservation because the survival question turns on them. In the 1981 hypothetical, a temporal person's life and death can each be present to an eternal entity without the person thereby existing eternally and without the two temporal events being simultaneous — the authors' own denial, later quoted by the article, that presence to eternity confers eternal existence on the temporal relatum [S-P2T12-001]. And the article's incarnation discussion invokes one person with divine and human natures while explicitly leaving the outline rudimentary and unanalysed; it supplies no general criterion of numerical identity for human persons. The proposal, at its strongest, concerns the eternal entity's relation to temporal lives — not those lives' continuation.

Lewis's 1984 reply, inspected as an exact journal copy, opens the critic chain. Direct temporal observation, he argues, requires its object to exist at that time — his broadly necessary principle allowing a causal remnant when an object has ceased — so a directly observed God would have at least the temporality of existing alongside temporal things [S-P2T12-002]. He challenges whether an eternal cause is part of reality when its temporal effect occurs, argues that objects being jointly present to an epistemic subject does not itself establish possible causal connection, and — the concession that frames everything after — grants the eternal being calendar and propositional knowledge while pressing that knowing which events presently exist is the missing item. The inspected copy misprints the 1981 source volume as 77 where the inspected 1981 header reads 78 — a recorded provenance anomaly that establishes no alternative chronology.

The 1988 criticism sharpens the demand from knowledge to existence. Lewis interprets the classical doctrine as requiring temporal things to be existentially, not merely epistemically, present in eternity, argues that its spatial-containment analogies require past, present and future things to be ontologically on a par, and concludes that eternal omniscience so understood entails tenseless time — noting the tension with tensed motivations for defending eternity [S-P2T12-003]. His further claim that tenseless persons cannot bear moral responsibility explicitly depends on his separate 1986 article, which the inspected 1988 paper does not develop; that argument and its actual reply are treated below as their own exchange. The classical texts Lewis quotes are quoted within his argument — the record did not independently reopen Boethius, Anselm or Aquinas editions for this packet.

The 1992 defense, inspected as an exact journal copy whose issue header reads October 1992 while its body contains an early-1993 reference — a recorded version anomaly — answers a specified target. The paper explicitly treats perfect possession of life as requiring eternity, uses analogical predication to resist both temporalizing divine duration and making it wholly equivocal, and illustrates with a specious-present discussion and a fictional story that stipulate rather than demonstrate cross-mode access [S-P2T12-004]. Its revised relation, ET′, adds capacity clauses: direct and immediate causal relations and, where capable, direct awareness, in both reference frames. Against a shared-mode requirement it deploys an internal analogy — a nonspatial God's awareness of spatial creatures — and the citation discipline is preserved: the paper explicitly answers Lewis's 1988 existential-presence objection paired with a Hasker objection, identifying Lewis 1988 in note 35, and does not cite Lewis 1984 in its complete body or notes, so the direct reply relation is established only for the specified 1988 target.

The recorded adjudication of the exchange is symmetric and unresolved. The revision makes the intended cognitive and causal relation explicit, and defining a capacity does not alone establish that its clauses are jointly possible; the defenders themselves acknowledge that three-dimensional "here" has no equivalent of the absolute temporal present, while maintaining in a note that their model need not deny real temporal passage — an acknowledged analogy limit that leaves the tense objection substantive [S-P2T12-004]. The article's verdict is inherited exactly: the reply improves the proposal by identifying the relation it needs; it does not establish the possibility of that relation by definition, and the absolute-present disanalogy is precisely where the spatial comparison may fail.

Craig's tensed-fact essay, inspected as an author-hosted page without a publication date in the inspected file, generalizes the presence objection into a dilemma for timeless omniscience. Conditional on objective A-theoretic tense and a knower's need to know what is presently true, a cognitively perfect ground of a tensed world requires intrinsic cognitive succession; Craig explicitly says a B-theory avoids the argument, and he argues that replacing propositional beliefs with direct intuition does not by itself remove the objection if irreducibly tensed reality must still be known [S-T11-004]. The recorded limits run in both directions: the disputed semantics and knowledge premise are not proven by the existence of a temporal world alone, and Craig's summaries of the modern defenders he criticizes are not substitutes for their uninspected originals.

Craig's personhood essay, inspected under the same version limits, defends the other flank. Necessary conditions of personhood must not be inferred solely from how finite humans learn; a possible knower may have changeless self-awareness and rational volition without deliberative updating, since not every intention is future-directed or reached by deliberation [S-T11-005]. His own preferred model — timeless without creation, temporal with creation — is recorded with its classification: a temporal alternative once a world exists, not foundation-level timelessness throughout the relation, and the transition into time must not posit a time before time. Simple personhood arguments do not automatically refute timelessness; actual interaction still needs a model, and neither an unexercised capacity nor a Trinitarian illustration supplies one.

What the timeless-foundation construction amounts to, on the record, is a conditional model with named debts. One non-successive act that knows and grounds a temporally ordered history can be posited, provided the grounding relation and individuated subjectivity are independently intelligible — a project reconstruction, not either source's verbatim model. Two supporting distinctions are recorded as contested rather than settled: knowing a dated event and each person's indexical belief need not mean being that person or successively acquiring those beliefs, and a changing time-indexed relation to a fixed foundation is distinct from a new intrinsic state of that foundation. The dependence audit adds the deflationary footnote: the Boethian and Craig discussions share premises about knowledge and personhood, so their agreement is not independent corroboration, and conditional coherence establishes neither actuality nor equal support against alternatives.

Preservation in a universal mind versus continued subjectivity

The survival consequence of the whole exchange is a non-entailment, and the record states it twice over. The selected model itself distinguishes the eternal knower and creator from temporal persons: a grounding or awareness relation is neither numerical identity nor a rule determining which later subject is the same human person [@S-P2T12-001; @S-P2T12-004]. Even actual eternal awareness of a finite life would not alone establish that its human subject has experiences beyond bodily death; both actual further subjectivity and a justified same-person relation remain necessary for a survival conclusion. The defenders' own boundary — presence to eternity does not make the temporal person eternal — is doing the work here, not a critic's importation.

The article's diagnosis of the preservation substitution is inherited with its record support. The recorded inference gives this teeth: preservation-only models can retain information, represented relationships or value while the original subject ceases, and upgrading preservation to survival requires a defended subject-preserving relation — a burden that cannot be discharged by renaming the foundation's knowledge as the person's life.

Identity criteria and their consequences

The psychological criterion enters through its classical source. Locke — inspected in the Bennett modernization of the chapter added to the 1694 second edition, with editorial additions marked — distinguishes identity of person, human animal and substance, and lets personal identity follow consciousness extending to past acts rather than sameness of material particles or soul [S-T13-001]. He considers forgetfulness, sleep, changing substances and the prince-and-cobbler transfer, a conditional illustration rather than an observed result, and his response to irreversible forgetting distinguishes person from man. The record's version note holds: this is a modernization, not a diplomatic original, and the correct criterion remains contested.

The repaired continuity account and its known failure modes are recorded as project analysis. Defining genuine memory as memory of one's own past can presuppose the identity it purports to establish, so a causal quasi-memory criterion must specify its link independently of sameness of person; overlapping causal links avoid requiring every later stage to remember every earlier stage, and a nonbranching clause addresses duplication — both repairs needing independent justification rather than stipulation. The branching constraint is structural: if two successors are distinct, strict numerical identity cannot identify both with the one predecessor, so branch-preserving concern or psychological succession uses a different relation than identity. These are logical observations under the project's definitions, with no inspected attribution to Parfit, Butler or Reid.

The organismic criterion is recorded through Olson's argument for animalism, inspected as a repository accepted version that includes a 2006 reference and cannot be assumed identical to the published 2003 chapter [S-T13-002]. A human organism is present; it thinks; there is exactly one relevant thinker; so the person is the organism — with the unique-thinker premise made explicit and the costs of denying animal thought or multiplying thinkers pressed against rivals. An animal can persist without the psychological features by which it is recognized, so a disembodied copy alone does not preserve the organism. The transplant intuition remains the standing objection, recorded as a competing conditional judgment; animalism alone is not a complete theory of death, resurrection or animal persistence, and Olson's reconstructions of other authors are not inspections of their originals.

The simple-subject criterion is recorded through Swinburne's short 2019 essay — a publisher blog piece, not the technical book argument. A hypothetical split leaves two equally psychologically supported candidates; if there is a determinate fact about which, if either, is the earlier subject beyond the total qualitative facts, that further fact requires a bearer the physical description misses, and a soul is the candidate [S-T13-003]. The recorded objection is precise: epistemic underdetermination does not entail an immaterial entity — the inference needs determinate further identity facts, their compatibility with the stipulated evidence, and a reason those facts require an immaterial bearer. And the recorded scope limit matters for this chapter: a simple soul is not immortality; a surviving soul and continuing experience require additional persistence and activation premises the essay does not supply.

Two cross-criterion inferences discipline the survival application. Interrupted awareness or ordinary forgetting does not alone decide persistence — an organism, a simple subject or an adequately continuous psychological history may bridge sleep under different assumptions, and no inference from bridging sleep to bridging death is recorded. And no selected identity criterion automatically establishes universal subjecthood, timelessness, intention, goodness or moral authority; each component needs its own bridge and specified bearer. The criteria adjudicate what survival would require; none of them supplies evidence that it occurs.

The tenseless-persons exchange, added by the Phase 2 reinspection as exact journal copies, supplies the missing developed human-identity objection. Lewis's 1986 article asks what human persons are and what cross-time sameness means, excluding the evidential question; it argues that tenseless time requires persons to be extended composites with temporal parts, and challenges responsibility assignment to a tenseless whole — which he denies is conscious — to a present stage for an earlier stage's deed, and to bundles of stages [S-P2T12-005]. Embodied actions and many mental actions, he argues, require an interval with a persisting agent; he concludes conditionally that tenseless persons cannot be morally responsible agents. The record preserves his own hedges: possible instantaneous mental acts are allowed through a quoted discussion, and his "perduring" denotes persistence generally, not the modern technical term.

Oaklander's reply — the actual published critique, its direct-reply relation established from body and reference rather than title or order — reconstructs the argument and proposes three diagnoses: a false premise, an invalid inference between senses of persistence, or a question-begging substantialist assumption [S-P2T12-006]. He disputes that tenselessness entails temporal parts, citing accounts inspected only through his presentation; offers temporally indexed properties without incompatible unqualified predicates; distinguishes reference to a stage from reference to the whole person; sketches psychological and physical continuity as alternatives compatible with agency; and separates the ordinary requirement that responsibility involve the same persisting agent from the stronger claim that agency requires a simple substance. He expressly declines to settle the tense debate, and his closing possible Lewis response is hypothetical — no later Lewis rebuttal was located in bounded forward searches, an absence that proves nothing.

The empirical material in this chapter's scope concerns how altered temporal experience is measured, and the instrument audit comes first. A high score on that item is therefore not a count of one uniquely defined timeless experience, and the original 1983 validation was not reinspected. The one controlled comparison in scope is Timmermann and colleagues' 2018 DMT study, inspected in full HTML without supplement or participant-level data. The recorded inference from the overlap is symmetric, and this chapter inherits it whole. 8. Does Significance Require Endurance?

The previous chapter separated survival from preservation and priced the timeless proposals that promise to avoid loss altogether. This chapter asks the value question those proposals serve: whether significance requires endurance. It expands the first half of the article's seventh section through two packets — the meaning-and-endurance audit and its Phase 2 reinspection — covering the final-loss argument in its strongest inspected forms, Wolf's engagement account of finite meaning, the Williams–Fischer immortality exchange, and Metz's purpose theory with its own author's qualifications. Loss, erasure and the final-loss argument

The article frames the strongest endurance argument as beginning with loss, not vanity. Love, inquiry and creative activity can be worthwhile yet vulnerable; death removes opportunities to sustain them and may frustrate projects whose value depends on continuation. A final-loss argument can therefore claim that lasting preservation would improve a life without assuming that only infinite achievements matter. The further step is where the argument divides: from the badness of ending valuable activity to the absence of value in the activity that occurred. If a friendship's ending is bad because the friendship mattered, that explanation does not by itself erase its earlier significance — the ending's badness presupposes rather than cancels the value it ends.

The necessity position is documented in its strongest inspected advocate. Craig requires both God and immortality for ultimate significance while denying that mere endless duration suffices, and he presents resurrection and fellowship with God positively while explicitly saying the discussion has not shown Christianity true [S-T14-001]. The record preserves both the position and its self-limit: this is a documented philosophical requirement claim, not an established value fact, and the essay's psychological assertions and claims against atheist morality remain unverified and outside the accepted attribution. The inspected version is the current author-site text; an older 1994 chapter identity is a search-metadata lead only.

The audit's reconstruction identifies what the necessity thesis owes. Endurance can enhance significance under stated premises — same subjects retained, relevant goods preserved, additional worthwhile experience available without overriding harm — but necessity requires more: a further principle that loss of all enduring bearers or recognition defeats every relevant kind of significance, not merely future continuation. That principle remains disputed, and the recorded annotation is pointed: calling the target "ultimate" cannot settle the value dispute by stipulation. Comparative enhancement and strict necessity are different theses with different burdens.

The Phase 2 reinspection supplies the exchange the first audit lacked. Yoshizawa's 2015 study explicitly targets the seventh chapter of Metz's 2013 book — a chapter whose identity is verified but whose body remains unavailable to this project, so its position is triangulated, never quoted [@S-P2T13-002; @S-P2T13-001]. Yoshizawa argues that a concern about losing already valuable ordinary life is not captured merely by demanding perfect value, and he builds a framework separating the person possessing meaning-making properties from the life assessed, whole from part, and pro tanto from on-balance meaning. His own earlier 2011 Japanese paper principally addressed Metz 2003 and was not inspected; the reconstruction of Tolstoy he works from is expressly not certified as Tolstoy's intended argument, by his own footnote.

Yoshizawa's decisive distinctions are the ones the erasure inference needs and lacks. He distinguishes an existing person without meaning-making properties from there being no such person, and rejects inferring general meaninglessness from the latter; he ultimately rejects the general claim that a person's death itself makes her life meaningless, favouring naturalistic objectivism — an attribution that does not establish naturalism about consciousness — while allowing that particular deaths prevent achievements and that continuity, posthumous events and others' survival can matter [S-P2T13-002]. He also expressly distinguishes the four-dimensional existence of a finite life from eternity or transcending temporality — a distinction the record retains with its limits that his Singer quotation is not an independent inspection of Singer and that neither eternalism nor a timeless conscious bearer follows. These are attributed philosophical positions, contested at every joint; their value is that they make the final-loss argument state its bridge instead of assuming it.

Metz's actual 2015 reply, inspected in its relevant sections, constrains the exchange in both directions. He grants the distinction between absent perfect value and losing imperfect value, and grants that the latter deserves attention — a concession of an omitted rationale, not of its soundness [S-T14-004]. He then objects that calling the ended life already meaningful concedes meaning without immortality, and proposes a stricter rationale under which the ending is what makes the life meaningless; he further questions whether endlessly retained love or contemplation would still be merely ordinary value. The record marks his suggested response on Yoshizawa's behalf as hypothetical — no inspected later publication continues the chain — and neither formulation proves a finite life valueless.

The recorded adjudication of the final-loss exchange is a failure-to-establish finding with explicit limits. The selected exchange does not establish that ending a life erases its earlier value; treating absence as an existing state without meaning requires a further bridge no inspected author supplies. The strongest strict-necessity advocate can dispute retrospective attribution of value, and the packet settles no theory of value or time — but the unmet burden is the loss-to-meaninglessness bridge, and the reconstructed strict rationale is endorsed by neither of the authors who examine it. Finite meaningful engagement

Wolf's account is the article's developed alternative to erasure, and the record supports its use within stated limits. Meaning, on her account, arises through active, caring engagement with independently worthy objects — subjective attraction and objective worth constraining each other — with pleasure alone insufficient and beneficial activity wholly disconnected from engagement insufficient too [S-T14-002]. Her illustrative counterexamples are philosophical devices, not clinical findings. The account's strength, as the article puts it, is to identify a kind of significance for which endless duration is not part of the proposed explanation: a finite episode could matter to the person living it and be worth that concern, without any claim about what happens later.

Wolf's own limits are preserved because they block a substitution the chapter must not make. She acknowledges that her account need not address the desire for a large, lasting cosmic impact; her final first lecture explicitly limits what a qualitative account offers a person distressed by cosmic smallness, and she declines to promise a theory of objective value or an infallible identification procedure [S-T14-002] — final-2010-lecture findings the Phase 2 reinspection recorded under the same source identifier whose canonical provenance remains the 2007–2008 draft, with no textual-identity claim between versions. Within-life meaning cannot simply replace the enduring or cosmic target that motivates the survival literature — the record states this as a scope limit on both sides, and the second lecture, commentaries and response were not inspected.

The finite-value argument the audit builds on this base is conditional and says so. A finite instance of learning, care or relief can benefit actual subjects, and the worth of that benefit need not depend entirely on subsequent recognition; the non-erasure principle — later absence alone does not retroactively make every earlier benefit worthless — grounds finite significance under an explicit value premise without answering why a cosmos exists or whether its subjects endure. The recorded objection and reply are preserved: a meaningful episode may still lack an ultimately worthwhile setting, and the reply distinguishes missing further goods from nonexistence of prior goods while expressly not claiming to answer the larger purpose question.

The desirability of endurance

Williams's challenge, inspected in the complete user-supplied 1973 chapter, is stronger than the boredom caricature and the record keeps it so. He grounds reasons to avoid death in categorical desires that motivate continuing life — death can be bad at a particular time even if endless life is undesirable, and no inference follows that every additional period is harmful [S-T14-007]. His challenge to immortality then imposes two conditions: an adequate immortal prospect must preserve the person, and it must relate appropriately to the aims motivating survival. Psychologically disjoint survival can meet the first condition while failing the second — being the same person and having a future worth anticipating as one's own are different requirements.

The heart of the challenge is impoverishment, not unpleasantness. Williams argues that enduring human life threatens categorical motivation through an impoverished relation to the world — not merely an unpleasant feeling of boredom — with extended fixed-character life undermining motivation while disjoint or radically accumulated lives fail the appropriate-relation condition [S-T14-007]. The record's classification is careful: this is supported source attribution replacing an earlier project-only reconstruction, its claimed necessity remains contested, and the modal step — generalizing from conditions of recognizably human character to endless human existence — is flagged as the argument's contested bridge. Williams addresses endless human life; application to a strict timeless foundation or transformed persons needs additional premises.

Fischer's reply, inspected in a later reprint layout, contests each pillar without claiming what it cannot. He distinguishes the identity condition from the attractiveness condition; separates outwardly absorbed experiential content from ownership of experience, rejecting the automatic inference from absorption to loss of the subject; argues an appealing endless life could contain varied activities and periods of boredom; distinguishes self-exhausting from repeatable pleasures, relative to a person and their distribution; and permits character changes arising through appropriate reflection to remain attractive while excluding coercion and deception [S-T14-003]. The recorded limits: possible desirability is not necessary goodness, objective meaning, physical feasibility or actual immortality, and the embedded Williams passages are Fischer's reconstruction, not original inspection.

The version note and the adjudication both matter here. The inspected Fischer copy is a reprint whose pages (224–238), editorial 1996 publication note and 2004 translation postdate the 1994 journal original; the Broadview 2009 anthology is a supported candidate edition, the PDF's 2022 dates do not date the argument, and the journal identity is supported by metadata while exact proof-to-final collation remains unresolved — an attribution qualification, not a defect in the argument [S-T14-003]. The recorded adjudication of the exchange keeps both sides alive: Fischer's variety, repeatability and ownership distinctions contest inevitable exhaustion and indiscriminate identity-loss claims, while a strengthened Williams-side response asks whether renewal preserves genuine engagement and prospective reasons rather than merely suppressing awareness of impoverishment. Occasional pleasantness does not settle that question; universal necessity is not established by the comparison; conditional worthwhile endurance remains open.

The comparative conclusion the record licenses is enhancement under premises, never more. Keeping the same persons in worthwhile relationships and projects could preserve goods lost on extinction and add significance — assuming same subjects, worthwhile content, non-dominating costs and an appropriate comparison rule — and it does not follow that every enduring life is better. The identity discipline of the previous chapter carries over unchanged: preservation of information, recognition or a complete history need not be the original person's continuing subjectivity, so the value comparison cannot be settled by a model that preserves a record while ending its owner. Possible worthwhile continuation requires relevant goods, an appropriate relation to the person, and actual further experience before it supports any survival claim.

The consciousness-factory proposal and its predictions

The proposal the research plan contributes turns on a distinction the record states exactly: a universe containing conscious life and a universe intended to produce conscious life are distinct hypotheses, and outcome alone supplies no observed designer preference or intended domain. Observing that experience occurs — however richly — does not by itself distinguish among the discrimination design's declared ends of production, development and preservation, or select an intended end over incidental occurrence. The factory metaphor is the research plan's name for the intended-production hypothesis; nothing in the occurrence of consciousness selects it.

What an intentional reading requires is recorded as a three-part debt. Purposeful creation can explain a cosmic role conditional on agency, an end and a causal or grounding link — and the worth of that role needs a separate normative premise, because authorship supplies a purpose relation, not worth by definition; arbitrary or harmful ends would still be purposes. The timeless-foundation constraint from the previous chapters recurs: a strictly timeless foundation cannot acquire experiences in sequence in the same respect, so a timeless agent model owes a coherent account of intention — whole-history intention or temporal agency — assessed with the same standards as rival primitive laws and value commitments.

Metz's 2000 article, inspected complete, supplies the purpose-theory groundwork with its own boundaries. Purpose theory is defined as a conditional account of what could confer meaning, not a claim that God or an assigned purpose exists, and it can permit meaning without an afterlife [S-P2T13-003]. The article distinguishes a purpose assigned from a purpose fulfilled, separates the grounding account from epistemic knowledge of which purpose or how to fulfil it, and argues some versions avoid the disrespect objection by making a respectful request for free moral action rather than manipulating — while rejecting an infinite-damnation version outright. These are conditional analyses resting on contested normative premises; no actual purpose is verified anywhere in the chain.

Metz's own principal objection and his qualifications are preserved together, because later commentary has blurred them. The 2000 article frames its objection around God's being necessary for any meaning — explicitly distinguished from God's possible enhancement of meaningful lives — and proposes that uniquely valuable divine qualitative properties best explain strict divine necessity while prima facie conflicting with purposiveness [S-P2T13-003]. The same original already says the objection does not require disbelief in purpose theory and presents two reply strategies, so the 2015 concessions — that he did not conclusively establish the atemporality-purposiveness incompatibility or the exclusivity of his preferred divine properties — are continuous with the 2000 text, not a wholesale retraction [S-T14-004]. Poettcker's and Affolter's originals remain uninspected, and no direct reply coverage is fabricated in their place.

The factory proposal's testable form is a design discipline, not a result. Production, development and preservation are different proposed ends; each must be paired with a declared population, means, constraints and tradeoffs, because outcomes weaken a model only when excluded or disfavored by fixed commitments made in advance. Observer selection belongs in every comparison — conscious observers necessarily observe from a conscious perspective — and the same data must be run across intentional, impersonal-teleological and incidental models. The recorded objection is preserved: unknown goals and resources can accommodate almost any outcome, so unrestricted hidden purposes discriminate weakly; only precisely constrained variants face qualitative tests. No population survey, likelihood assignment or completed test exists in the record, and the suffering analysis is deferred to the next chapter's material.

The dependence warning generalizes across this chapter and its neighbors. Consciousness-related value premises, and the same observed experiences reused across purpose, survival and foundation arguments, are not independent evidence — a life that motivates a survival hope, grounds a meaning claim and figures in a design argument is one evidential item, not three. And the three claims the factory proposal is tempted to run together stay separate by record: an occurrence of consciousness, an intended role, and a worthy role are distinct, with duration or value alone establishing none of the agency, intention and normative bridges.

Compatibility of finite meaning and valuable endurance

The article's constructive conclusion, which this chapter has now grounded record by record, is that neither position licenses a blanket verdict. An attractive possible continuation need not make compulsory immortality desirable for everyone; the possibility of boredom need not make every extension worthless. Finite meaning and valuable endurance are compatible claims: Wolf's account and the finite-value argument ground a kind of significance, under an explicit value premise, for which duration is not part of the proposed explanation, while the enhancement reconstruction and Fischer's replies keep worthwhile continuation open as a conditional possibility. A conscious foundation could enhance significance by sustaining worthwhile lives; it need not be invoked to rescue every finite good from meaninglessness.

What the compatibility conclusion does is remove a premise, not add one. It removes one reason for treating cosmology as a prerequisite of ordinary significance — the strict necessity thesis, which no inspected author successfully defends: Yoshizawa rejects the general claim that a person's death itself makes her life meaningless, and Metz proposes a stricter formulation without endorsing its soundness — without trivializing death's harms, which the final-loss argument locates in prevented goods and frustrated projects. It adds nothing to the metaphysical ledger: no enduring bearer, no actual continuation, no cosmic intention, no worthy purpose. The value question and the existence questions remain exactly as separable as the article claims, and the chapters on either side of this one carry the existence questions.

From explanation of agents to obligations between them

The article's framing sets the burden exactly. An account of why beings exist is not yet an account of what they owe one another; even shared consciousness would not settle whether another subject's interest supplies an overriding reason for me. The question is not whether a metaphysics can produce agents, or even valuable agents, but whether it can explain the normative relation between them — and the article's comparison finds each of its three stopping points, constructivism, realism and conscious authority, owing something at precisely this point.

The audit's strongest integrated consciousness-grounding case is recorded as a chain of separately contestable bridges. Constructivism and the reflective bridge

Korsgaard's proposal, inspected in the original chapters, is the article's constructivist bridge: an agent must take some consideration as a reason, and practical identity helps determine which considerations can be endorsed. She grounds practical authority in reflective agency without treating reflective self-conception as proof of a metaphysical self; links reasons and obligations to practical identity while distinguishing value from right action and authoritative requirements; argues that valuing anything requires valuing humanity as the source of contingent identities; and uses the shareability of reasons to defend obligations to others [S-P2T06-001]. The recorded argument seeks to connect commitment to one's own humanity with recognition of humanity in others — rather than deriving obligation from sheer power or causal origin, which is exactly the derivation the previous section found missing.

Korsgaard's own limits are recorded, and they matter because the exchange turns on them. She distinguishes formal universalization from a moral law ranging over all rational beings — the first argument does not by itself establish the interpersonal domain; the humanity argument's interpersonal extension requires an additional argument; she extends moral standing to conscious animals without making them reflective agents, on illustrations that are not independently verified empirical evidence in this packet; she denies that bare reflective endorsement makes every action right — psychological explanation alone is not her criterion of justification; and she permits conflicts between obligations while expressly not disproving complete practical normative skepticism [S-P2T06-001]. A theory this candid about its remaining steps supplies the map of what any successor — including a consciousness-based one — would have to complete.
