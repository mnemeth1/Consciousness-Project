**Research plan: Consciousness, existence, meaning, purpose, and morality**

Revised for AI execution, 11 September 2026. This is an investigation plan, not a report of completed findings.

**Central question**

Does an enduring, timeless consciousness provide a better foundational explanation of reality than its strongest alternatives, when we consider existence, qualia, the relationship between mind and brain, near-death experiences, meaning, purpose, and morality together?

The investigation will take the cumulative hypothesis seriously. It will assess whether a common foundation explains several phenomena together, while checking whether the proposed connections follow or require additional assumptions. Neither physicalism nor a divine mind will be assumed at the outset. A satisfying account and a true account are not automatically the same thing.

**1. Specify the hypothesis and its components**

Working hypothesis: conscious reality is fundamental; the physical universe and individual experience depend on it. This foundation is not confined to physical spacetime. Embodied lives may express or participate in it, and their experiences may retain significance beyond bodily death.

We will distinguish the following claims rather than treating them as a single package:

| Component | Question to establish | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fundamental consciousness | Is experience irreducible, rather than wholly explained by nonexperiential properties? | One mind, a creator, or an afterlife |
| Universal consciousness | Do individual subjects depend on one underlying mind? | That the mind is necessary, timeless, or morally good |
| Timelessness | Does that consciousness exist without temporal succession? | Persistence of individual persons |
| Endurance | Can consciousness exist independently of a functioning body? | Timelessness, rather than continued existence in time |
| Preservation | Are personal identity, memories, relationships, or the significance of experience retained? | That a retained record is a continuing subject |
| Explanatory foundation | Does conscious reality explain why the universe exists and has its structure? | Intentional creation or an ultimate purpose |
| Intention and purpose | Is existence directed toward ends? | That those ends are good |
| Moral foundation | Does the foundation ground objective value, reasons, or obligations? | That its power or commands alone create moral authority |

Definitions to settle in the opening memo: consciousness versus intelligence; fundamental versus necessary; timeless versus eternal; survival versus preservation; personal versus universal identity; causal explanation versus metaphysical dependence; subjective meaning versus objective value versus cosmic purpose.

Deliverable: a hypothesis statement, a glossary, and a table of necessary versus optional commitments. This prevents changing the hypothesis to fit each new result without acknowledging the change.

**2. Establish a fair comparison among alternatives**

Consider developed versions of:

- Physicalism with consciousness identified with, or weakly emerging from, physical organization.
- Fundamental psychophysical laws connecting physical organization with experience.
- Panpsychism or related accounts in which elementary reality includes experiential or proto-experiential properties.
- Dualism in which minds and matter are distinct, without assuming that minds survive or that God exists.
- Idealism or a universal-mind account, including versions with and without intentional creation.
- Theistic accounts in which a creator and created individual minds are distinct.

These overlap in places. We will compare explicit versions, not force them into mutually exclusive categories before their commitments are clear. A physicalist account need not entail moral nihilism, and consciousness surviving death need not entail theism.

Assess each for internal coherence, explanatory scope, independently supported assumptions, fit with evidence, capacity for prediction, and unresolved problems. Count complexity by the commitments an explanation requires, not merely by how many entities its vocabulary names.

Deliverable: a comparison matrix with each position's strongest argument and strongest unresolved challenge, represented in terms its defenders would recognize.

**3. Investigate existence, laws, and intelligibility**

Questions:

- Does contingent existence require a sufficient explanation?
- Is the universe contingent, and what warrants extending principles about its contents to the whole?
- Could an infinite explanatory chain suffice?
- Does a necessary foundation follow? If so, why identify it with mind?
- Can a mathematical description explain why anything instantiates that description?
- Is mathematical intelligibility more expected under a rational creator than under specified impersonal alternatives?
- Do fine-tuning arguments have defensible assumptions about possible values, probabilities, and observer selection?
- Does consciousness arise as a byproduct of the universe, or is there evidence it is an intended outcome?

Use John Lennox's arguments about mechanism and agency, intelligibility, reason, and the scope of science as a principal strand. Separate the argument that mechanisms do not exclude agency from the positive argument that agency is the best explanation. Examine the strengths and limitations of analogies to human designers.

Do not infer that mathematics is a physical actor, or that a successful model establishes its own metaphysical interpretation. Conversely, do not assume mathematically represented structures lack physical counterparts.

Deliverable: explicit argument maps showing premises, conclusions, objections, replies, and any remaining gap between a foundation and a conscious creator. Start with [Lennox's own argument](https://www.cslewisinstitute.org/resources/science-and-faith-friendly-allies-not-hostile-enemies/) and the [cosmological-argument literature](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmological-argument/).

**4. Investigate qualia and the explanatory gap**

Separate four achievements: explaining behavior; predicting reported experience; identifying conditions associated with experience; explaining why there is subjective experience at all.

Examine:

- Whether a complete physical description entails experiential facts, or whether the apparent gap reflects different ways of knowing the same reality.
- Whether weak emergence can explain subjectivity without assuming the conclusion.
- Whether strong emergence supplemented by fundamental psychophysical laws is coherent, and what kind of explanation it provides.
- Whether panpsychism improves the explanation and can resolve the combination problem.
- Whether a universal mind can explain separate, private subjects: the corresponding problem of explaining individual perspectives from a whole.
- Whether illusionist and identity accounts answer the question or successfully challenge assumptions within its formulation.

Evaluate concrete neuroscientific theories as well as philosophical proposals. Neural interventions and correlations constrain all accounts; they should not be presented as automatically establishing a metaphysical identity. Equally, lack of a satisfactory explanation is not a demonstration of impossibility.

Deliverable: a clear inventory of what each theory explains, assumes, and leaves unresolved. Starting sources: [Chalmers on the hard problem](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html), [strong and weak emergence](https://consc.net/papers/emergence.pdf), [panpsychism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/), [IIT 4.0](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465), and the [2025 theory-comparison experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1).

**5. Conduct a focused audit of the strongest NDE evidence**

Start with Mary Neal and Eben Alexander because they were specifically raised. Add cases selected for documentary strength and relevance, including cases identified by advocates and disputed cases identified by critics. Follow with a broader systematic review; the initial case sample must not be treated as representative.

For each case, record:

- The precise claim in its earliest available form.
- Medical chronology, treatment, monitoring, and what the records do and do not establish.
- When the experience was first reported, and when corroborating information became available.
- Independent witnesses and whether their statements were recorded before hearing the full account.
- Claimed accurate perceptions, foreknowledge, or recovery, assessed separately.
- Plausible ordinary information access, competing explanations, discrepancies, and unresolved documentation.
- Which hypothesis the case would support if its strongest claimed features were verified.

For foreknowledge, seek specific, independently dated records preceding fulfillment. Assess ambiguity, time windows, ordinary predictability, other predictions, and unsuccessful predictions. A retrospective assertion that a prediction occurred is evidence of a different strength from a contemporaneous record.

For unusual recovery, examine confirmed diagnosis, prognosis, interventions, recovery timeline, durability, and independent medical assessment. Distinguish an unexpectedly favorable outcome from evidence identifying its cause.

For perception during a crisis, establish exposure to information and physiological timing as closely as the records allow. Distinguish absent circulation from absent brain activity, and absence of detected signals from proof that all relevant processing was impossible. Do not claim a specific patient's experience is explained merely by naming a possible mechanism.

Include prospective research such as [AWARE II](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37423492/). Preserve its denominators and distinguish cardiac arrests, survivors, interviewees, monitoring, and target exposure. Neither unsuccessful target identification nor isolated successes should be overstated.

Credentials and sincerity matter to testimony, but do not establish its interpretation. Publication or financial benefit does not establish deception. Private testimony can have evidential weight even when public verification is limited; label that limitation rather than erase the evidence.

Deliverable: individual case dossiers and an evidence table distinguishing documented findings, corroborated testimony, uncorroborated testimony, interpretations, and unknowns. Entry point: [UVA's NDE research program](https://med.virginia.edu/perceptual-studies/our-research/near-death-experiences-ndes/).

**6. Assess the cumulative and historical NDE record**

Use a documented search strategy for prospective studies, retrospective collections, cross-cultural studies, and historical sources. Record inclusion criteria, duplicate accounts, recruitment methods, and reasons for excluding evidence.

Examine recurrence of clarity, bodily separation, life review, altered time, unity, encounters, and lasting changes. Include distressing experiences, experiences with conflicting implications, non-NDE survivors, and comparable reports outside medical crises.

Distinguish independently collected observations from copied narratives and shared cultural expectations. Historical records establish the antiquity of motifs with varying reliability; they are not automatically equivalent to medically documented episodes.

Compare what shared physiology, psychological processes, cultural transmission, and transcendent-access accounts predict. Similarities and differences should both count. Thousands of reports may establish a robust experience without constituting thousands of independent tests of one explanation.

Deliverable: a synthesis of recurring patterns, cultural and methodological variation, and the subset of claims that meaningfully distinguish explanations.

**7. Test timelessness, endurance, and personal identity**

Treat subjective timelessness as a finding to explain, without presuming it establishes existence outside physical time.

Separate reports of changed speed, indeterminate duration, lack of succession, simultaneity, and later narration of an apparently timeless experience. Do not use a percentage for general temporal alteration as a percentage for complete timelessness.

Investigate:

- Whether a timeless mind can know, intend, or ground changing experiences without itself changing.
- Whether embodied history could be encompassed as a whole instead of accumulated sequentially.
- Whether consciousness could survive in time rather than outside it.
- Whether individual identity requires continuity, memory, embodiment, or another relation.
- Whether preservation in a universal mind amounts to personal survival or only preservation of information.
- Whether any observations could distinguish these possibilities.

Include comparisons with other altered states. Similar phenomenology does not by itself prove identical causes, but it constrains claims that a feature is unique evidence of separation at death.

Deliverable: a set of coherent models connecting timeless consciousness and temporal life, each with explicit identity commitments and evidential limits. Relevant starting studies: [NDE temporal alteration](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00203/full) and [narrative sequence](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00311/full).

**8. Examine meaning and purpose as distinct but related questions**

Preserve the concern motivating the investigation: temporary local values may not supply the enduring significance or intention behind existence at the center of this project.

Distinguish:

- Meaning experienced within a finite life.
- Objective value independent of someone's preferences.
- Preservation of experiences, relationships, and understanding.
- A purpose intended for the universe as a whole.

Ask whether endurance is necessary for value, sufficient for greater value, or a source of a particular kind of value. Examine finite-value accounts fairly without using them as a substitute answer to the question of cosmic purpose.

Develop the consciousness-factory proposal explicitly: what would distinguish a universe that produces experience from one that exists in order to produce it? What does it predict about the distribution, development, suffering, and possible preservation of conscious life? Admit where the purpose remains unspecified.

Our desire for lasting significance identifies a question to address; by itself it does not establish the answer. A purpose hypothesis gains explanatory force when its intentions are specified independently enough to make some observations more expected than others.

Deliverable: an account of what enduring consciousness would explain about significance, and what still requires intention, value, or identity premises.

**9. Investigate morality's foundation**

Keep the origin of moral behavior separate from moral justification. An evolutionary account of cooperation does not by itself establish an obligation to cooperate.

Compare moral naturalism, rational or constructivist accounts, non-theistic non-natural moral realism, and theistic accounts of value and obligation. Separate these moral positions from assumptions about whether their adherents behave morally.

Evaluate the proposed divine foundation:

- Why would a creator's will have moral authority?
- Can obligations be grounded in a necessarily good nature without circularity?
- Does shared consciousness explain why others' suffering gives us reasons to act?
- Does unity supply a moral reason or only an incentive based on self-interest?
- Does enduring consciousness improve the grounding of morality, or mainly provide eventual accountability?

Study suffering and divine hiddenness as potential counterevidence to a loving, purposeful foundation. Distinguish challenges to that version of God from objections to every possible creator. Evaluate the strongest replies, including their explanatory costs.

Deliverable: an argument linking consciousness, value, and obligation, together with the strongest alternatives and outstanding objections. Starting points: [moral naturalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/naturalism-moral/), [moral arguments for God](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-arguments-god/), [evil](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evil/), and [hiddenness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-hiddenness/).

**10. Evaluate how the ideas stack together**

Assess the combined hypothesis after evaluating its components. Maintain two separate records:

1. An evidence record: observations, testimony, philosophical premises, and unresolved claims.
2. A dependence map: which conclusions depend on which assumptions.

Ask whether one independently motivated foundation explains several domains with fewer unsupported commitments, or whether each apparent success requires a new attribute: timelessness for survival, intention for purpose, goodness for morality, and additional rules for individual identity.

Test whether the framework retains explanatory power when its strongest disputed case or premise is removed. Examine contrary evidence rather than only how each observation can be accommodated.

Avoid counting the same underlying premise multiple times. For example, a conviction that experience has intrinsic value may inform arguments about morality and meaning without constituting two independent observations supporting a creator.

Deliverable: a cumulative comparison that identifies genuine mutual support, shared assumptions, independent evidence, and points where the package remains underdetermined.

**11. Update confidence transparently**

Withdraw the apparent precision of the earlier 40/30/20/10 allocation. If numerical estimates are eventually useful, provide ranges and show how they depend on assumptions, rather than manufacture precise likelihoods for poorly specified theories.

Assess confidence separately in:

- Irreducibility or fundamentality of consciousness.
- Consciousness independent of ordinary brain function.
- Survival of individual identity beyond death.
- Timelessness.
- A universal conscious foundation.
- Intentional creation or cosmic purpose.
- A morally good foundation.
- Any particular religious identification, only if separately investigated.

Prestate what would materially change confidence: reproducible or strongly corroborated inaccessible-information findings; specific prospective predictions; theoretical explanations with distinctive successful tests; demonstrable contradictions; failures of predictions; and evidence that initially compelling cases admit better-supported alternatives.

A finding can favor several alternatives at once. An anomaly does not automatically establish God; an adequate physical mechanism does not automatically disprove God.

Deliverable: a reasoned confidence assessment, its sensitivity to contested premises, and a list of observations or arguments most worth pursuing next.

**Execution order and completion criteria**

Work in this order: define the hypothesis and alternatives; audit the strongest cases and mechanisms; assess the broader NDE record; evaluate philosophical foundations and connecting arguments; synthesize the cumulative case.

The investigation is ready for a defensible provisional conclusion when each core claim has an explicit argument, the principal case claims have documented provenance or stated gaps, serious alternatives have been represented fairly, counterevidence has been addressed, and dependencies have been checked.

Final outputs: a source-linked synthesis, case and study evidence tables, an argument-dependence map, a comparison of explanatory strengths and weaknesses, and a transparent statement of remaining uncertainty.

The result could support the full proposal, support only some components, favor another account, or leave several accounts viable. The plan aims to learn which conclusion the evidence and arguments warrant.


**12. AI execution structure**

Execute this plan through an overseer, empirical and philosophy domain leads, bounded research agents, and independent reviewers reporting directly to the overseer. The overseer owns the canonical evidence record, task graph, resource budget, disagreements and final synthesis. A researcher cannot approve their own findings. Agent agreement is not independent evidence.
