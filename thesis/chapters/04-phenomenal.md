<!-- id: c04-p001 -->
The previous chapter inventoried the positions; this chapter analyzes the dialectic that motivates taking experience as basic, and prices it. The question is not which family is true — that question is gated and stays gated — but what, exactly, a theory purchases when it includes experience among its primitives, and what debts it takes on in the same transaction. Four exchanges from the record carry the analysis: the explanatory-gap debate between Chalmers and Papineau, the illusionism debate between Frankish and Nida-Rümelin, the causal-emergence debate among O'Connor and Wong, Kim and Shoemaker, and the constitution debate between Strawson and Goff. The chapter closes with the burdens that any conscious-ground proposal inherits, because those burdens structure everything the later, empirical chapters can and cannot test.

<!-- id: c04-p002 -->
A standing caution governs every sentence that follows. This chapter's inputs contain no empirical observation: they are inspected philosophical texts and reviewed project analyses of them. Every "argues," "proposes" and "concedes" below is a documented attribution to an identified version of a text, and no attribution establishes the truth of what is attributed. The exchanges are also internally dependent — the gap debate and its phenomenal-concepts sequel share authors and a running dispute, and each debate forms a single dependency group — so nothing in this chapter multiplies into independent lines of support however many claims it yields.

## The demand that survives functional explanation

<!-- id: c04-p003 -->
The demand is Chalmers's, and the record carries it as a distinction rather than a doctrine: explaining discrimination, access or behavioral control is one kind of task, and explaining why those processes have a subjective character is another [@S-T03-001]. The inspected text — an author-hosted version of the 1995 article, examined at its relevant sections — does not claim that functional explanation has achieved nothing. The claim is conditional and structural: completing the functional program would not obviously complete the different task of accounting for experience. That conditional is what survives ordinary explanatory success, and it is what every position in this chapter is answering, deflating or refusing.

<!-- id: c04-p004 -->
A theory that includes experience among its primitives meets the demand in one precise sense: it ceases to require the experiential to arise from a vocabulary stipulated to contain nothing experiential. This is genuine relief, but it is definitional relief. Declining to derive experience is not yet explaining anything about it — which experiences occur, in which systems, with which structure. The article's framing, which this thesis inherits, is that the advantage is real only if the demand is correctly identified, and the identification is exactly what the physicalist side contests.

<!-- id: c04-p005 -->
The record's developed antiphysicalist argument runs through knowledge and conceivability. On the author text, inspected at its relevant sections, Chalmers argues against the phenomenal-concept strategy and for a conditional conclusion: if the physical facts can be conceived without the experiential facts, and if the relevant conceivability entails the relevant possibility, physicalism is false [@S-T05-003]. The record marks all three bridge principles — the modal bridge from conceivability to possibility, the epistemic bridge from knowledge intuitions to conceivability, and the explanatory bridge from gap to ontology — as contested, and the conclusion as conditional on them. The text is an author PDF identified by exact URL, its publication year carrying 2006 and 2007 variants in discovery metadata.

<!-- id: c04-p006 -->
The physicalist defense in the record has two prongs. The first is the phenomenal-concept strategy itself: Papineau defends the view that phenomenal and physical concepts are distinct epistemic routes to one property, so epistemic difference need not refute identity [@S-T05-004]. The inspected text is an author-hosted typeset chapter whose exact publication year the record leaves unresolved. Chalmers's counter, in the same dependency group, poses a dilemma for the strategy — roughly, that the conceptual features doing the explaining either themselves resist physical explanation or fail to explain the epistemic situation they are invoked for, including its truth and justification — and the record leaves the dilemma, like everything else here, as an attributed argument with contested premises.

<!-- id: c04-p007 -->
The second prong attacks the demand's explanatory standard. On the inspected published article — an author-hosted text of the 2011 *Philosophia* paper — Papineau challenges what an identity owes us: identities, once warranted, need no further explanation, so the residual "why is this process experienced this way" question may be a demand for something no true identity could supply [@S-T05-005]. The felt gap is relocated into psychology — a resistance to identification whose causes are open to empirical study — rather than into ontology. This deflation does not close the gap; it reclassifies it.

<!-- id: c04-p008 -->
The methodological upshot, stated in the article this chapter expands, is that the burden runs both ways. The antireductionist must defend the transition from a gap in explanation to a gap in the world — the explanatory bridge is a premise, not a datum. The reductionist must show that the proposed identification preserves its explanandum rather than replacing it — an identity earns its explanatory relief only once warranted, and the mere possibility of identity explains nothing in particular. Neither move comes free, and the record prices both.

## Illusionism and the acquaintance exchange

<!-- id: c04-p009 -->
Illusionism makes the reductionist's burden — preserve the explanandum — unusually explicit, by refusing to preserve it and saying so. On the inspected author version, Frankish retains experience in an inclusive sense, perceptual activity, access, reports and introspective representation, while rejecting genuinely qualitative properties whether physical or nonphysical, so that the central task becomes explaining why a system represents itself as possessing such properties [@S-P2T05-001]. The record retains the account's self-description as a programme with proposed content mechanisms rather than a completed explanation, and its motivations — conservative explanation, introspective fallibility, abduction from anomalousness — as proposals whose premises are contested.

<!-- id: c04-p010 -->
The programme's structural discipline is worth separating from its truth. It avoids the conscious spectator: no inner audience inspects internal representations, since that audience would recreate the problem one level up. And it meets the regress worry — that each apparent feel needs a higher representation, threatening a circle or a concealed phenomenal starting point — with fine-grained nonphenomenal seemings and a limited higher-order stop. The record's assessment is exact on this point: the regress identifies a real explanatory constraint, not an automatic contradiction, once functional seeming and limited introspection are allowed. What the constraint costs is a worked account of why nonphenomenal representations present determinate apparent qualities; no such completed account is in the record.

<!-- id: c04-p011 -->
The recorded objection is stronger than the slogan that an illusion must itself be conscious — its author notes that quick line and sets it aside. On her inspected manuscript — an author manuscript whose final print was not available for byte-level or visual verification — Nida-Rümelin identifies experiential properties through subjects and examples, so their reference does not depend on a description an illusionist could reject, and argues that instantiating the relevant property already involves awareness of it [@S-P2T05-002]. Acquaintance, on this account, is not a second introspective judgment that could misrepresent an independently occurring state; there is no gap in which the illusionist's error theory can live. Her conclusion is expressly conditional on an experiential example actually being instantiated.

<!-- id: c04-p012 -->
Frankish's inspected reply grants the ordinary examples and the subject-property reformulation for argument, and denies precisely what the objection needs: that reference by example fixes the property's realist nature [@S-P2T05-003]. What the examples pick out may admit a representational interpretation; cognitive access to structural physical properties requires mediation, so robust unmediated acquaintance is rejected — a rejection the reply candidly derives from physicalist commitments and expects not to persuade this critic. The exchange ends with each side's decisive premise intact and explicitly identified: adequacy of the functional account against identification of awareness with instantiation.

<!-- id: c04-p013 -->
The dispute therefore concerns what must be preserved in explaining the examples, and the article's framing of the stalemate is the one this thesis keeps. Defining every seeming as phenomenal assumes the realist premise; defining apparent character as nothing beyond representation assumes too much the other way. The stronger illusionist response must explain the richness and authority of phenomenal judgments without quietly relying on their truth; the stronger realist response must show why its identification of the target resists that explanation. Within this dialectic, experiential primitiveness has a genuine dialectical advantage for the realist, which the record states carefully: it preserves rather than revises the target. It does not yet establish that preservation is explanatorily superior to a successful account of misrepresentation — and whether the misrepresentation account can succeed is exactly what the record marks unresolved.

## Emergence, exclusion and latent powers

<!-- id: c04-p014 -->
Accepting the target does not select an ontology, and the causal-emergence exchange shows why. O'Connor's 1994 formulation required supervenience, nonstructurality and downward causal influence [@S-P2T01-003]; the 2005 account with Wong explicitly repudiates the supervenience requirement and posits basic nonstructural properties causally generated and sustained over time by organized physical systems [@S-P2T01-001]. Both are inspected originals. The two dependence accounts are recorded as distinct and must not be merged: the model that matters downstream is the temporal, generation-and-maintenance one.

<!-- id: c04-p015 -->
Kim's challenge — on the inspected 2008 reprint; the original 1999 journal pagination was not inspected — is narrower than its reputation and sharper for it. He allows ordinary downward causation and treats phenomenal qualia as promising candidates for irreducibility; the argument is that where an emergent has a sufficient noncausal basal condition, the base threatens to preempt the emergent as a cause, and relocating the emergent's effects later in time does not by itself remove the threat [@S-P2T01-002]. The record marks the argument's own load-bearing premises — noncausal determination, basal sufficiency, exclusion of systematic overdetermination — as substantive commitments, not logical truths.

<!-- id: c04-p016 -->
The structural lesson of the reply is that naming an emergent property answers nothing; a premise must be rejected. The 2005 account rejects the noncausal-dependence premise: if the base causally generates the emergent, the emergent is an intermediate cause in a temporal process rather than a redundant competitor, and the preemption inference fails against this model. The record prices the escape exactly — a disputed simplicity comparison against latent-power alternatives, a thought experiment that stipulates its own discriminating conditions, contested first-person motivation, and no independently confirmed emergence law.

<!-- id: c04-p017 -->
Shoemaker's inspected countercase supplies the other horn of a productive choice. Micro-latent powers — powers of ordinary microconstituents manifested only in suitable, emergence-engendering combinations — let complete microfacts fix macrofacts while distinguishing causal power from mere nomic necessitation; his preferred identity-and-description treatment differs from irreducible emergent properties, and he considers and rejects a distinct-property option from his essential-individuation commitments [@S-P2T01-004]. The record's comparison point is exacting: a successful reply to Kim does not certify the nonstructural ontology, and deciding between the replies requires adjudicating property individuation, dependence and specified empirical discriminators — not counting replies.

<!-- id: c04-p018 -->
The exchange thus establishes a choice of explanatory commitments rather than a winner. Rejecting the qualified completeness of a nonexperiential causal account makes irreducible experiential causation intelligible, and incurs an obligation to explain the actual causal organization — including the inherited neural-dependence patterns any such model must reproduce. Preserving completeness through latent powers incurs the mirror obligation of specifying powers that nothing in familiar manifestations predicts. Neither side wins by treating its causal vocabulary as compulsory, and the record's cost inventory is explicitly not a parsimony score or a ranking.

## Constitution, combination and individuation

<!-- id: c04-p019 -->
Experiential constitution relocates the difficulty a third time. On the inspected later authorial version — the exact 2006 journal text was never inspected, and the two are one work-version family, not independent corroboration — Strawson argues conditionally: experience is concrete; assume reality is physical and constituted by ultimates; no experiential reality can emerge from wholly nonexperiential reality; so some ultimates involve experience, with panpsychism proper requiring a further homogeneity premise [@S-P2T03-004]. The no-radical-emergence premise is recorded as a defended intuition, not a demonstration, and the argument's conclusion as conditional on premises the record leaves unassessed.

<!-- id: c04-p020 -->
Goff's inspected objection asks why the forbidden brute emergence does not simply reappear one level down: a plurality of experiences does not transparently constitute a further unified experience, and if experiential being is exhaustively how it feels, a constituent's character should figure in the whole's — slight pains do not sum to a severe pain of a new quality [@S-P2T03-002]. The objection also carries a parity charge: Strawson's own use of experiential transparency against a posteriori physicalist identification threatens to prove too much, since experiential constitution is no more transparent than the physicalist's identity.

<!-- id: c04-p021 -->
The inspected reply concedes the ground it can afford and keeps the claim it cannot do without. Strawson accepts that the objection refutes one full-revelation reading of his view; distinguishes first-order acquaintance from exhaustive disclosure, so absent microsubjects in acquaintance do not establish absent microsubjects; revises his ontology toward active experiences with internal causal powers, an acknowledged cost; acknowledges a defective argument in his own text at the recorded locus; and concedes that he has no account of how microexperience constitutes macroexperience, resting the claim that it does on a transcendental argument plus smallism [@S-P2T03-003]. The record's verdict is symmetric restraint: neither constitution established nor impossibility demonstrated, with the positive burden undischarged.

<!-- id: c04-p022 -->
Taking experience as basic therefore answers the demand for experiential ingredients while transferring the burden to organization and ownership: which pluralities compose, under what relation, and why the result has one subject rather than many or none. The transfer is not a failure — as the alternatives chapter recorded, every family carries transferred burdens — but it is a debt, and the record forbids paying it with the observation that the ingredients are at least the right kind. Kind-rightness is the definitional relief of this chapter's opening section, purchased again at a lower level, and it explains nothing about composition.

<!-- id: c04-p023 -->
A universal consciousness reverses the direction of the same debt. Instead of composing one subject from many, it must differentiate many private perspectives within one — and the article's framing is that the reversal still owes an account of private perspectives and individuation. The legitimate attraction of a conscious ground, in either direction, is explanatory continuity: experience never has to arise from the nonexperiential. What continuity does not supply is a criterion of subject boundaries, an account of physical structure, or any discount on the individuation debt. The inspected constitution exchange, for its part, gives the reversal no support: micropsychism and experiential constitution entail no universal subject.

## The transferred burdens of a conscious ground

<!-- id: c04-p024 -->
What primitiveness purchases, this chapter inventories as the article's two listed advantages — a chapter-level application of that inventory, not a ledger census, and conditional throughout on the demand being correctly identified. First, definitional relief: the explanatory demand, if correctly identified, is met in the precise sense that nothing experiential must arise from a stipulated nonexperiential base. Second, dialectical position: against illusionism, the primitivist preserves rather than revises the target, which matters if and only if the target's realist identification survives the representational alternative. Both purchases are conditional on the demand being a fact about the world rather than a fact about our concepts — the very point Papineau's deflation contests and the record leaves standing as contested.

<!-- id: c04-p025 -->
What it does not purchase is any of the following, each blocked by a recorded boundary. Not explanatory superiority: preservation of the target has not been shown superior to a successful misrepresentation account, because no completed account exists on either side. Not a mechanism: generation laws, constitution relations and individuation criteria are all recorded as unsupplied. Not empirical advantage: the 2005 maintenance model must reproduce the inherited neural-dependence patterns and specify discriminating dynamics before empirical comparison, the constitution defender's recorded bound is his own acceptance of the relevant experiential states as brain states, and none of the inspected texts supplies a discriminating prediction. Not actuality: conditional availability of a model — causal-emergent, constitutive or universal — is availability, not evidence that the model is instantiated.

<!-- id: c04-p026 -->
The component boundaries translate these refusals into the thesis's working vocabulary. If the 2005 emergence ontology is granted, a limited property-level reading of conscious fundamentality is satisfiable despite instance-dependence — a conditional taxonomy fact, not an acceptance of the component. Causal mental influence under a maintenance condition establishes nothing about independence from brain function. Micropsychism and constitution entail no universal subject, no survival and no brain-independent consciousness; the inspected constitution defender accepts the relevant experiential states as brain states. And the inspected constitution chapter treats experience as requiring time, while the reply distinguishes activity from intentional agency; neither statement — nor anything else in these exchanges — supplies timelessness, preservation, intention, goodness or authority as established.
