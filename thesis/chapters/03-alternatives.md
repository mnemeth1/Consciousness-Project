<!-- id: c03-p001 -->
This chapter is the thesis's literature review. Its job is to set out the six families of positions against which every later assessment is made. They are physicalism with its illusionist edge, psychophysical-law and strong-emergence views, panpsychism, substance dualism, idealism in its universal and plural forms, and theism. Each family is presented through the strongest formulation available in the project record, together with its strongest recorded challenge and, where one was inspected, the actual reply. Let me be careful about that word "strongest." "Strongest available" is a bounded claim about this record, not about the literature as a whole. For that reason every position carries a coverage mark. It entered the record in one of four ways: as an inspected original text, as a secondary attribution reported through another inspected text, as a project reconstruction built for comparison, or as an uninspected variant known only as a lead. These marks do real work. The recorded synthesis constraints bar any broad ranking of families wherever a rival's strongest formulation was never directly inspected. They permit at most an explicitly narrower comparison of the formulations actually examined. And the frozen protocol's own header records a standing limit on the whole space, which I take up at the close of this chapter.

<!-- id: c03-p002 -->
The material comes in two layers, and the reader should keep them apart. At the project's opening, each family received what the project calls an operational comparator. This is a deliberately schematic model that stipulates four things: the family's required commitments, its principal explanatory bet, its strongest generic objection and its strongest generic reply. These comparators are project reconstructions. They were reviewed as method rather than as scholarship, and no philosopher is responsible for them. On top of them, the evidence tasks assembled developed exchanges: original defenses, direct objections and actual replies, each inspected at an identified version with locators. This chapter presents both layers and keeps them distinct. Why? Because a schematic model can be tidier than any position a philosopher actually holds, and that tidiness is itself a risk of distortion. Nothing in this chapter ranks the families. The comparison's verdict-bearing work happens downstream, under gates that remain withheld.

## Physicalist identity and the illusionist program

<!-- id: c03-p003 -->
Begin with the operational physicalist comparator. Its bet is that a complete physical realization account of experience is possible. The bridge that carries the bet is the principle that conceptual differences need not establish ontological differences. Its stipulated strongest objection is that phenomenal character may remain unexplained when every functional fact is in. Its stipulated reply is that identity or grounding need not be conceptually transparent. That is all a reconstruction can do: it sets the family's shape through a stipulated bet, objection and reply. The motivation recorded with the comparator is economy, in the sense that no extra ontological laws are required.

<!-- id: c03-p004 -->
The developed identity defense in the record is Papineau's. On the inspected text, he argues that a mind–brain identity can be warranted a posteriori, without the mental being derivable a priori from the physical. He argues further that an identity, once warranted, needs no further explanation. There is no residual question of why a thing is itself [@S-T05-005]. What becomes of the famous felt gap? Papineau relocates it. On his account it is not evidence of two properties but a psychological resistance to identification, and the causes of that resistance are open to empirical investigation. The project's conceptual analysis adds the discipline this exchange forces, and the discipline cuts both ways. A justified identity differs from an assumed one. And a conceptual gap alone neither refutes nor establishes ontological identity.

<!-- id: c03-p005 -->
Two working definitions from the record frame everything that follows. Weak emergence means that experience is completely grounded in the physical, with no new basic experiential law. Strong emergence adds irreducible properties or laws, and in that form it can overlap with property dualism. That line is the real divide. The families in this chapter differ, at bottom, on where they place experience relative to it, and on what they are prepared to pay for their placement.

<!-- id: c03-p006 -->
Illusionism is the record's developed program for answering the gap by denying its target. It does not try to close the gap. It denies the reality of the thing the gap seems to separate us from. Frankish's strong illusionism, on the inspected author version, denies genuinely qualitative phenomenal properties altogether, whether physical or nonphysical. It keeps experience in an inclusive sense: perceptual states, access, reports and introspective representations are all real, and none of them is phenomenally qualitative [@S-P2T05-001]. The central task then becomes explaining why systems like us represent ourselves as having qualitative states. Three motivations are recorded: conservative explanation, introspective fallibility and an abduction from the anomalousness of phenomenal properties. It should be said plainly that the account is a program with proposed content mechanisms, not a completed theory. The inspected text itself says so.

<!-- id: c03-p007 -->
The direct challenge in the record is Nida-Rümelin's. Her text was inspected as an author manuscript; the final print was not available for byte-level or visual verification. On that manuscript, the illusionist has misdrawn the target from the start. Phenomenal consciousness concerns subjects instantiating experiential properties, not inner events bearing theoretical "phenomenal properties." The relevant properties are introduced by examples, so their reference does not depend on any philosophical description an illusionist could reject [@S-P2T05-002]. Her positive premise is simple to state. To undergo an experiential property is already to be aware of it. Awareness is the instantiation itself, not a second, error-prone introspective act aimed at it. Two qualifications belong here. Her anti-illusionist conclusion is expressly conditional on at least one experiential example actually being instantiated. And she notes, but sets aside, the popular quick objection that an illusion of experience would itself have to be experienced.

<!-- id: c03-p008 -->
Frankish's actual reply was inspected in the same exchange. He grants the recasting in terms of subjects and properties for the sake of argument, and he accepts the ordinary examples. What he denies is that reference by example fixes the realist nature of what is referred to. The shared feature the examples pick out may admit a representational, nonphenomenal interpretation. Cognitive access to structural physical properties requires mediation, so direct acquaintance cannot, by his lights, settle the question [@S-P2T05-003]. He is candid about where this move comes from. His rejection of robust acquaintance flows from his physicalist commitments, and he does not expect it to persuade this critic. The reply also corrects an earlier causal shorthand: it is representations, not their nonexistent intentional objects, that produce effects. It distinguishes illusionism from physicalism itself, and from any blanket claim that experience judgments are unreliable. He even concedes that Russellian realism need not contradict science; he prefers illusionism on explanatory grounds alone.

<!-- id: c03-p009 -->
Where does the exchange rest? It is recorded as unresolved, and the open question is this: would an adequate functional account preserve the intended target, or would it quietly change the subject? There is also the regress worry. Each apparent feel seems to need a higher representation, which threatens either a circle or a concealed phenomenal starting point. The record treats this as a real explanatory constraint rather than an automatic contradiction, because the illusionist may allow fine-grained nonphenomenal seemings and a limited higher-order stop. Each side rests on a premise that carries the weight of its case: the adequacy of the functional account on one side, the identification of awareness with instantiation on the other. Both remain what they are. They are premises.

## Strong emergence and psychophysical laws

<!-- id: c03-p010 -->
The operational psychophysical-law comparator begins with a concession: additional experiential resources may be required beyond the physical inventory. Its bet is a constrained mapping. Fundamental laws link physical organization to experience, and they explain instances of experience without explaining their own existence. Its stipulated objection is that the central dependence stays brute. Its stipulated reply is that every model needs stopping points, and that the same explanatory stopping rules should apply across models. The anchoring attribution in the record is Chalmers's proposal of fundamental experience with supplementary psychophysical principles, which its author describes as naturalistic dualism [@S-T03-001]. The inspected text concedes that this does not explain the existence of basic experience.

<!-- id: c03-p011 -->
The recorded costs of this family are two, one causal and one epistemic. Under physical causal closure and a ban on systematic overdetermination, a distinct experiential influence threatens to be excluded. And if experience does not cause our reports, epiphenomenalism threatens any knowledge based on experience. The mapped replies split the family. One variant appeals to lawful tracking and direct acquaintance, with no extra physical effects. The other questions closure and proposes interaction laws. The comparison packet that mapped this split recorded, within its own scope, that the interaction and substance variants then lacked inspected direct chains of defender and reply. A later task repaired part of that remainder. The substance-dualism section below therefore rests on directly inspected Lowe, Engelhardt and Bennett texts, including Engelhardt's interactive inclusive variant. What still has no inspected defense of its own, within this chapter's inputs, is a Cartesian-style two-way interactionism. It appears only by contrast, in the non-Cartesian label of the inspected position. Two project analyses set the outer limits of the family's reach. First, property dualism alone supplies no detachable subject and no survival condition, since instantiation can remain tied to an organized substrate. Second, a closure-based exclusion argument needs three premises: sufficient physical causation, mental causal relevance and no overdetermination. Energy conservation alone is not that argument.

<!-- id: c03-p012 -->
Causal emergence is the strong-emergence wing of the family, and the record develops it through an inspected exchange. O'Connor's 1994 formulation required supervenience, nonstructurality and downward causal influence [@S-P2T01-003]. The 2005 account with Wong replaces it. That account posits basic nonstructural properties that organized physical systems causally generate and sustain over time; it rejects general synchronic supervenience, and it explicitly repudiates the earlier formulation [@S-P2T01-001]. The revision matters for everything downstream. On this model, experience is a caused, maintained and genuinely novel feature of certain systems. It consists of basic nonstructural properties, every instance of which depends on organized matter. What this means for fundamentality is deferred to its recorded conditional form below.

<!-- id: c03-p013 -->
The selected challenge is Kim's preemption argument. It is worth noticing what Kim allows. On the inspected reprint, he allows ordinary downward causation, and he treats phenomenal qualia as promising candidates for irreducibility. His challenge is narrower and sharper than a blanket denial. Where an emergent has a sufficient noncausal basal condition, the base threatens to preempt the emergent as a cause, and merely relocating the emergent's effects later in time does not remove the problem [@S-P2T01-002]. The argument has premises of its own: noncausal determination, basal sufficiency and exclusion. They are substantive, and the record treats them as such.

<!-- id: c03-p014 -->
The 2005 reply meets the argument at its noncausal premise. If the base causally generates the emergent, then the emergent is an intermediate cause in a temporal process, not a redundant competitor. Take away that disputed premise and the preemption inference fails against this model. The inspected text is honest about what the reply costs. It costs a disputed simplicity comparison against latent-power alternatives. It costs a history-dependent thought experiment that stipulates its own discriminating conditions. And it costs a contested first-person motivation. No independently confirmed emergence law is supplied, and the record notes the omission.

<!-- id: c03-p015 -->
Shoemaker's countercase shows that one can resist Kim without the nonstructural ontology. On the inspected article, he appeals to micro-latent powers: powers of ordinary microconstituents that are manifested only in suitable, emergence-engendering combinations. These powers let the complete microfacts fix the macrofacts, while keeping causal power distinct from mere nomic necessitation. Shoemaker considers a distinct-property option and rejects it, because he is committed to essential causal individuation [@S-P2T01-004]. The project's comparison point follows from this. To decide between the temporal-emergence reply and the latent-powers reply, one must adjudicate property individuation, dependence relations and specified empirical discriminators. Counting successful replies to exclusion will not do it.

<!-- id: c03-p016 -->
What does this family buy? On the record, something definite and strictly bounded. If the 2005 ontology is granted, strong emergence satisfies a limited property-level reading of conscious fundamentality, even though every instance is causally dependent. But the maintenance condition requires supporting organization. Causal mental influence therefore does nothing to establish brain independence, and the model must reproduce the neural-dependence patterns already in the record before any empirical comparison favors it. A fair cost inventory counts, on each side, basic experiential properties, latent powers and laws, generation and maintenance conditions, and the assignment of qualities and subjects. No inspected original derives a uniquely confirmed theory from those costs.

## Panpsychism and experiential constitution

<!-- id: c03-p017 -->
The operational panpsychist comparator bets that experiential constituents can support an account of complex subjects. Its bridge is an explicit constitutive relation that grounds unified subjecthood. Its stipulated objection is that many subjects do not evidently constitute one subject. The developed position in the record is constitutive Russellian panpsychism, as presented in the inspected Chalmers manuscript. A version note is needed at once: that manuscript is an undated author text labeled forthcoming, and the record does not fix its exact published version. On this view experience realizes the intrinsic natures of physical dispositions. Microexperience therefore has causal roles through physics rather than intervening from outside, and macroexperience is grounded in microexperience plus whatever further relations the base provides [@S-T12-001]. The attraction is integration. Phenomenality and causal relevance come in one package, without new macro-level laws.

<!-- id: c03-p018 -->
The same inspected text is the source of the family's canonical difficulty. Chalmers separates the combination problem into three components: subject, quality and structure. How do microsubjects make a macrosubject? How do microqualities make macroqualities? How does microstructure make the smooth structure of experience? He argues that solving one of these leaves the others standing. His anti-combination arguments rely on modal premises that he himself marks as disputable, and he treats phenomenal bonding as promising while questioning its nature and boundaries. The project analysis draws the family's outer boundary. An experiential intrinsic-nature model entails neither a subject for every aggregate nor one universal subject.

<!-- id: c03-p019 -->
The record's developed positive argument is Strawson's. On the inspected authorial version, realistic monism runs as a conditional argument. Experience is concrete reality. Assume that concrete reality is physical and constituted by ultimates. No experiential reality can emerge from wholly nonexperiential reality. Therefore some ultimates involve experience; that is micropsychism. Panpsychism proper follows only with an added homogeneity premise [@S-P2T03-004]. The premise that does the work, no radical emergence, is an intuition the text defends but cannot demonstrate. There is also a version boundary to respect. The selected 2006 journal text was never inspected. The inspected version is the later authorial chapter, and the two are one work-version family, not independent corroboration.

<!-- id: c03-p020 -->
Goff's inspected objection turns Strawson's own weapon against him. Microexperiences do not transparently supply a further subject. So the brute emergence that Strawson forbids between the nonexperiential and the experiential seems to reappear between micro and macro. Furthermore, Strawson uses transparent experiential knowledge against a posteriori physicalist identification, and that threatens to prove too much, since experiential constitution is no more transparent [@S-P2T03-002]. The phenomenal-parthood argument sharpens the point. If experiential being is exhaustively how it feels, then a constituent's character must figure in the whole's character, and slight pains do not sum to a severe pain of a new quality. On the stronger transparency and parthood premises, novel experiential combination is not merely unexplained. It is incoherent.

<!-- id: c03-p021 -->
Strawson's inspected reply concedes remarkably much while holding the line. He accepts that Goff's argument refutes one natural full-revelation reading of his position. His answer is a distinction between first-order acquaintance and exhaustive disclosure. Having an experience does not reveal every constitutive fact about it, so the absence of microsubjects from the macrosubject's acquaintance does not establish that they are absent [@S-P2T03-003]. Constituting microsubjects are numerically distinct, and they need not share their perspectives with the subject they constitute. Experiences are active realities whose causal powers are internal to their nature, and he explicitly acknowledges this as a revision of his earlier view. The concessions go further. He acknowledges a defective argument in his own text at the recorded locus. He concedes that he has no account of how microexperience constitutes macroexperience. He rests the claim that it does on a transcendental argument plus smallism, and he acknowledges that appeals to prior commitment stand in formal parity on both sides.

<!-- id: c03-p022 -->
Where does this leave us? With restraint on both sides. The exchange does not establish subject combination. Nor does it demonstrate that combination is impossible, except by relying on the disputed revelation and parthood bridges. An epistemic defense does not discharge the positive burden of showing how constitution works. And the family's reach is bounded on its own terms. Micropsychism and experiential constitution entail no cosmic subject, no surviving person and no brain-independent human consciousness. Strawson himself accepts the relevant experiential states as brain states within his account, and he treats experience as requiring time.

## Substance dualism

<!-- id: c03-p023 -->
The operational dualist comparator posits distinct, irreducible bearers of subjectivity that stand in coherent and constrained relations to bodies. Its stipulated objection is conflict with a complete physical causal account. The developed position is Lowe's non-Cartesian substance dualism, on the inspected article. The self is a persisting bearer of properties, distinct from its body and its parts. Lowe motivates that distinctness by considerations about replacement and about what bears thoughts, and he makes no commitment to the possibility of disembodiment [@S-P2T02-001]. His causal proposal turns on a distinction between intentional fact causation and neural event causation. The self causes the fact that some appropriate neural events occur, while the physical event chain remains complete. Closure is conceded conditionally, for the argument's sake.

<!-- id: c03-p024 -->
The recorded challenges are two. Engelhardt argues that the sufficiency of physical events supports the sufficiency of occurrence facts, which reinstates exclusion against Lowe's fact-causation distinction [@S-P2T02-002]. A version note applies. The Engelhardt text was inspected through an author-linked transcription carrying the printed page markers; the exact publisher text is unverified, and the author's page generally labels its linked works penultimate drafts. Bennett's inspected chapter applies her counterfactual overdetermination test to dualism, conditional on physical completeness. Genuine independent sufficient causes must pass counterfactual independence checks that dependent causal descriptions fail. A dualist with merely contingent psychophysical links therefore needs a response that the physicalist's dependence strategy cannot supply. The exception would be a coherent, motivated necessitating relation between the distinct substances, an option she doubts but does not close [@S-P2T02-003].

<!-- id: c03-p025 -->
Engelhardt's own inclusive variant answers by revising the position. Let the distinct mind and body share one causally relevant physical property instance, so that two bearers do not supply two independent physical pushes. And require the counterfactual comparisons to hold a defensible embodiment relation fixed, rather than severing that relation and testing a different situation [@S-P2T02-002]. The inspected text is careful about what it claims. It disclaims meeting the independent-powers ambition that it attributes to Lowe's fuller view. Colocation, token property sharing and the embodiment relation stand as substantive burdens. A correlation that is merely named will not carry them.

<!-- id: c03-p026 -->
What has the exchange achieved? It improves the conditional comparison of bearer models and causal models. It does not establish that a distinct mental substance exists. Two boundary inferences matter for later chapters. First, a primitive continuing subject supplies no observed postmortem identity test, so the exchange establishes nothing about survival or timelessness. Second, the exclusion burden concerns sufficient causes, dependence and event individuation. Energy conservation alone does not settle this dispute either way.

## Idealism: universal and plural grounds

<!-- id: c03-p027 -->
The operational idealist comparator bets that a conscious basis could ground both experience and a public, ordered world, and that it would earn its support through overall explanatory success. Its stipulated objection is that experiential knowledge does not prove a mental ontology. The record's developed universal version is Kastrup's dissociation-based cosmic idealism. It was inspected as an identified journal-layout version, accessed through a transcription with printed page markers. The model has three parts. There is one cosmic conscious primitive, whose patterns of self-excitation are experiences. Finite private perspectives arise by dissociation, located at the boundaries of living organisms. And bodies and brains are the extrinsic appearances of dissociated experiential processes, with cross-boundary impingement and coded perception explaining the perceived world [@S-T12-002]. The selling point is economy: one ontological kind. The inspected text conditions that economy explicitly on explanatory adequacy.

<!-- id: c03-p028 -->
The record is strict about what supports this model. Kastrup's clinical dissociation findings, his no-report paradigms, his quantum-measurement claims and his biological illustrations all enter the record as attributions; their original studies were not inspected. No clinical or physical fact is accepted from the philosophical paper. The project analyses press two structural points. Dissociation in embodied patients does not by itself establish that a cosmic subject divides. The analogy must ground finite phenomenality, not merely block communication. And to call neural activity an appearance, one must recover exact neural interventions and public regularities; otherwise the data are redescribed rather than explained. A lawful shared mental world is a coherent target for comparison. But specifying a shared substrate does not derive its particular laws.

<!-- id: c03-p029 -->
The recorded serious challenge is Chalmers's conditional subject-constitution objection. It was inspected in draft and final-layout versions, and the record leaves their exact publication history unresolved. The objection runs as follows. A cosmic subject with all its cosmic mental states can seemingly be conceived without any further distinct macrosubjects. If grounding requires necessitation, and if the relevant conceivability entails possibility, then pure cosmic grounding of distinct subjects fails [@S-T12-003]. Chalmers also considers the identity reading, on which we are the cosmic subject under restricted modes. He treats this mode-relative cognitive fragmentation as coherent but expensive, in introspective and interpersonal costs. The final layout adds a no-highest-level countercase against cosmic holism. One asymmetry must be noted. The inspected Chalmers text engages Kastrup's earlier paper, not the inspected 2018 article. The confrontation is therefore an argumentative reconstruction, not a verified direct reply chain.

<!-- id: c03-p030 -->
The same material generalizes into the record's comparison of cosmic and plural idealism. Whole-first models relocate the constitution problem rather than automatically solving it. Physical holism alone does not select an experiential ground. Identity versions revise ordinary individuality, and nonidentity versions owe a sufficient grounding principle. Plural idealism, with multiple basic subjects lawfully coordinated, is a recorded alternative, and it permits a mental ontology without one universal subject. A shared bearer, a shared kind of reality and numerical identity are three different claims. Motifs of unity establish none of them.

<!-- id: c03-p031 -->
The record's developed plural comparator is historical, and it was inspected: Leibniz. The Monadology argues from composites to genuine simples. It rejects any mechanical explanation of perception by the mill argument. It equips each monad with an intrinsic representational nature and a qualitative individuality, and it distinguishes bare perception from apperception, so that not every monad is a conscious person [@S-T10-002]. One disclosure belongs here. Those Monadology claims were inspected in the 1998 Francks and Woolhouse translation, at printed pages 268 to 270, 274 to 277 and 279. The record carries them under the same source identity as the Bennett modernization at the cited address, whose own inspected sections concern sufficient reason, God and the best world. The Bennett text was not reinspected for the mill argument. Created monads remain plural while depending on God and representing one universe. Ideal coordination and distinct laws for soul and body do not identify created subjects with one mind. The New System supplies the 1695 published statement of substantial unities and divine initial coordination. It was inspected in an identified translation with its later amendments marked [@S-P2T04-002].

<!-- id: c03-p032 -->
The inspected challenge is Foucher's 1695 reply, and it presses three points. Coordinated sequences are possible, but why have bodies at all, if appearances are internally sufficient and bodies are unnecessary? A theory built to preserve prior separations may lack independent justification. And interaction by effort and resistance is excluded only by premises a critic need not grant [@S-P2T04-003]. Leibniz's actual 1696 published explanation answers on the record. Harmony is grounded in law rather than in perpetual intervention. The foundations of mass differ from abstract extension, and genuine animal unity differs from clockwork. God prefers a plurality of substances with external correspondence. Force is intrinsic, and conservation is invoked against interactionism [@S-P2T04-004]. The limits are recorded too: abbreviated translations, an omitted chain of rejoinders, and theological premises of divine actuality, choice and coordination doing essential work.

<!-- id: c03-p033 -->
What do these inspected versions give the family? A developed and bounded comparison: universal and plural mental ontologies, each with explicit mechanisms and explicit costs. They establish none of the following: fundamental or universal consciousness, brain independence, survival, timelessness, preservation, intention, goodness or authority. Leibniz's conclusions about preservation and moral order rest on additional theological premises, and the comparison does not adjudicate them.

## Theistic accounts

<!-- id: c03-p034 -->
The operational theistic comparator is built from two bets. Contingent reality may require explanation, and intentional agency may provide it. Its stipulated bridge is unusually demanding, and the project imposed that demand deliberately. Independent arguments are required for agency, for good ends and for moral authority, because explanation alone does not identify mind, goodness or accountability. The stipulated strongest objection is that very gap. The stipulated reply is to supply the distinct arguments, and to test the specified ends against suffering and hiddenness rather than asserting them.

<!-- id: c03-p035 -->
Within this chapter's inputs, the theistic family's developed content is thinner than that of its rivals. This must be said plainly. The family appears here as the project-stipulated comparator, plus the inspected theological commitments inside the Leibniz texts: divine creation, coordination and a preference for plurality. Those commitments enter as attributions within an idealist comparison, not as a defended contemporary theism. The developed positive material bearing on this family, the necessary-foundation arguments, fine-tuning and design, is assigned under the Phase 3 plan to the existence chapter. The moral-grounding material is assigned to the morality and goodness chapters. Those assessments are made in chapters 5, 9 and 10, and none of them is imported into this chapter's comparison. For the comparison in this chapter the consequence is a coverage asymmetry, and it has teeth. There is no primary contemporary theist defender among this set's inspected originals. The recorded constraints therefore bar broad elimination claims against the family, and they equally bar any credit to it beyond its stipulated shape.

## Common evaluation criteria and overlap

<!-- id: c03-p036 -->
All six families are assessed under the same recorded conventions. Each declared model carries required, excluded and open commitments. These are classifications of content, not of confidence. Moral and identity positions are never inferred from family membership. The comparison criteria are those of the methodology chapter, operationalized in the project's comparison annex as six checks. The last check is remaining problems: what a position still cannot explain after its best defense. The duty to take the strongest formulation runs symmetrically. The record's illusionism is Frankish with his concessions intact, and the record's dualism is Lowe with the exclusion challenge at full strength.

<!-- id: c03-p037 -->
How do the coverage marks fall across this chapter's inputs? What follows is this chapter's own application of the recorded schema to the versions its records name; it is not the result of a dedicated coverage audit. The inspected original chains of defender, objection and reply are these: the illusionism exchange, the causal-emergence exchange, the panpsychist combination exchange, the substance-dualist exclusion exchange including Engelhardt's interactive inclusive variant, and the Leibniz-Foucher exchange. The inspected originals without full reply chains are Papineau's identity defense; Chalmers's combination analysis, inspected as an undated forthcoming manuscript; Chalmers's idealism analysis, inspected in both draft and final layout with its exact publication history unresolved; and Kastrup's 2018 article. The six operational comparators are project reconstructions. The secondary attributions are Kastrup's clinical and physical premises, the commentator positions seen only through Frankish's replies, and Goff's cited antecedents. The uninspected variants stand just as the records leave them: the exact 2006 Strawson journal text, the 2021 reprint of the Chalmers idealism chapter, and the later rounds of the Shoemaker exchange. Verdicts in later chapters are restricted by these marks.

<!-- id: c03-p038 -->
Dependence crosses family lines, and the record tracks it. Consider the three Chalmers papers, which the record treats as one dependent conceptual lineage. One author's framework supplies the foil for the identity dialectic, the taxonomy of the combination problem and the machinery of the idealism objection. Material used across three of this chapter's sections is therefore partly one thinker's premises, and any cumulative weighing counts it once. Each developed exchange is likewise one dependent unit, however many claims it yields. The illusionism records share a single-exchange dependency group, and so do the emergence, combination, exclusion and Leibniz materials. Shared premises that carry weight are tracked the same way. No radical emergence, the bridges from conceivability to possibility, and grounding as necessitation each enter the record once, as a labeled premise, and never as an added observation. That is the same treatment shared samples receive in the empirical chapters.

<!-- id: c03-p039 -->
One constraint binds every family alike. Reviewed neural dependence constrains each model's mapping to embodied experience, whether the model works by identity, psychophysical law, constitution, distinct substance or appearance-grounding. By itself, however, it decides nothing among them. And one prohibition binds every family's ambitions. None of these family-level arguments automatically supplies personal survival, timelessness, preservation, intentional creation, goodness or moral authority. Those components need their own bridges, argued in their own chapters. Where a component has not been assessed, that absence is never converted into equal support.

## What this chapter has not shown

<!-- id: c03-p040 -->
Let me be clear about what this chapter has not shown. This survey establishes a comparison space, not a result. It cannot rank the six families, and it does not. The coverage asymmetries just recorded are the very conditions under which the recorded constraints withhold family rankings. There is an uninspected exact first version at the heart of the panpsychism exchange. There is an undated forthcoming manuscript in the combination material, and an idealism analysis inspected in draft and final layout but with its publication history unresolved. The Kastrup and Engelhardt texts were inspected through transcriptions. The theistic family is present mainly by stipulation. And every one of the twelve withheld stronger-conclusion gates remains withheld at the end of this chapter. None is opened here. Two standing limits frame the whole space. The frozen protocol's own header records that the six families were not source-verified as representative of the literature when they were fixed. And the register's original-defender coverage gap remains open. The five inspected exchanges are selected chains, and the register is explicit that completing later packets does not by itself close gaps in representative coverage. Alternatives that are unassessed or thinly covered are not thereby refuted. Nor are they thereby supported.

<!-- id: c03-p041 -->
What the chapter does deliver, and what later chapters consume, is a disciplined inventory. For each family it gives a strongest recorded formulation with named versions, a strongest recorded challenge met by an actual reply where one exists, explicit bridge premises, explicit costs, and a coverage mark on every text that bears weight. Each of the six families remains a live comparator, and each carries specific unresolved burdens. The physicalist must show that the target is preserved. The psychophysical-law theorist has a brute mapping, and on the family's strong-emergence wing the generation laws are unconfirmed. The panpsychist is silent on constitution. The dualist owes an answer to exclusion. The idealist owes accounts of individuation and lawfulness. The theist has not discharged the agency and goodness bridges. But liveness is not support. A position present mainly by stipulation stands in the comparison without acquiring the standing of the inspected ones, and the absence of assessment is never converted into equal support. No metaphysical conclusion follows from any of this, and none is drawn. All twelve withheld stronger-conclusion gates remain withheld at the close of this chapter.
