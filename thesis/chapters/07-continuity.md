The previous chapter closed with an asymmetry: controlled interventions supply discriminating observations about neural dependence, while crisis reports supply reports requiring explanation. This chapter takes up the question that would remain even if that asymmetry reversed — what personal survival requires beyond the recurrence of a conscious state or the preservation of information, and whether timelessness offers a way to avoid loss altogether. It expands the article's sixth section through three packets: the physics and philosophy of time audit, which prices the distance between physical formalism and a timeless conscious bearer; the personal-identity audit, which sets out psychological, organismic and simple-subject criteria and what each costs; and the Phase 2 reinspection of the Stump–Kretzmann eternity proposal with its complete critic-and-reply chain, including the separately inspected Lewis–Oaklander exchange over tenseless persons and moral responsibility. The chapter closes with temporal phenomenology treated as a finding to explain, the dependence accounting, and what none of this material concludes.

## Survival, recurrence and preservation distinguished

<!-- id: c07-p003 -->
The article's framing sets the target. Even evidence of consciousness under unexpected physiological conditions would leave personal survival to be argued, because survival concerns a further subject who is the same person — not merely the recurrence of a type of conscious state, and not merely the preservation of information about a life. A duplicate's accurate memories could matter greatly without settling strict identity, and concern for a successor may be rational without answering the original survival question. The project's component definitions hold the boundary: survival requires the same person's subjectivity beyond irreversible bodily death, and nothing weaker — resemblance, record, representation, another subject's complete knowledge — satisfies that requirement by redescription.

<!-- id: c07-p004 -->
The audit distinguishes the candidate models by what each would deliver. A first model is temporal continuation: personal persistence within time, before and possibly after bodily death, with no timeless foundation required. A second adds a timeless foundation grounding temporal persons, where individual survival remains an additional condition the foundation does not automatically supply. A third proposes complete-history inclusion: every event of a finite life represented in, or present to, an eternal knower. A fourth is preservation without survival: information, represented relationships or value retained while the original subject ceases. The recorded inferences keep these apart — inclusion of a finite life or its record alone does not meet the survival requirement, and a conscious foundation's awareness of a life is not automatically that life's subjectivity.

<!-- id: c07-p005 -->
The handoff inference states why the distinction is load-bearing rather than pedantic. Representing or including all events of a finite life preserves specified contents; a record or a containing knower is not automatically numerically identical to the represented person. An enlarged history containing genuinely continuing subjectivity outside embodied succession is a distinct, stronger hypothesis — one deserving assessment under an explicit identity relation, which is precisely what the identity-criteria section below supplies. Merely calling the inclusion survival does not establish it, and the burden list is recorded: bearer, death condition, numerical identity, continuation domain, memory and branching.

## Eternity proposals and the presence objection

<!-- id: c07-p006 -->
Physics enters first, because timelessness is often introduced with its vocabulary. Einstein's 1905 kinematic argument derives frame-dependent distant simultaneity from a relativity principle, invariant vacuum light speed and clock synchronization; the inspected transformation and moving-clock relations retain elapsed clock readings, and unequal rates do not eliminate temporal succession [@S-T11-001]. In the displayed Lorentz relation, positive elapsed time along a moving clock's path remains positive below light speed — a complete-history representation need not collapse ordered clock events into one simultaneous event. The inspected version is the 1999 Walker electronic edition of the 1923 Methuen translation, with notation and editorial corrections disclosed; the original German and modern precision tests were not audited.

<!-- id: c07-p007 -->
Rovelli's relational formalism prices the more radical option. The inspected preprint distinguishes measurable partial observables from predictive complete correlations, treats physical clocks relationally, and shows a constrained formulation that does not require privileged external time — while explicitly restricting its detailed development to finite degrees of freedom and retaining histories and relational predictions [@S-T11-002]. The version identity is disclosed rather than smoothed: the arXiv v3 header is dated 21 January 2002 while the rendered body carries a title date of October 23, 2018 — a retained discrepancy — and the published journal version was not separately inspected. A theoretical framework, not an empirically confirmed quantum-gravity cosmology.

<!-- id: c07-p008 -->
The bridge assessment is where the audit does its work. Neither coordinate-invariant observables nor omission of privileged external time entails absence of physical change, experience or all intrinsic succession; and relativity, a relational formalism and eternalism do not alone identify an experiential bearer or the grounding relation a foundational-consciousness claim needs. A formal representation does not determine the full ontology of what it represents — even an eternalist reading contains earlier/later relations and does not identify a single experiencing mind. The recorded underdetermination principle generalizes the point: if rival metaphysical models yield the same physical and report predictions, those observations cannot distinguish them, and a discriminating test requires independently specified differing expectations.

<!-- id: c07-p009 -->
The classical eternity proposal precedes its modern formalization. In the inspected 1897 translation of Boethius, Philosophy contrasts whole possession of life with endless sequential existence and places divine priority in simplicity rather than earlier duration; a separate book distinguishes unified providence from its temporal unfolding, and the foreknowledge reply distinguishes conditional from simple necessity [@S-T11-003]. The record's version discipline applies: this is a dialogue voiced by a character in a historical translation whose "single moment" wording is read against its explicit contrast with temporal succession, not as a coordinate instant, and the account still owes an adequate relation between infallible knowledge, grounding and free choices.

<!-- id: c07-p010 -->
Stump and Kretzmann's 1981 article, inspected as an exact journal copy, develops the richer proposal the article credits. Eternity is defined as life with illimitability, duration and no succession — distinguished from both mere atemporality and beginningless endless temporal life — and the authors expressly disclaim proving that an eternal entity exists or that God must be eternal [@S-P2T12-001]. Their ET relation has an eternal and a temporal relatum and uses presentness relative to observers in both modes; it is symmetric but not reflexive or transitive, and it does not make two temporal events simultaneous merely because both relate to eternity. That last formal feature matters: the proposal need not collapse distinct temporal moments into one another, which is the crude objection it is built to avoid.

<!-- id: c07-p011 -->
The 1981 model's mental-life sketch is candid about its limits. The proposed timeless mental life excludes deliberation, anticipation, memory and planning while considering knowing, willing and awareness; an independently existing immaterial mind is assumed for the argument, and the authors call the capacity sketch informal and insufficient as a possibility proof [@S-P2T12-001]. The account distinguishes an atemporal act from its temporally located effect, proposing that a prayer and its response-effect can be causally dependent without the eternal willing occurring later than the prayer; the tensed-knowledge response says an eternal knower can know all events at each dated temporal now, and their being experienced as present, without a succession of divine knowledge states. The illustrative examples verify nothing — they state what the model would say if true.

<!-- id: c07-p012 -->
Two internal boundaries deserve preservation because the survival question turns on them. In the 1981 hypothetical, a temporal person's life and death can each be present to an eternal entity without the person thereby existing eternally and without the two temporal events being simultaneous — the authors' own denial, later quoted by the article, that presence to eternity confers eternal existence on the temporal relatum [@S-P2T12-001]. And the article's incarnation discussion invokes one person with divine and human natures while explicitly leaving the outline rudimentary and unanalysed; it supplies no general criterion of numerical identity for human persons. The proposal, at its strongest, concerns the eternal entity's relation to temporal lives — not those lives' continuation.

<!-- id: c07-p013 -->
Lewis's 1984 reply, inspected as an exact journal copy, opens the critic chain. Direct temporal observation, he argues, requires its object to exist at that time — his broadly necessary principle allowing a causal remnant when an object has ceased — so a directly observed God would have at least the temporality of existing alongside temporal things [@S-P2T12-002]. He challenges whether an eternal cause is part of reality when its temporal effect occurs, argues that objects being jointly present to an epistemic subject does not itself establish possible causal connection, and — the concession that frames everything after — grants the eternal being calendar and propositional knowledge while pressing that knowing which events presently exist is the missing item. The inspected copy misprints the 1981 source volume as 77 where the inspected 1981 header reads 78 — a recorded provenance anomaly that establishes no alternative chronology.

<!-- id: c07-p014 -->
The 1988 criticism sharpens the demand from knowledge to existence. Lewis interprets the classical doctrine as requiring temporal things to be existentially, not merely epistemically, present in eternity, argues that its spatial-containment analogies require past, present and future things to be ontologically on a par, and concludes that eternal omniscience so understood entails tenseless time — noting the tension with tensed motivations for defending eternity [@S-P2T12-003]. His further claim that tenseless persons cannot bear moral responsibility explicitly depends on his separate 1986 article, which the inspected 1988 paper does not develop; that argument and its actual reply are treated below as their own exchange. The classical texts Lewis quotes are quoted within his argument — the record did not independently reopen Boethius, Anselm or Aquinas editions for this packet.

<!-- id: c07-p015 -->
The 1992 defense, inspected as an exact journal copy whose issue header reads October 1992 while its body contains an early-1993 reference — a recorded version anomaly — answers a specified target. The paper explicitly treats perfect possession of life as requiring eternity, uses analogical predication to resist both temporalizing divine duration and making it wholly equivocal, and illustrates with a specious-present discussion and a fictional story that stipulate rather than demonstrate cross-mode access [@S-P2T12-004]. Its revised relation, ET′, adds capacity clauses: direct and immediate causal relations and, where capable, direct awareness, in both reference frames. Against a shared-mode requirement it deploys an internal analogy — a nonspatial God's awareness of spatial creatures — and the citation discipline is preserved: the paper explicitly answers Lewis's 1988 existential-presence objection paired with a Hasker objection, identifying Lewis 1988 in note 35, and does not cite Lewis 1984 in its complete body or notes, so the direct reply relation is established only for the specified 1988 target.

<!-- id: c07-p016 -->
The recorded adjudication of the exchange is symmetric and unresolved. The revision makes the intended cognitive and causal relation explicit, and defining a capacity does not alone establish that its clauses are jointly possible; the defenders themselves acknowledge that three-dimensional "here" has no equivalent of the absolute temporal present, while maintaining in a note that their model need not deny real temporal passage — an acknowledged analogy limit that leaves the tense objection substantive [@S-P2T12-004]. The article's verdict is inherited exactly: the reply improves the proposal by identifying the relation it needs; it does not establish the possibility of that relation by definition, and the absolute-present disanalogy is precisely where the spatial comparison may fail.

<!-- id: c07-p017 -->
Craig's tensed-fact essay, inspected as an author-hosted page without a publication date in the inspected file, generalizes the presence objection into a dilemma for timeless omniscience. Conditional on objective A-theoretic tense and a knower's need to know what is presently true, a cognitively perfect ground of a tensed world requires intrinsic cognitive succession; Craig explicitly says a B-theory avoids the argument, and he argues that replacing propositional beliefs with direct intuition does not by itself remove the objection if irreducibly tensed reality must still be known [@S-T11-004]. The recorded limits run in both directions: the disputed semantics and knowledge premise are not proven by the existence of a temporal world alone, and Craig's summaries of the modern defenders he criticizes are not substitutes for their uninspected originals.

<!-- id: c07-p018 -->
Craig's personhood essay, inspected under the same version limits, defends the other flank. Necessary conditions of personhood must not be inferred solely from how finite humans learn; a possible knower may have changeless self-awareness and rational volition without deliberative updating, since not every intention is future-directed or reached by deliberation [@S-T11-005]. His own preferred model — timeless without creation, temporal with creation — is recorded with its classification: a temporal alternative once a world exists, not foundation-level timelessness throughout the relation, and the transition into time must not posit a time before time. Simple personhood arguments do not automatically refute timelessness; actual interaction still needs a model, and neither an unexercised capacity nor a Trinitarian illustration supplies one.

<!-- id: c07-p019 -->
What the timeless-foundation construction amounts to, on the record, is a conditional model with named debts. One non-successive act that knows and grounds a temporally ordered history can be posited, provided the grounding relation and individuated subjectivity are independently intelligible — a project reconstruction, not either source's verbatim model. Two supporting distinctions are recorded as contested rather than settled: knowing a dated event and each person's indexical belief need not mean being that person or successively acquiring those beliefs, and a changing time-indexed relation to a fixed foundation is distinct from a new intrinsic state of that foundation. The dependence audit adds the deflationary footnote: the Boethian and Craig discussions share premises about knowledge and personhood, so their agreement is not independent corroboration, and conditional coherence establishes neither actuality nor equal support against alternatives.

## Preservation in a universal mind versus continued subjectivity

<!-- id: c07-p020 -->
The survival consequence of the whole exchange is a non-entailment, and the record states it twice over. The selected model itself distinguishes the eternal knower and creator from temporal persons: a grounding or awareness relation is neither numerical identity nor a rule determining which later subject is the same human person [@S-P2T12-001; @S-P2T12-004]. Even actual eternal awareness of a finite life would not alone establish that its human subject has experiences beyond bodily death; both actual further subjectivity and a justified same-person relation remain necessary for a survival conclusion. The defenders' own boundary — presence to eternity does not make the temporal person eternal — is doing the work here, not a critic's importation.

<!-- id: c07-p021 -->
The article's diagnosis of the preservation substitution is inherited with its record support. Complete divine awareness could preserve the truth about a finite life while adding no further experience for its subject; a theory that preserves information while ending its owner answers a different existential concern than the one that motivates survival hope. Wanting one's relationships and activity to continue is not satisfied by their being eternally known. The recorded inference gives this teeth: preservation-only models can retain information, represented relationships or value while the original subject ceases, and upgrading preservation to survival requires a defended subject-preserving relation — a burden that cannot be discharged by renaming the foundation's knowledge as the person's life.

<!-- id: c07-p022 -->
The strongest survival-compatible proposal in the record is stated with its costs. An enlarged first-person history — the foundation grounding a concrete history that includes subjectivity outside the embodied-life boundary, unified with the embodied life by a defensible identity relation — could satisfy the survival requirement even if the foundation has no intrinsic succession. The recorded objection is the honest one: the asserted ownership or extension may simply restate survival, since a universal knower can preserve a finite life without the person continuing; the recorded reply treats subjectivity and individuation as substantive ontology with identifiable constraints, which blocks dismissal by definition while adding explanatory and evidential costs. Two of those costs are recorded by name: the strict organism criterion rejects disembodied extension unless a same-organism relation is defended, and "beyond" the bodily life must be specified relationally if not temporally. No observation in the record establishes an enlarged history, and the physics of the earlier section supplies no discriminating prediction for one.

<!-- id: c07-p023 -->
Relativity and dimensional stories recur inside the eternity literature, and the reinspection packet prices them the same way the physics audit did: in the selected articles they function as analogies, and neither formal simultaneity nor physical theory supplies an observed timeless mind or a discriminating prediction for the model [@S-P2T12-001; @S-P2T12-004]. The recorded conclusion boundary for the whole exchange follows: no family winner, and no automatic upgrade from conceptual coherence to actuality, fundamentality, universality, survival or timelessness of human subjects. All twelve withheld stronger-conclusion gates remain withheld through this material.

## Identity criteria and their consequences

<!-- id: c07-p024 -->
The psychological criterion enters through its classical source. Locke — inspected in the Bennett modernization of the chapter added to the 1694 second edition, with editorial additions marked — distinguishes identity of person, human animal and substance, and lets personal identity follow consciousness extending to past acts rather than sameness of material particles or soul [@S-T13-001]. He considers forgetfulness, sleep, changing substances and the prince-and-cobbler transfer, a conditional illustration rather than an observed result, and his response to irreversible forgetting distinguishes person from man. The record's version note holds: this is a modernization, not a diplomatic original, and the correct criterion remains contested.

<!-- id: c07-p025 -->
The repaired continuity account and its known failure modes are recorded as project analysis. Defining genuine memory as memory of one's own past can presuppose the identity it purports to establish, so a causal quasi-memory criterion must specify its link independently of sameness of person; overlapping causal links avoid requiring every later stage to remember every earlier stage, and a nonbranching clause addresses duplication — both repairs needing independent justification rather than stipulation. The branching constraint is structural: if two successors are distinct, strict numerical identity cannot identify both with the one predecessor, so branch-preserving concern or psychological succession uses a different relation than identity. These are logical observations under the project's definitions, with no inspected attribution to Parfit, Butler or Reid.

<!-- id: c07-p026 -->
The organismic criterion is recorded through Olson's argument for animalism, inspected as a repository accepted version that includes a 2006 reference and cannot be assumed identical to the published 2003 chapter [@S-T13-002]. A human organism is present; it thinks; there is exactly one relevant thinker; so the person is the organism — with the unique-thinker premise made explicit and the costs of denying animal thought or multiplying thinkers pressed against rivals. An animal can persist without the psychological features by which it is recognized, so a disembodied copy alone does not preserve the organism. The transplant intuition remains the standing objection, recorded as a competing conditional judgment; animalism alone is not a complete theory of death, resurrection or animal persistence, and Olson's reconstructions of other authors are not inspections of their originals.

<!-- id: c07-p027 -->
The simple-subject criterion is recorded through Swinburne's short 2019 essay — a publisher blog piece, not the technical book argument. A hypothetical split leaves two equally psychologically supported candidates; if there is a determinate fact about which, if either, is the earlier subject beyond the total qualitative facts, that further fact requires a bearer the physical description misses, and a soul is the candidate [@S-T13-003]. The recorded objection is precise: epistemic underdetermination does not entail an immaterial entity — the inference needs determinate further identity facts, their compatibility with the stipulated evidence, and a reason those facts require an immaterial bearer. And the recorded scope limit matters for this chapter: a simple soul is not immortality; a surviving soul and continuing experience require additional persistence and activation premises the essay does not supply.

<!-- id: c07-p028 -->
Two cross-criterion inferences discipline the survival application. Interrupted awareness or ordinary forgetting does not alone decide persistence — an organism, a simple subject or an adequately continuous psychological history may bridge sleep under different assumptions, and no inference from bridging sleep to bridging death is recorded. And no selected identity criterion automatically establishes universal subjecthood, timelessness, intention, goodness or moral authority; each component needs its own bridge and specified bearer. The criteria adjudicate what survival would require; none of them supplies evidence that it occurs.

<!-- id: c07-p029 -->
The tenseless-persons exchange, added by the Phase 2 reinspection as exact journal copies, supplies the missing developed human-identity objection. Lewis's 1986 article asks what human persons are and what cross-time sameness means, excluding the evidential question; it argues that tenseless time requires persons to be extended composites with temporal parts, and challenges responsibility assignment to a tenseless whole — which he denies is conscious — to a present stage for an earlier stage's deed, and to bundles of stages [@S-P2T12-005]. Embodied actions and many mental actions, he argues, require an interval with a persisting agent; he concludes conditionally that tenseless persons cannot be morally responsible agents. The record preserves his own hedges: possible instantaneous mental acts are allowed through a quoted discussion, and his "perduring" denotes persistence generally, not the modern technical term.

<!-- id: c07-p030 -->
Oaklander's reply — the actual published critique, its direct-reply relation established from body and reference rather than title or order — reconstructs the argument and proposes three diagnoses: a false premise, an invalid inference between senses of persistence, or a question-begging substantialist assumption [@S-P2T12-006]. He disputes that tenselessness entails temporal parts, citing accounts inspected only through his presentation; offers temporally indexed properties without incompatible unqualified predicates; distinguishes reference to a stage from reference to the whole person; sketches psychological and physical continuity as alternatives compatible with agency; and separates the ordinary requirement that responsibility involve the same persisting agent from the stronger claim that agency requires a simple substance. He expressly declines to settle the tense debate, and his closing possible Lewis response is hypothetical — no later Lewis rebuttal was located in bounded forward searches, an absence that proves nothing.

<!-- id: c07-p031 -->
The exchange's recorded yield is bounded on all sides. The claimed necessary incompatibility of tenselessness with human moral agency is contested by explicit alternatives, and neither article establishes an evidential same-person test, a postmortem subject or an actual timeless ground. One further distinction guards the chapter's central boundary: tenseless existence across ordered times is distinct from the nonsuccessive life proposed for an eternal ground, so preserving human numerical identity on a tenseless model would neither remove temporal succession nor establish a timeless human subject. The 1988 moral-persons consequence now traces to an inspected original and an inspected direct reply — a completed chain that adds argument coverage, not evidence.

## Temporal phenomenology as explanandum

<!-- id: c07-p032 -->
The empirical material in this chapter's scope concerns how altered temporal experience is measured, and the instrument audit comes first. Greyson's 1990 comparison — the self-selected newsletter cohort met in the previous chapter — uses a broadened time item whose highest response option groups apparent simultaneity, stopped time and time losing meaning into one category [@S-T08-006]. A high score on that item is therefore not a count of one uniquely defined timeless experience, and the original 1983 validation was not reinspected. Composite instruments mix distinct phenomenologies; any argument from scale endorsements to a specific temporal ontology inherits that mixture.

<!-- id: c07-p033 -->
The one controlled comparison in scope is Timmermann and colleagues' 2018 DMT study, inspected in full HTML without supplement or participant-level data. Thirteen psychedelic-experienced volunteers received placebo and DMT in a fixed placebo-first order with single blinding and heterogeneous doses; all 13 met the NDE-scale threshold — a score of 7 or above — after DMT against one at threshold after placebo, repeated conditions in 13 people, not 26 independent people [@S-T13-004]. The time-item means were 1.15 (SD 0.80) under DMT and 1.54 (SD 0.78) in the separate 13-person retrospectively recruited NDE comparator, selected as above the standard threshold with no raw boundary-score list published and no response-category counts supplied; non-significance is not an equivalence test. The recorded case notes add that the comparator's published sex counts (seven women, six men) differ from the experimental group's (six women, seven men) despite described matching, that comparator overlap with other collections is unestablished, and that sample sizes are not to be added.

<!-- id: c07-p034 -->
The recorded inference from the overlap is symmetric, and this chapter inherits it whole. Phenomenological overlap after DMT shows that similar scale endorsements can arise in a drug context; it does not establish endogenous DMT as any particular crisis experience's cause, and it does not explain away any specific anomalous detail. Conversely, the survival-friendly reply — that drugs might change access to experience rather than generate it — is a possible interpretation requiring additional discriminating predictions, not a rebuttal the data supply. Resemblance alone identifies neither mechanism nor ontology; the recorded recommendation is prospective designs with independently scored information and resolved timing, plus the separate question of what identity relation any result would support.

<!-- id: c07-p035 -->
The crisis-report material already audited contributes exactly one thing here: reported temporal phenomenology as a documented explanandum. Alexander's reviewed altered-duration account — months or years seeming to pass — concerns remembered phenomenology under an inherited attribution from the audio-unverified talk transcript, and it is not the same claim as no intrinsic succession [@S-T06-001]. Resuscitated-subject reports and aggregate brain-monitoring records do not supply the survival component's irreversible-death boundary, episode-specific experience timing or a numerical-identity bridge [@S-T07-007]. Subjective temporal alteration is a finding to explain; it is not evidence of ontological timelessness, in either the experiencer or the world.

<!-- id: c07-p036 -->
The coding discipline closes the loop between phenomenology and ontology. Reports of no succession, simultaneous content, indefinite duration and later narrative order require separate coding, because testimony may support the occurrence of an experience without establishing the ontology attributed to it — and the converse guard is preserved: an atemporal awareness could be narrated sequentially afterward, so narrative order alone does not refute it [@S-T08-006; @S-T13-004]. No validated instrument in the record uniquely identifies timelessness. The explananda are documented and recurrent reports; their ontological interpretation is exactly as open as the eternity exchange left it.

## Dependence accounting, access gaps and non-conclusions

<!-- id: c07-p037 -->
The dependence structure for this chapter is dominated by shared philosophical premises rather than shared samples. The physics material divides into the Einstein kinematic formalism, the Rovelli relational formalism and the physics-to-metaphysics bridge; the classical and modern eternity material divides into the Boethian timeless-knowledge group, the Craig tensed-knowledge and personhood groups, the timeless-foundation construction, and the eternity-personhood exchange (Stump–Kretzmann 1981 and 1992, Lewis 1984 and 1988), with the Lewis 1986–Oaklander 1987–88 exchange as its own group; the recorded shared-premises claim marks the Boethius and Craig discussions as non-independent. The identity criteria carry per-author groups (Locke, Olson, Swinburne) plus the project's identity-bridge inferences; the empirical material carries the DMT study group, the IANDS-descended retrospective group for the Greyson instrument, and the inherited Alexander and resuscitation-report chains from chapter 6, which add no new observations here. Nothing in this chapter sums across a group.

<!-- id: c07-p038 -->
The access gaps are declared. The Craig essays lack publication dates in their inspected versions; repository metadata attributing one to April 2000 was not independently checked. Modern defenders Craig criticizes, the authors Olson reconstructs, the accounts Oaklander cites, and the technical literatures behind the 1992 defense's third-party discussions remain uninspected originals; Parfit was not directly accessed. The published version of Rovelli's preprint, the original German Einstein, the Latin Boethius, the diplomatic Locke and the published 2003 Olson text were not inspected; the Swinburne essay is not his book-length argument. A bounded forward search located no later Lewis rebuttal to Oaklander — a search result, not a literature fact — while the absence of any Lewis 1984 citation from the 1992 paper's complete body and notes is an inspection finding in the exact 1992 copy, not a search result. What this chapter cannot conclude, it states plainly: it cannot conclude that any timeless model is actual or impossible, that any identity criterion is correct, that anyone survives death, or that any reported temporal phenomenology reveals the world's temporal ontology. The eternity proposal remains an improved, still-unproven relation; the preservation substitution fails as an answer to survival; and every component of the fundamental-consciousness package — fundamentality, universal subjecthood, brain independence, survival beyond irreversible death, timelessness, preservation, intention, goodness, moral authority — remains exactly as unestablished after this chapter as before it, with all twelve withheld stronger-conclusion gates intact.
