<!-- id: apD-p001 -->
This appendix is the map behind chapter 11. It records which conclusions depend on which observations, which testimony, which philosophical premises and which identity and value assumptions, and it shows where the same commitment is doing work in more than one place. The map was built at the accepted synthesis stage as thirty-one inference routes toward the nine hypothesis components and four cross-cutting targets, with thirty-four project bridge premises and fourteen dependence groups. Three reading rules apply throughout. Within a route the required inputs are conjunctive, so the route delivers nothing unless every one of them holds. Across routes, separate support routes for one component are alternatives, and no independence or exhaustiveness is assumed among them. And a challenge route is not a logical negation of a support route; it constrains, and only an explicitly marked definitional consequence counts as entailment. The bridge premises are project-authored conditional premises whose truth is not established by their listing. Everything here is a reading convenience over canonical records; the claim, argument, case and source identifiers are the evidence, and this appendix adds none.

## The bridge premises

<!-- id: apD-p002 -->
Thirty-four premises carry the proposal from its records to its conclusions, and each is stated here in plain words so that a reader can see exactly what is being assumed where. Three of them are required inputs of no route. The necessity premise and the unity-to-obligation premise are overlays introduced at the sensitivity stage to test extensions that no support route in the map actually uses. The agency premise, that an actual agent directs the relevant cosmic domain toward independently specified ends, appears in the map as a node and as one of the thirteen added commitments of the full package. But no route requires it. The only intention support route requires the candidate and expectation premises instead, and the sensitivity report keeps actual-agent grounding separate from that candidate-conditioned comparison. The other thirty-one are inputs to routes. None of the thirty-four is a finding. Where a premise is contested in the record, the chapter that examines it says so.

- PHEN: there is a phenomenal explanatory target beyond a merely verbal report target.
- MODAL: the relevant ideal conceivability gap warrants a metaphysical non-grounding conclusion.
- FUND: the unexplained experiential feature is basic, rather than strongly emergent or grounded in another irreducible but nonbasic bearer.
- IDENTITY: independent explanatory and empirical warrant identifies or fully physically grounds experience despite distinct concepts.
- PSR: an adequate noncircular explanation is required for the contingent concrete totality, with a concrete ultimate ground.
- MENTAL: the independently warranted ultimate ground is experiential, and an impersonal necessary basis is insufficient under a defended comparison.
- GROUND: a specified conscious basis actually grounds concrete physical reality and its laws, and the relation does explanatory work.
- INDIV: one conscious subject or whole grounds all subjects in the declared domain through a defensible individuation relation, without equating mutually distinct subjects.
- NEURALMAP: the ground-to-embodiment relation recovers the observed neural interventions and shared lawfulness, with constraints fixed before evaluation.
- REPORTTRUE: the selected reported acquisition is accurate in its relevant content, identity and chronology; accepted reporting alone does not establish this.
- TIMELOCK: the content-acquisition episode is independently timed to the claimed interval.
- EXCLUSION: during that interval the processes necessary under the targeted dependence model are excluded, and the relevant ordinary information routes are assessed.
- BMAP: the candidate independence model specifies its bearer and process and its differential expectations for the acquisition; failure of a rival alone does not establish it.
- PROXY: human report and animal arousal markers validly track the specified phenomenal or access target to the limited degree claimed.
- DEATH: actual subjectivity occurs beyond this person's irreversible bodily-death boundary; resuscitation is insufficient.
- SAME: a declared psychological, organism or simple-subject criterion establishes numerical identity and handles interruption and branching.
- STORE: a specified record, relation, value or subjectivity actually remains available or significant in an identified bearer.
- TIMELESS: the specified foundation has no intrinsic succession while one non-discursive act grounds ordered temporal contents.
- NOW: objective changing presentness must be perfectly known and requires intrinsically successive cognition.
- CANDIDATE: a candidate agent has independently specified ends, scope and constraints, without assuming that the agent exists.
- AGENCY: an actual agent grounds or directs the relevant cosmic domain toward independently specified ends.
- EXPECT: fixed agent preferences and impersonal comparisons give different expectations for the specified order and life evidence, with observation selection handled.
- VALUE: specified experiences, relationships and interests have normative value; the claimed extension across persons needs justification.
- ENHANCE: continuing the same subjects and worthwhile goods adds value without overriding harm, under an explicit value ordering.
- NECESSITY (overlay): loss of enduring subjects or recognition defeats all significance in the claimed domain, not only further continuation.
- GOOD: the actual foundation meets an independently intelligible good-nature or agency standard; intention or power alone is insufficient.
- AUTH: the foundation's relation to agents legitimately grounds binding reasons, with commands reliably identifiable and consistent with its good nature.
- UNITYNORM (overlay): shared subjecthood supplies impartial obligations protecting distinct persons, rather than only prudential incentives.
- PERMISSION: permitted harms satisfy independently defended justification conditions addressing necessity, proportionality and alternatives.
- EVIL: the specified knowledge, power and perfect-goodness attributes hold, and apparently gratuitous suffering is defeasible evidence against adequate permission reasons.
- LOVE: perfect love entails available conscious reciprocal relationship whenever a person is capable and nonresistant, belief is necessary for it, and relevant nonresistant nonbelief occurs.
- RESTORE: the same victims actually receive integrating restorative goods; this addresses final loss without itself justifying prior permission.
- NATURALNORM: a natural interest or impartiality property has the normative identity and force claimed, rather than merely explaining approval.
- FINITE: actual finite benefits retain some value despite later loss; this leaves cosmic enduring significance unanswered.

## Routes by component

### H-F: fundamentality

<!-- id: apD-p003 -->
Two support routes and one challenge route. The gap route runs from Chalmers's distinction between functional and phenomenal explanation and his argument against the phenomenal-concept strategy, together with the psychophysical-law proposal and its non-detachability limit, through PHEN, MODAL and FUND. Its recorded limit is that even irreducibility requires the extra basic-ontology premise, and that neither one subject nor survival follows. The ground route runs from Leibniz's principle of sufficient reason and the project's conditional reconstruction of it through PSR, MENTAL and FUND; its limit is that sufficient reason alone gives neither mentality nor one subject, so MENTAL is a new bridge rather than a Leibniz attribution. Against both stands the identity challenge, resting on Papineau's defense of the phenomenal-concept strategy and his challenge to the explanatory demand on identities, through IDENTITY; it blocks an automatic gap-to-ontology inference without establishing an actual complete physical identity account. The gap and identity routes share the Chalmers and Papineau lineage; the ground route shares the explanatory-premise group. FUND is one premise used in two routes.

### H-U: universal subjecthood

<!-- id: apD-p004 -->
One support route and one challenge route. The universal-ground route requires the fundamentality component itself plus GROUND, INDIV and NEURALMAP, and rests on Kastrup's dissociation proposal and Chalmers's distinction between cosmic idealism and generic cosmopsychism; it is marked as a condition map, since at least one of its required inputs specifies the target, and its limit is that fundamentality is a prerequisite for this selected integrated route only, with numerical identity of persons and the whole never assumed. The individuation challenge requires no premise and rests on the subject, quality and structure combination problems, the uninspected status of Kastrup's illustrations, and the point that specifying a shared substrate does not derive its laws; its limit is that an unknown relation is not a proven incoherence. Both routes sit in the cross-domain common-foundation group, and the challenge also shares the Chalmers lineage.

### H-B: brain independence

<!-- id: apD-p005 -->
Four support routes and two challenge routes. The prospective route rests on the AWARE I event-recall cases and their missing shelves and neural records, the AWARE II deployment count with its unknown exposure intersection, the absence of explicit target recall, and the two-of-twenty-eight EEG intersection; the Reynolds route on the eight Reynolds reinspection claims; the dentures route on the seven dentures claims; and the Alexander route on the eight Alexander claims. Every one of the four requires the same conjunction: REPORTTRUE, TIMELOCK, EXCLUSION and BMAP. Their recorded limits are, respectively, that independent time-lock and excluded-process premises remain unmet and a missing target opportunity is not a failed trial; that the checkable perceptions lie outside the alleged standstill while the monitoring and hearing replies and original chronology remain incomplete; that one witness chain and no direct patient interview or tracing exist and public replies close neither timing nor access; and that illness severity is real in the published record while complete inactivity and content timing are not established, with a solely drug-induced dismissal equally unwarranted. The timing challenge requires no premise and rests on the Alexander, AWARE II and cross-case timing claims; it infers no unique cause and treats generic ordinary alternatives as possibilities until established for the case. The neural challenge requires PROXY and rests on the fusiform, macaque, anesthesia and aggregate intervention claims; it challenges denial of neural contribution without negating every independence model or proving identity. The four support routes draw on four dependence groups, and the map is exact about which. The prospective route draws on the AWARE study arms and versions (DEP-G2-03). The Reynolds route draws on the common case debate (DEP-G2-10). The dentures route draws on the Dutch cohort-and-documentary link together with that same case debate (DEP-G2-04 and DEP-G2-10). The Alexander route draws on the Alexander phases (DEP-G2-02). The Neal chain (DEP-G2-01) sits on none of the four; in the map's vocabulary it is no acquisition route. Chapter 6 treats Neal as attributed experience and a conditional argument, not survival, and the sensitivity tests handle the chain as a retrospective reporting chain. The timing challenge draws on the Alexander, AWARE and case-debate groups, and the neural challenge on the repeated-trials group (DEP-G2-08).

### H-S: personal survival

<!-- id: apD-p006 -->
One support route and one challenge route. The continuation route requires DEATH and SAME and rests on the Locke, Olson and Swinburne attributions, the branching and quasi-memory analyses, and the enlarged-history claim; it is a condition map, because DEATH already assumes beyond-death subjectivity, so the route states what would have to be shown rather than supplying evidence that it has been, and brain independence is not inserted as sufficient. The boundary challenge requires no premise and rests on the claims that resuscitation reports and aggregate monitoring supply neither the death boundary nor an identity bridge, and that a preserved record is not a continuing subject; its limit is that absence of evidence here is not impossibility. Both routes sit in the shared identity-and-value group.

### H-T: timelessness

<!-- id: apD-p007 -->
One support route and two challenge routes. The timeless-foundation route requires TIMELESS, GROUND and INDIV and rests on the Boethius attribution, the Stump and Kretzmann relation, the project's conditional model and the distinction between a time-indexed relation and a new intrinsic state; it is a condition map with no independent actuality inference, since grounding and finite subjectivity cannot be inferred from non-successive representation. The presence challenge requires NOW and rests on Craig's tensed-fact objection and the indexical-knowledge distinction; it targets a perfect knower under objective tense, and a B-theory or a defended non-successive access contests its premise. The physics challenge requires no premise and rests on the Einstein and Rovelli formalism claims, the bridge-assessment claims, and the phenomenology and coding claims from the DMT and Alexander material; its limit is that neither a relational formalism nor apparently stopped time proves the absence of intrinsic succession. The support and presence routes share the explanatory-premise group; the physics challenge also touches the uncertain-overlap group through the DMT comparator.

### H-P: preservation

<!-- id: apD-p008 -->
Two support routes and one challenge route. The record route requires STORE and rests on the preservation-model distinctions and the claim that preserved information need not be a continuing subject; it is a condition map, and its limit is that the records-without-subject model can meet this narrow target while the subject ceases, without proving that a cosmic store exists. The subject route requires the survival component itself and rests on the temporal-continuation and enlarged-history claims; only continuing subjectivity follows, not perfect memory, worthwhile content, intact relationships or permanent preservation. The loss challenge requires no premise and rests on the branching claim, the records-without-subject claim, and the Williams distinction between same person and appropriately related future; a preserved representation may fail the same-person or worthwhile-continuation requirements without refuting record preservation. All three routes sit in the shared identity-and-value group.

### H-I: intention

<!-- id: apD-p009 -->
One support route and one challenge route. The order route requires CANDIDATE and EXPECT and rests on the Lennox, Collins and stringency claims, the qualitative-support claim and the factory-test claim; its limit is that order and life can matter qualitatively if the expectations are justified, and that compatibility alone and uncalibrated sensitivity do not select a designer. The selection challenge requires no premise and rests on Hume's Philo and Cleanthes exchange, Weisberg's screening-off result and its matching-assumption dependence, and the distinction between a universe that contains conscious life and one intended to produce it; its limit is that the screening-off result depends on matched distributions and dismisses neither all design evidence nor all purpose. Both routes sit in the explanatory-premise and common-foundation groups, and the entire fine-tuning corpus is one shared dependency group by record.

### H-G: goodness

<!-- id: apD-p010 -->
One support route and two challenge routes. The restoration route requires VALUE, GOOD, PERMISSION, RESTORE and SAME and rests on Adams's per-participant requirement, her defeat-versus-outweighing distinction, her internal-consistency defense, the endurance-and-accountability claim and the inscrutability-symmetry claim; it is a condition map, since assumed goodness and restoration can defend consistency or address final loss without being new evidence that either exists. The suffering challenge requires EVIL and rests on Rowe's argument with his concessions and necessary conditions, Sutherland's criticism, the priced justifying goods and the symmetry claim; it applies to the stipulated power, knowledge and perfect-goodness package, and insufficient known reasons need not establish absent reasons. The hiddenness challenge requires LOVE and rests on Schellenberg's linkage of perfect love to available relationship, his scope limits, and Howard-Snyder's proposed reasons and concessions; the two authors are inspected selected positions rather than a direct historical exchange, and perfect love is added to bare goodness. All three routes sit in the shared identity-and-value group. SAME is one premise shared with the survival route; GOOD and VALUE are shared with the authority route.

### H-A: moral authority

<!-- id: apD-p011 -->
One support route and two challenge routes. The nature route requires VALUE, GOOD and AUTH and rests on Craig's grounding of value in God's nature and obligation in commands, his contingent-commands correction, and the project's two bridge claims about valenced experience and creator status; it is a condition map, and its limit is that nature and value on one side and commands and duties on the other remain distinct, with consciousness itself establishing no normative bridge. The unity challenge requires no premise and rests on the claims that shared subjecthood aligns incentives without establishing impartial obligation, that endurance enables accountability without explaining wrongness, and that creation and power do not entail authority; a defender may add UNITYNORM, but descriptive unity or sanctions alone cannot warrant it. The rival challenge requires NATURALNORM and rests on Railton's objective interests, his impartiality criterion and his separation of applicability from categorical reasons; it challenges the uniqueness of divine grounding while moral naturalism remains contested and no categorical reason for every agent is guaranteed. All three routes sit in the shared identity-and-value group.

### Cross-cutting targets

<!-- id: apD-p012 -->
Four targets sit above the components. The existence-and-lawfulness route requires the fundamentality component plus GROUND and rests on the conditional sufficient-reason reconstruction, Kastrup's proposal and the shared-world claim; basic consciousness alone is not an account of why anything exists, and the primitive ground, the laws and the grounding relation remain stopping points. The enhancement route requires the survival component plus VALUE and ENHANCE and rests on the Craig, Williams and Fischer attributions and the enhancement and necessity claims; enhancement under these conditions is distinct from the necessity overlay and from actual survival, and finite meaning may remain. The finite-value route requires VALUE and FINITE and rests on Wolf's account, her acknowledged limit and the finite-value and necessity claims; it neither establishes enduring significance nor entails that the universe has no purpose. The full-package route requires all nine components plus GROUND, NEURALMAP, VALUE, ENHANCE and PERMISSION; its recorded statement of genuine mutual support is that one constrained conscious grounding relation could explain experience and shared lawfulness, that identity-preserving continuation could make intended developmental goods enduring, and that a defensible good-nature relation could connect value and obligations, these being mutually intelligible explanatory relations and not independent observations. A shared ontology can unify without furnishing nine confirmations.

## Shared evidence and shared premises

<!-- id: apD-p013 -->
Eleven claim records serve routes in more than one component, and each is one observation or one analysis however many routes it enters. The conditional sufficient-reason reconstruction serves fundamentality and the existence target. Kastrup's proposal and the shared-world claim serve universal subjecthood and the existence target. The branching claim, the enlarged-history claim and the records-without-subject claim serve both survival and preservation. Williams's same-person-versus-appropriate-relation distinction serves preservation and the enhancement target. The valenced-experience bridge and the creator-status bridge serve authority and the full package. The endurance-and-accountability claim serves authority and goodness. And the claim that resuscitation reports establish neither survival nor fundamental mind serves both brain independence and survival. The premises are shared more widely still. GROUND is required by four routes (universal ground, timeless foundation, existence and the full package), VALUE by five (restoration, authority, enhancement, finite value and the full package), and FUND, INDIV, NEURALMAP, SAME, GOOD, PERMISSION and ENHANCE by two each; REPORTTRUE, TIMELOCK, EXCLUSION and BMAP are required together by all four crisis-case routes. When a sensitivity test removes one of these, every route that requires it loses its conclusion at once, and that is by design.

## The fourteen dependence groups

The synthesis audit fixed fourteen semantic dependence groups, and every route above carries its group identifiers. They are listed here with their members so that no reader mistakes a retelling for a replication.

- DEP-G2-01, one person with related phases: C-T04-001 and C-T04-002. One Neal reporting chain; rescue and intensive-care phases distinguished; books and interviews are retellings.
- DEP-G2-02, one person with related phases: C-T06-001 and C-T06-002. Alexander's illness and recovery share patient and narrative; family interviews and a patient-reviewed chart article add documentary roles, not new experiences.
- DEP-G2-03, study arms and versions: C-T07-001, C-T07-002, C-T07-003, S-T07-002 and S-T07-007. AWARE I is a separate study; AWARE II's prospective and community arms are one article with distinct ascertainment; the excerpt and the full paper are one publication.
- DEP-G2-04, cohort follow-up and documentary link: C-T07-004 and C-T19-001. The Dutch follow-ups reuse one cohort; the dentures anecdote's appearance in the cohort paper establishes no cohort membership and changes no denominator.
- DEP-G2-05, common literary source: C-T08-001, C-T08-002 and C-T08-003. Distinct Bede narratives share one lineage; none is a clinical control or an independent manuscript witness.
- DEP-G2-06, published reanalysis: C-T08-004 and C-T08-005. The India collection and the Japanese reanalysis are different sources whose differently prompted categories cannot be pooled.
- DEP-G2-07, possible sample and recruitment overlap: C-T08-006, C-T08-007, C-T08-008 and C-T13-001. IANDS and Liège recruitment and the DMT comparator may overlap; the Greyson instrument and sample are reused; unknown overlap is neither duplication nor independence.
- DEP-G2-08, repeated trials and data: C-T09-002, C-T09-003 and C-T09-004. Macaque trials and awake data, a shared anesthesia-and-sleep cohort, and one stimulated patient contain repeated observations.
- DEP-G2-09, one experiment with many papers: C-T09-001 with S-T09-001, S-T09-006, S-T09-007, S-T09-008 and S-T09-009. Protocol, main paper, supplement, critique and correction concern one experimental program.
- DEP-G2-10, common case debate: C-T19-001 and C-T19-002. The dentures chain returns to one nurse; the Reynolds sources reuse one investigator's account of one surgery; advocate, critic and reply multiplicity is not event multiplicity.
- DEP-G2-11, shared philosophical lineage: tasks T03, T05 and T12. The Chalmers and Papineau texts recur across the comparison, the gap debate and the mind-model packet; each distinct inference is credited once.
- DEP-G2-12, shared explanatory premises: tasks T10, T11 and T12. Sufficient reason, explanatory completeness, necessary ground and primitive stopping points recur in the existence, time and mind maps.
- DEP-G2-13, shared identity and value premises: tasks T13, T14 and T15. Identity continuity supports preservation, survival and proposed restoration; enduring value, objective worth and authority are distinct recurring bridges; restoration assumed to defend goodness is not evidence for survival.
- DEP-G2-14, cross-domain common foundation: tasks T10 through T15. A common experiential ground may unify several explananda if the grounding, individuation, intention, preservation and normative bridges work; unification may have explanatory value without multiplying independent evidence.

## Where removal changes an assessment

<!-- id: apD-p015 -->
The sensitivity tests executed at the accepted synthesis stage, restated in full in chapter 11, change component assessments as follows. The named-case removals, singly and jointly, change brain independence and survival only. The removal of all retrospective narratives changes universal subjecthood, brain independence, survival, timelessness, intention and goodness. The withdrawal of uncertain-dependence contributions changes universal subjecthood, brain independence and timelessness. The identity and preservation variations change brain independence, survival, timelessness, preservation, intention and goodness. Removing the necessity overlay changes nothing, because no route uses it; removing the unity-to-obligation overlay changes authority; removing AUTH changes goodness and authority. Removing PSR changes fundamentality and universal subjecthood: the global-explanation route is disabled, and one upstream motivation from necessary explanation to a mental foundation is lost. It does not change the intention assessment; what it removes there is a reuse, since global-explanation pressure can no longer be spent as creator support. Removing MODAL changes fundamentality and universal subjecthood; removing EXPECT changes intention and goodness. Removing the fusiform case, the macaque case or the animal proxy changes fundamentality, universal subjecthood and brain independence; weakening the human report proxy changes those three together with survival and timelessness. Restricting claims about authors to inspected originals changes all nine. In every scenario the change is a narrowing or widening of conditional warrant; no baseline label crosses a threshold, and the accepted execution records the reason in each row.

## Genuine mutual support versus reuse

<!-- id: apD-p016 -->
The map's own semantics state the test that separates the two. Common grounding can reduce separate explanatory posits only if the same constrained relation genuinely explains several targets; an ontology with one noun can still carry many independently adjustable commitments. On that test the full package offers three candidate relations of genuine mutual support, named above, and thirteen added commitments beyond the minimal thesis. The four circularity rules mark the places where mutual support would otherwise be manufactured by reuse: restoration assumed for goodness and then cited for survival; designer preferences assumed for life and then cited for the designer; the desire for endurance cited as evidence of endurance; and the phenomenal-gap premise spent three times. A reader who wants to know whether a given convergence in the proposal is real or manufactured can check it here in two steps: find the routes that converge, and ask whether their required inputs are distinct premises with distinct warrant or one premise under several names.
