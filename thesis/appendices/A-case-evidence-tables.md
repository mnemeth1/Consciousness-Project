<!-- id: apA-p001 -->
This appendix gives one evidence table for each of the twenty-three case and study records in the project's case ledger. Each table is rendered as a labeled list, because the build permits no other form, and each row answers a fixed question: what was claimed in its earliest inspected form; what the medical or documentary chronology is; how the timing of the report compares with the availability of corroboration; how independent the witnesses are; what is claimed by way of perception, foreknowledge and recovery, assessed separately; what ordinary routes of access remain open; and what is unknown. A final row classifies the record's contents under five headings: documented findings, corroborated testimony, uncorroborated testimony, interpretation, and unknown. Denominators are preserved for the prospective studies at their source units, an unknown is never replaced with zero, and nothing is summed across records. Two Phase 2 sources that chapter 6 uses, Heim's 1892 collection and Parnia's 2001 Southampton study, carry source records but no case record and are therefore not tabled here.

<!-- id: apA-p002 -->
The rules of chapter 2 govern every row. A verified report is not a verified event. A named witness reached only through the narrator is one voice in the narrator's chain, not an independent witness. A physiological interval, however long, does not establish when remembered content was formed. A possible ordinary mechanism is not a demonstrated explanation of a particular episode. Repeated publications, translations and interviews about one episode are one case however large its literature. And where a number appears without a recoverable denominator, the table says so.

## Named retrospective cases

### C-T04-001: Mary Neal, reported 1999 kayak entrapment

<!-- id: apA-p003 -->
One reported drowning and rescue episode on a Chilean river and its later reporting chain, in dependence group D-T04-Neal1999 with the intensive-care episode below. Sources: a publisher-hosted author interview of unknown date, a user-supplied purported talk transcript with 1,210 cues whose audio was never verified, and a converted 2012 ebook of the memoir with 2019 conversion metadata and no print-page equivalence; the original 2011 edition was identified but never inspected. Phase 2 reinspection accepted thirty-three claims.

- Earliest-form claim: the earliest inspected version is the converted 2012 ebook and the interview of unknown date; the 2011 first text and the dated first disclosures were not inspected, so earliest-form priority is unknown.
- Chronology: reported crisis in 1999, with sources conflicting on the date (January 14 or February 3) and no exact-day documentation inspected; publisher editions listed May 2012; a 2021 manuscript is a retelling. Rescue interval reported as eleven to fourteen minutes on a rescuer's watch in the memoir, fifteen minutes in the transcript, a thirty-minute estimate in a 2013 news report, and thirty minutes in a compiled accident database whose injury field says near-drowning.
- Report timing versus corroboration: every inspected account postdates the episode by years; the memoir names four rescuers and reports interviewing each, but no separately authored witness statement, interview transcript or information-exposure history was inspected.
- Witness independence: one narrator; witness thoughts reach the record only through her.
- Perceptions: an apparent external vantage on the rescue, calm surrender, intermittent returns to breathe, and seeming simultaneity alongside narrated sequence; attributed experience, external accuracy not established. Foreknowledge: assessed under the intensive-care record. Recovery: survival with reported testing and no brain damage is a narrated outcome, not a chart diagnosis; later saturation figures concern hospital care.
- Ordinary-access alternatives: not measured for this case; the rescue intervals measure neither circulation nor oxygen delivery nor neural activity.
- Unknowns: original memoir and earliest narrative; independent witnesses and exposure history; medical records and timestamped measurements; prognosis and interventions; primary critic full text; audio alignment of the transcript.
- Classification: documented findings, the publication dates and the son's death date from a dated newspaper; corroborated testimony, none independent of the narrator; uncorroborated testimony, the rescue vantage, the intervals and the recovery; interpretation, survival and brain independence; unknown, everything in the previous row.

### C-T04-002: Mary Neal, reported intensive-care episode and recovery

<!-- id: apA-p004 -->
A reported spiritual encounter during intensive-care recovery after the river episode, a distinct episode with the same narrator and an overlapping reporting chain; the two are never summed. Source: the purported talk transcript only. Phase 2 reinspection accepted sixteen claims.

- Earliest-form claim: the transcript passages at the recorded cue ranges; no earlier dated account inspected.
- Chronology: first weeks after the rescue, as reported; subsequent months of pain, operations, rehabilitation and retrospective investigation, dates unverified; the medication word in the transcript is garbled.
- Report timing versus corroboration: the foreknowledge claim, that the narrator learned during the crisis that her oldest son would die, was disclosed in every inspected source after his death in a roller-ski collision on June 21, 2009; the complete dated news article discloses no earlier prediction, which is a bounded content check; the memoir's own draft labels conflict (a spring 2009 first draft revised and saved June 21, against a Q&A calling the hours-before-death manuscript the first complete draft), and the transcript does not explicitly state same-day completion.
- Witness independence: one narrator; early confidants are reported by her.
- Perceptions: the intensive-care encounter, attributed. Foreknowledge: an attributed prediction with documented reporting chronology; no independently dated prefulfillment record, no fixed deadline, cause or scoring set, and no all-prediction denominator; the memoir also attributes to the son a childhood belief that he would not reach eighteen and then narrates his life past eighteen. Recovery: reported, undocumented.
- Ordinary-access alternatives: retrospective assertion is consistent with ordinary memory and retelling; no case-specific mechanism is demonstrated either.
- Unknowns: independent intensive-care dates and charts; any dated prediction record; audio.
- Classification: documented findings, the death date; corroborated testimony, none; uncorroborated testimony, the encounter, the prediction and its timing; interpretation, foreknowledge; unknown, the denominators.

### C-T06-001: Eben Alexander, 2008 meningitis and reported experience

<!-- id: apA-p005 -->
A single 2008 illness and hospitalization with reported onset November 10, 2008; recalled spiritual phases and the later photograph recognition all belong to one reporting chain, in the Alexander dependence group with the recovery episode below. Sources: a purported talk transcript with unverified audio; a converted 2012 ebook with 2023 conversion metadata disclosing changed names and editorial assistance; a 2018 external clinical review; a 2013 magazine article; a defender's long reply; the author's current webpage; a 2011 research report page updated in 2014; a published skeptical criticism; and a treating physician's separately authored signed statement. Phase 2 reinspection accepted thirty-three claims.

- Earliest-form claim: the earliest dated text inspected is the 2012 memoir; the 2012 AANS account was identified but inaccessible; the reported six-week, more-than-20,000-word account written before reading the literature rests on later statements, with no dated manuscript inspected.
- Chronology: acute Escherichia coli meningitis with prompt antibiotics, coma with ventilation, and awakening reported on day 7 by memoir, physician statement and current page, against the 2018 review's extubation on day 6 and improvement on day 7; admission Glasgow Coma Scale 11 in the review against 8 in the memoir and a defender's account; laboratory timing differs across tellings; no electroencephalogram was recorded.
- Report timing versus corroboration: the 2018 review reports disclosure on day 9 and retrospective corroboration of praying visitors reportedly present on day 5, inferring an experience interval within days 1 to 5, with no original interview dates and no validated exclusion of ordinary acquisition; the memoir identifies its temporal anchors as one absent-person report and six recalled faces and calls other simultaneity conjectural.
- Witness independence: the memoir states that the author reconstructed the week's ordinary events from others because he remembers none of them; the physician's statement is separately authored but is not a chart; the 2018 reviewers report a review of records this project has not inspected; the photograph corroboration is a defender's 2013 retrospective, unblinded, family-circle interview.
- Perceptions: the reported phases and the six faces, attributed. Foreknowledge or recognition: the birth sister's photograph, received four months after discharge and recognized the following morning during a reading of an analogous case, with the author already aware before the coma that a birth sister had died in 2000 and having reunited with birth relatives in 2007; no blinded comparison, no dated pre-photograph description. Recovery: remarkable recovery attested by the physician; mortality figures given without denominators or a calculation model; recovery is not a chart diagnosis of prior cortical destruction.
- Ordinary-access alternatives: the attending physician, as reported in 2013, described chemical sedation and delirious responsiveness; the 2018 reviewers leave the relative contributions of infection and sedation undetermined while arguing against a solely drug-induced account; the memoir's own appendix says full-thickness cortical destruction is unnecessary to its argument.
- Unknowns: original charts and interview recordings; experience-to-measurement synchronization; any independent dated pre-photograph description; the 2012 account; audio alignment.
- Classification: documented findings, the diagnosis, treatment and awakening as attested and reviewed, and the absence of an EEG; corroborated testimony, the praying visitors on day 5 as retrospectively corroborated by the review; uncorroborated testimony, the experience contents, the timing of the recognition and the prior written account; interpretation, cortical incapacity during the experience, survival, a creator; unknown, the interval, the exclusion and the records.

### C-T19-001: the dentures case, patient B and nurse TG

<!-- id: apA-p007 -->
One reported resuscitation at the old Canisius Hospital near Nijmegen, attributed to late 1979 with the date unverified, whose entire substantive content reaches the record through nurse TG; the anecdote's appearance in the Dutch cohort paper is a documentary link and not cohort membership. Sources: Smit's 2008 article, a 2008 companion interview summary, and the defenders' 2010 rejoinder, with the 1991 nursing-journal article and the 1994 interview transcript identified but uninspected. Phase 2 reinspection accepted twenty-seven claims.

- Earliest-form claim: the 1991 article is the earliest known publication and was not inspected; the earliest inspected form is the 1994 interview as excerpted in translation and the 2008 article.
- Chronology: the event probably in 1979 by inference; TG's retelling more than a week after the resuscitation; the 1991 article; the interview of February 2, 1994 with its transcript dated February 3; the 2008 article and the follow-up interview, published as a nine-page summary of a record exceeding thirty pages; the 2008 critic-and-reply set; the 2010 rejoinder, which states that the official medical records were destroyed.
- Report timing versus corroboration: every patient detail is retrospective through TG; the intermediary who carried the story to the 1991 author did not participate in the resuscitation; no investigator ever interviewed the patient.
- Witness independence: one principal witness across repeated interviews; the patient's account exists only as reported by the nurse.
- Perceptions: the patient reportedly later identified the nurse who removed his dentures and described an unusual cart with a flat wooden pull-out shelf already extended, a description that specifically challenges a metal-drawer hearing explanation, while the Dutch interview article itself contains a colloquial drawer word; the published interview's pump-first passage was corrected by TG in her 2008 reply to denture removal before the compression device was switched on, with an estimate of more than five minutes of transport without cardiac massage that does not exclude earlier bystander or ambulance resuscitation. Foreknowledge: none claimed. Recovery: the patient survived; no clinical detail inspected.
- Ordinary-access alternatives: hypothermia, resuscitation-restored awareness, sensation, hearing and later reconstruction proposed by the critic, who corrected his own earlier assumptions; the defenders' proposed ten-participant blindfold experiment was never performed; no mechanism demonstrated for the event.
- Unknowns: patient interview; raw charts, tracings or independent dated perception or witness statements; the patient's full information access; the 1991 and 1994 documents and the Dutch 2008 originals; the remaining pages of the 2010 Dutch response.
- Classification: documented findings, the publication chain and its dates; corroborated testimony, none beyond the single witness; uncorroborated testimony, the identification, the cart description and the corrected sequence; interpretation, extracerebral perception or ordinary hearing; unknown, timing, records and access.

### C-T19-002: Pam Reynolds, 1991 surgical report

- Chronology: surgery in August 1991 with the day unverified; an interview several days later according to a defender; the 1994 interview; the 1998 book; the 2002 and 2011 debate; the rejoinder and the letter thereafter.

## Prospective studies

### C-T07-001: AWARE I

<!-- id: apA-p009 -->
A prospective multi-center study at fifteen hospitals in the United States, the United Kingdom and Austria, main enrollment July 2008 to December 2012, with a 2007 pilot excluded from the main sample. Source: the 2014 article, whose final flow figure was inspected in Phase 2. Phase 2 reinspection accepted five claims.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 2,060 recorded arrest events; 330 discharge survivors, with the event-to-person conversion unexplained; 140 stage-one interview completers, against printed counts of 52 in-hospital plus 90 post-discharge interviews and a table denominator of 142, an unresolved discrepancy that AWARE II's introduction compounds by citing 143; 101 stage-two completers, 39 non-completers; 55 with a positive memory response at stage one and 85 without; at stage two, 46 with no memory, 46 with non-NDE memories, 9 at the Greyson threshold including 2 with auditory or visual event recall; 1 of the 2 followed up, the other in failing health; interview timing 3 days to 4 weeks in hospital and 3 months to 1 year by telephone.
- Findings: both event-recall cases occurred at locations without installed target shelves; the authors report medical-record corroboration for one case and infer approximately three minutes of awareness from a defibrillator sequence, with awareness up to three minutes and arrest lasting at least three minutes being different quantities; no participant-linked neural recording accompanies any reported content; the main study abandoned programmed auditory cues after the pilot.
- Report timing versus corroboration: recollection at interview, days to a year after the event; corroboration from records for one case as reported by the authors.
- Unknowns: separate eligibility and approach counts; the exposed-and-interviewed intersection; the 140 versus 142 discrepancy; no pooled rate.
- Classification: documented findings, the flow counts and the shelf locations; corroborated testimony, the one record-corroborated recall as the authors report it; uncorroborated testimony, the remaining memories; interpretation, the three-minute inference; unknown, the intersections.

### C-T07-002: AWARE II, prospective arm

<!-- id: apA-p010 -->
A feasibility phase from April 2013 to May 2015, a five-site pilot to May 2017, and a twenty-five-site phase to March 2020, with consecutive recruitment during weekday daytime hours. Sources: the 2023 article and its excerpt, the Greyson and van Lommel letter with Parnia's reply, and the Martial letter with the authors' direct reply. Phase 2 reinspection accepted eighteen claims.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 567 recruited; 354 without sustained return of circulation and 213 with it; 160 hospital deaths after return; 53 discharge survivors, 44 with favorable outcome, 8 with severe disability, 1 vegetative; 28 interviewed and 25 not (4 refused, 7 disabled, 3 other, 11 unreported); 11 with any memory or perception, in overlapping categories, and 17 with none; 6 transcendent experiences at the Greyson threshold, with median scale score 7 and maximum 18; 2 with recollections of emergence during resuscitation and 2 after, 3 dreamlike; equipment deployed in 365 of 567, which is not verified stimulus exposure; EEG captured from 85 subjects and interpretable in 53, of whom 49 also had cerebral oximetry; 851 EEG images captured and 466 interpretable; exactly 2 interviewees with EEG data, neither among those with explicit recall.
- Findings: no explicit recall of any image or auditory target among the 28 and no implicit visual identification; one correct three-fruit choice, for which the any-three-fruits request supplies no uniform chance model; one separate participant's overt recollection of clinical surroundings received chart verification of a shock event, with unmeasured prior resuscitation knowledge acknowledged; near-normal or physiological EEG rhythms reported up to 35 to 60 minutes into resuscitation on a limited frontotemporal montage during pulse-check pauses, with alpha-coma interpretation not excluded and suppression in 47 percent of EEG data, a data-unit figure and not a proportion of people.
- Report timing versus corroboration: recollection at interview after discharge; aggregate rhythm timing cannot date any particular memory's formation; the published exchanges supply argument, not the missing intersection.
- Unknowns: the supplement, its figures and its narratives; confirmed target exposure among interviewees; any participant, content and time linkage to a recorded absence of processing; exact category intersections and repeat-event handling; between-arm overlap.
- Classification: documented findings, the flow counts, the deployment count and the EEG data-unit findings; corroborated testimony, the chart-verified shock recollection as reported; uncorroborated testimony, the remaining recollections; interpretation, the disinhibition account in the authors' reply; unknown, every opportunity intersection.

### C-T07-004: the Dutch prospective cohort

<!-- id: apA-p012 -->
Ten Dutch hospitals from 1988 to 1992, four months to nearly four years per site, consecutive successfully resuscitated coronary-care patients. Sources: the 2001 article in its final published layout, and the 2002 correction, which changes one drug name in one sentence. Phase 2 reinspection accepted four claims.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 344 people and 509 resuscitation episodes, different units; 62 with a broad-definition experience (18 percent) and 41 core (12 percent), 282 without; 248 interviewed within five days printed as 74 percent, a figure whose denominator is unclear and is not manufactured; at two years, 37 initial experiencers (62 minus 19 deaths and 6 refusals) and 37 matched controls selected from 75 contacted, reclassified to 35 and 39; at eight years, 23 and 15; the same retained people throughout, not new cohorts.
- Findings: the correction alters no cohort count or follow-up result; site selection is acknowledged as possible; arrest timing is estimated; no participant-specific EEG accompanies any reported experience; the dentures anecdote printed in the article is contextually linked and never added to the denominator.
- Report timing versus corroboration: interview within days for most, by the authors' printed figure; longitudinal reclassification thereafter.
- Unknowns: initial arrest count; a separate pre-inclusion count; the 74 percent denominator.
- Classification: documented findings, the cohort counts and the correction; uncorroborated testimony, the experience reports; interpretation, the broad and core classifications; unknown, the initial-arrest denominator.

## Cross-cultural and retrospective collections

### C-T08-004: the North Indian incidental collection, 1986

<!-- id: apA-p014 -->
Sixteen included case reports from eighteen incidentally identified during reincarnation investigations, four narrated in detail as illustrations and not as additional cases, with a median investigation delay of about fourteen years. Source: the 1986 report. No Phase 2 reinspection.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 18 identified, 2 excluded as scant, 16 included; 10 direct subject interviews, 5 firsthand-informant cases without the subject, 1 secondhand; a subset of 4 with both subject and another informant; 11 male, 5 female; age known for 14; American comparison rows of 78 and, for two rows only, 106; no population or invitation frame.
- Findings: messengers in 12 of 16 and mistaken-identity return in 10 of 16 as selected-case descriptions; the authors argue that cultural variation proves neither belief-only production nor an afterlife.
- Report timing versus corroboration: long delays; no synchronization with neurological measurement.
- Witness independence: subjects and informants as recorded; elicitation unequal between the Indian and American material, with comparison-table blanks meaning no instance learned and possibly never asked.
- Unknowns: Hindi transcripts and a bilingual translation audit; the American source cohorts, not reopened; any prevalence.
- Classification: documented findings, the counts and the elicitation method; uncorroborated testimony, the reports; interpretation, the cultural explanations; unknown, prevalence.

- Unknowns: the person-episode crosswalk, the invitation denominator, the class frequencies.

### C-T08-007: the non-life-threatening and coma comparison, 2014

<!-- id: apA-p017 -->
Voluntary recruitment through IANDS France and Flanders and Liège web and media channels; 190 scale-selected reports divided into 50 from non-life-threatening events and 140 from self-reported coma, with historical prospective comparisons that reuse earlier data. Source: the 2014 paper. Phase 2 reinspection accepted eight claims.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 215 submitted, 25 excluded for scores below 7, 190 included; 50 and 140 by group, subgroups not extracted; 42 historical prospective reports pooled as documented analytic reuse (27 from a 2003 study, 11 from a 2002 study, 4 from Parnia 2001), and a broader figure pooling 429 retrospective and 47 prospective reports from published datasets with unknown participant overlap; mean reporting delay 24 plus or minus 15 years; no invitation or all-survivor denominator.
- Findings: no significant intensity or item-frequency differences between groups, which is not an equivalence result; cutoff wording internally inconsistent; clinical statuses medically uncontrolled self-report; a legend-year mismatch labeling the current study 2013 establishes no separate sample; its relabeling of Parnia's two bodily-awareness responses as out-of-body experiences is a scale-category use the original prose contradicts, qualified without altering any published number.
- Witness independence: volunteers; overlap with the 1990 cohort is explicit analytic reuse in one figure, with no claim that the 190 new reports share participants.
- Unknowns: original clinical records, uniform translations and individual data; a prospective matched non-crisis cohort.
- Classification: documented findings, the counts and the reuse; uncorroborated testimony, the reports and their clinical statuses; interpretation, the equivalence reading, which is withheld; unknown, overlap.

### C-T08-008: the self-described experiencer and denier comparison, 1990

<!-- id: apA-p018 -->
Two hundred forty-six newsletter respondents to a questionnaire invitation about close brushes with death, 183 describing themselves as experiencers and 63 denying an experience, groups that are not identical to scale-defined categories; the time item was broadened. Source: the 1990 paper. No Phase 2 reinspection.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 246 respondents; 183 self-described experiencers, of whom 169 scored at least 7 and 14 below; 63 self-described non-experiencers, of whom 18 scored at least 7 and 45 below; mean delay 18.1 years for the experiencer label and 22.3 for the other; all invited and any random clinical population unknown.
- Findings: the scale and the self-label pull apart in both directions; the broadened time item groups apparent simultaneity, stopped time and time losing meaning into one response category, so a high score is not a count of one uniquely defined timeless experience.
- Witness independence: newsletter recruitment; possible overlap with other collections; the cohort is reused in the 2014 comparison's figure.
- Unknowns: any random all-survivor sampling; validated absence of experience among deniers.
- Classification: documented findings, the counts and the item wording; uncorroborated testimony, the self-labels and responses; interpretation, any temporal ontology read from the item; unknown, overlap.

### C-T09-002: the macaque central-thalamic stimulation study

<!-- id: apA-p020 -->
Two rhesus macaques receiving deep brain stimulation under maintained propofol anesthesia, with three separate awake comparators and reused awake auditory data, out of five animals in total. Sources: the 2022 article and the Phase 2 supplementary methods. No Phase 2 case reinspection; two supplementary claims are recorded under the neural packet.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 5 male rhesus in total; 2 stimulated; 3 awake comparators; independent session counts unknown, since per-run volume counts and overlapping analysis windows are not run totals by animal and condition.
- Findings: high central-thalamic stimulation raised the behavioral arousal score from 0 of 11 to 9 of 11 in both animals, low stimulation reached 3 of 11, ventrolateral control remained 0 of 11, and awake animals score 11 of 11; fMRI dynamics shifted and hierarchical auditory responses were enhanced; each animal's high setting was selected empirically just above its behavioral threshold, limiting any dose-response reading.
- Report timing versus corroboration: not applicable; awareness is inferred from signatures, since macaques give no trialwise reports.
- Unknowns: supplement run and exclusion details; blinding and randomized condition order, not established in the inspected text; the auditory paradigm has no ventrolateral control arm.
- Classification: documented findings, the arousal scores and imaging results at their units; interpretation, awareness from arousal markers; unknown, the run totals.

### C-T09-003: the shared-cohort anesthesia and sleep study

<!-- id: apA-p021 -->
Forty healthy young men recruited; 39 anesthetized under randomized open-label propofol (19) or dexmedetomidine (20); 37 returned for a sleep arm on average eighteen weeks later; overlapping arms, not 76 participants. Source: the 2021 article. No Phase 2 case reinspection; two Phase 2 claims record the appendix search.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 40 recruited; 39 anesthesia; 19 and 20 by drug; 37 sleep returners; experience-report integers unknown, with subjective experience reported in 80 percent of anesthesia interviews and 71 percent of sleep interviews including contentless impressions, and a targeted search of the extended-data appendices finding regional PET tables rather than interview-count tables.
- Findings: PET contrasts locate state-linked thalamic, cingulate and angular blood-flow changes as associations within manipulated states; the authors note that absent response or absent recalled content cannot conclusively establish absent experience; three awareness-report states were excluded from the comparison.
- Report timing versus corroboration: immediate interviews where possible; none in deep unarousable states.
- Unknowns: exact interview denominators and raw reports.
- Classification: documented findings, the cohort counts and PET associations; uncorroborated testimony, the experience reports; interpretation, the disconnection reading; unknown, the integers.

### C-T09-004: the single-patient fusiform stimulation case

<!-- id: apA-p022 -->
One 45-year-old epilepsy patient in whom electrical stimulation of a right fusiform face-selective region pair elicited reported face distortions on seven real-face trials, while four sham trials and three trials stimulating a nearby control pair did not. Sources: the 2012 study and its 2013 correction, which changes only an author name. No Phase 2 reinspection.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 1 participant, aged 45; 7 real-face trials, 4 sham, 3 control-pair; famous-face and place naming tests present but not extracted as counts.
- Findings: the sham and control contrasts support a causal contribution within this case; nonspecific nonface changes also occurred; the two stimulated regions were not separated; current can affect connected sites; the small naming tests differed procedurally and their null differences support no equivalence; randomization and assessor masking not established; one patient's repeated trials are not independent people; no unitary face center follows.
- Report timing versus corroboration: reports at the time of stimulation; the video was not independently viewed.
- Unknowns: as listed; the local-versus-network question.
- Classification: documented findings, the trial outcomes as reported; interpretation, causal contribution to reported perceptual content; unknown, masking.

### C-T13-001: the DMT and near-death-scale comparison, 2018

<!-- id: apA-p023 -->
Thirteen healthy psychedelic-experienced volunteers measured after placebo and after DMT in a fixed placebo-first order with single blinding and heterogeneous doses, and a separate thirteen-person retrospectively recruited comparator selected as above the standard threshold; repeated conditions in thirteen people, not twenty-six, in the uncertain-overlap dependence group with the IANDS collections. Source: the 2018 article in full HTML without supplement or participant-level data. No Phase 2 reinspection.

- Denominators, preserved at source units: 13 experimental people, 2 conditions each; 13 at or above the threshold of 7 after DMT and 1 after placebo; 13 comparator people, selected above threshold with no raw boundary-score list; dose counts as recorded, 7 mg for 3 people, 14 mg for 4, 18 mg for 1 and 20 mg for 5; time-item response-category counts not supplied; eligible or approached totals unknown.
- Findings: time-item means 1.15 (standard deviation 0.80) under DMT and 1.54 (0.78) in the comparator, with non-significance not an equivalence test; published comparator sex counts (7 women, 6 men) differ from the experimental group's (6 women, 7 men) despite described matching.
- Report timing versus corroboration: immediate scale completion in the experiment; retrospective in the comparator, with delay unspecified.
- Witness independence: comparator overlap with the 2014 comparison or other Liège and IANDS studies unestablished; sample sizes are not to be added.
- Unknowns: participant-level data; separately reported EEG; exact pair matching.
- Classification: documented findings, the scale results at their units; uncorroborated testimony, the comparator's recollections; interpretation, endogenous-DMT causation or altered access, both withheld; unknown, overlap.

Read together, the twenty-three tables show why chapter 6 reached the verdict it did and why chapter 11's first three sensitivity tests behave as they do. Not one named case supplies documented findings for the conjunction that discrimination requires. The prospective studies supply documented flow counts and one chart-verified recollection but no confirmed opportunity denominator, and the AWARE II EEG units are documented findings about rhythms during resuscitation that are linked to no participant's report. The historical and cultural records supply dated recurrence under heavy literary and elicitation dependence. The controlled studies supply documented counts, arousal scores, imaging associations and trial outcomes at their units. The readings that turn those into awareness, disconnection or causal contribution to experience sit in the interpretation cell of each table, where the audit put them, and this appendix does not promote them. The classification rows are the audit's product, not the thesis's conclusion, and a reader who disagrees with a classification can find the record it rests on. Nothing in this appendix upgrades a report to an event, and nothing in it upgrades an interpretation to a finding.
