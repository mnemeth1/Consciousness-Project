# T05 — Physical description and phenomenal experience

Author submission, 2026-09-12; protocol_v1; independent R05 review required. This pilot assesses one developed exchange, not the whole consciousness literature. Exact provenance is in input_verification.json and submission_manifest.json. All substantive assessments below are proposals.

**Conclusion:** The inspected exchange supports a serious dispute about explanatory standards and epistemic access. It does not settle whether a complete physical base metaphysically necessitates experience. The inference from an epistemic gap to ontological irreducibility requires contested premises; moving onward to H-F needs an additional grounding argument. Neither conclusion supplies personal survival or theism.

## What the question means

Use P for a stipulated complete nonexperiential physical account, including relevant laws, totality and context conditions, and Q for a phenomenal truth. A future scientific theory is not assumed already complete. This stipulated P prevents an argument about today's missing neural details from masquerading as an argument about every possible physical account. Conversely, putting experience into P by definition changes the comparison with T03-PH.

| Achievement | What it establishes if successful | What remains a separate question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correlation | A measured neural variable covaries with an experiential report under stated conditions. | Whether reporting tracks experience adequately; causation; necessary realization; identity. |
| Intervention and prediction | A specified manipulation changes an outcome, or a model predicts withheld experiential reports. | Whether its success identifies consciousness with the model's physical variable. |
| Identity | The phenomenal property and the proposed physical property are one property. | Evidence for that identity; whether its discovery provides the desired explanation. An asserted identity is not an observed correlation. |
| Explanatory derivation | Given premises and bridge rules, Q follows and relevant contrasts become intelligible. | Whether experiential assumptions were smuggled into those rules; whether this standard requires a priori accessibility. |
| Metaphysical entailment | No possible P-duplicate differs regarding Q. | Whether human reasoning can derive Q, and whether the entailment gives explanatory understanding. |

These are project distinctions, not new empirical findings. A successful prediction can favor several metaphysical models sharing the same observable commitments. No specific neural identity, intervention result or current neuroscientific theory was tested here.

## Direct texts and argument maps

### A-T05-001: gap and nonreductive alternative

Chalmers argues that explaining functions leaves experience unaccounted for; conceptual separability motivates failure of purely physical derivation. He proposes basic experience plus psychophysical laws, while acknowledging that treating experience as basic does not explain its existence. His discussion explicitly recognizes the rival appeal to necessary a posteriori identities. [S-T03-001, §§3,5–6 and §6 technical note](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html).

Project map: functional completeness + an unclosed phenomenal explanatory target → an explanatory demand, not automatically a new ontology. A law connecting organization with experience could explain instances conditionally; its basic mapping remains a commitment needing justification. Calling that commitment “fundamental” does not demonstrate its necessity, cosmic scope, or superiority.

### A-T05-002: knowledge, conceivability and the bridge

Chalmers develops the knowledge/conceivability case and a dilemma: conceptual features C either resist physical explanation or fail to explain epistemic situation E, including truth and justification. Both variables are topic-neutral. [S-T05-003, PDF pp2–5,9–17,23–30,32–35](https://consc.net/papers/pceg.pdf).

Conditional map: ideal conceivability of P∧¬Q + B-T05-M → possible P∧¬Q → rejection of physicalism. Mary's apparent epistemic gain additionally requires B-T05-K. These bridges are below. Neither thought experiment is an observed event.

### A-T05-003: direct physicalist defense and answer to the dilemma

Papineau's revised account uses sensory templates and associated information, permitting derived concepts without current re-experiencing. Mary acquires a new way to think of an existing property. A physical concept need not activate what it refers to. Replying directly to Chalmers, he distinguishes phenomenal and physical descriptions of concepts, rejects an explanatory regress, and permits analogous non-indexical knowledge in hypothetical silicon subjects. [S-T05-004, printed pp124–127,130–142](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/perc_and_phen_concepts.pdf).

Project assessment: this is a real explanatory proposal, not just an appeal to future science. Its defensive conclusion is compatibility of conceptual difference with property identity. Compatibility alone does not establish the proposed identity, the truth of its cognitive assumptions, or a complete physical explanation. The central cost is defending the adequacy of the proposed knowledge relation without assuming precisely the identity under dispute.

### A-T05-004: identity and the demand for explanation

Papineau separately argues that identities themselves need no further explanation and that a priori derivation is unnecessary for evidencing them. He treats the felt gap as resistance to identity; he leaves its psychological causes for empirical investigation. His argument invokes causal closure and mental causation, and his neural examples are illustrative. [S-T05-005, printed pp5–10,13–19](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/explanatorygappdf.pdf).

Project assessment: this response challenges what counts as an unanswered question. It does not allow an identity to be stipulated as scientific evidence. A further question about why an identity holds may be misplaced once that identity is warranted, while a question about whether the identification captures the intended target can remain legitimate. This packet does not independently validate causal closure or the exclusion of systematic overdetermination. A closure argument also needs those premises and a defensible causal role for experience; correlation alone does not supply them.

## The strongest reply and where the exchange remains open

The Chalmers dilemma is the counterargument to A-T05-003, not a survey's attribution. Papineau's reply is directly available in the inspected chapter, and Chalmers's text directly addresses it. The shared point of dispute is whether the physical account captures E itself or only something behaviorally and inferentially similar. A-T05-003 therefore cannot be dismissed simply because it explains cognition, but it cannot be pronounced successful merely because its hypothetical system issues the right judgments.

| Disputed bridge | Objection class | What would change the assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B-T05-M: ideal conceivability → relevant metaphysical possibility | Modal premise truth | A defensible bridge that survives necessary-a-posteriori counterexamples, or a demonstration that the specified scenario is incoherent on ideal reflection. Ordinary imaginative ease is insufficient. |
| B-T05-K: new epistemic access → ontologically new fact | Semantic/epistemic premise truth | Specify the individuation of facts, concepts and knowledge, then show why a second route to the same property can or cannot explain the gain. |
| B-T05-E: physical explanation must make the high-level truth transparently derivable | Explanatory scope | Defend one standard consistently across physical and experiential cases. Causal explanation, constitutive explanation and acquiring first-person acquaintance must not be silently interchanged. |
| B-T05-C: the cognitive account explains the relevant truth-involving epistemic access | Explanatory adequacy | A worked account of reference and justification that preserves what matters about first-person knowledge without an unsupported phenomenal primitive. Empirical support for its cognitive mechanisms would help, but would not alone resolve the modal dispute. |
| T01-B01: explanatory/conceptual IR → ontological IR → H-F | Grounding premise truth | First exclude the relevant physical identity/grounding alternative; then show why the remaining experiential reality is basic rather than dependent on a different irreducible base. |

The challenge to the physicalist is strongest when it demands an account of the actual epistemic achievement, not a duplicate report. The challenge to the antiphysicalist is strongest when it asks why conceptual independence establishes ontological independence. An alleged solution that achieves only self-report needs qualification; an alleged refutation that merely restates an unexplained feeling also needs qualification.

This pilot does not decide between these standards by verbal stipulation. On either side, an explanation of why people believe a metaphysical thesis is distinct from a justification of the thesis. The existence of sincere introspective conviction is not automatically truth-tracking; conversely, a causal account of that conviction is not automatically a defeater of its content. Those links require argument.

These arguments form one interdependent philosophical exchange: two works by each of two authors, including same-volume engagement. They are not four independent observations favoring a hypothesis. Rejecting B-T05-M and B-T05-K leaves an explanatory question but removes this packet's route to ontological IR. Removing Papineau's cognitive account does not prove dualism; removing the gap argument does not prove physicalism. Even granting H-F, a subject could cease when its lawful realizing conditions cease; no inspected argument establishes continued personal subjectivity, a universal subject, timelessness, intentional creation or moral authority.
