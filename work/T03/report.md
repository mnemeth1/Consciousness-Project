## Common criteria and model declaration

Every comparison must state its ontology, dependence relation, bearer, laws, identity rule and explanatory stopping point. A model cannot gain support by silently adding a new attribute after an observation. The same criteria apply to the central conscious-foundation proposal and alternatives:

| Criterion | Common question and recording rule |
| --- | --- |
| C1 Coherence | Can the proposed properties coexist, including changing personal experiences and any timeless ground? Distinguish an unfilled mechanism from a contradiction. |
| C2 Explanatory scope | Separate existence/physical law (T01 X-EX), subjectivity, neural dependence, survival, time, significance, purpose and moral obligation. Record what is derived, assumed or left open. |
| C3 Independent warrant and cost | List primitive properties, laws, subjects and bridge premises. Count independently adjustable commitments, rather than merely counting nouns or praising one substance. |
| C4 Evidential fit | Use the same reviewed observations and access limits. A reported experience is not its interpretation; a possible mechanism is not an established case explanation. |
| C5 Discrimination | State a prediction before evaluation, how alternatives differ, and what would weaken the model. Distinguish successful prediction from retrospective accommodation. |
| C6 Remaining problems | Preserve the strongest objection, reply and residual cost; compare explanatory stopping points symmetrically. |

These are a common assessment rubric, not six numerical scores. A broad metaphysical family may offer no distinctive empirical prediction until supplemented. An unconstrained model receives no predictive credit merely because it can accommodate everything. Empirical adequacy, conceptual explanation and normative justification remain different achievements.

## Motivations, challenges, replies and discriminators

The following conditional arguments are authored for comparison (A-T03-001–006); their premises await assessment. They do not count as six independent observations.

**Physicalism (A-T03-001).** Motivation: if physical realization can explain subjectivity and its relation to cognition, one explanatory framework could connect experience with embodied capacities without extra ontological laws. The relevant bridge is that identity or complete grounding can hold despite different first-person and third-person concepts. **Objection, explanatory adequacy:** a structural/functional account may leave phenomenal character unexplained. **Reply:** a gap between concepts or modes of access need not establish two kinds of reality; an identity can be discovered rather than transparent from definitions. **Remaining burden:** provide a defensible identity/grounding account and explain why this organization has this subjectivity. Merely invoking eventual scientific progress is insufficient. A successful, independently tested account of experiential differences would help; verified experience under conditions the specified realization model excludes would weaken that model, with timing and substrate exclusions explicitly established.

**Psychophysical laws (A-T03-002).** Motivation: if phenomenal facts need additional explanatory resources, general laws could connect them to physical organization without making every connection a separate exception. **Objection, explanatory economy:** a mapping may redescribe the dependence while leaving why these experiences accompany these structures brute. **Reply:** basic laws can explain particular instances without explaining why every primitive exists; that stopping rule must be applied to physical and conscious primitives alike. **Remaining burden:** show the laws constrain possibilities, determine subject boundaries and earn their complexity through independent success. Under PL1, neural dependence is expected, not counterevidence by definition. Prespecified cross-system mappings could distinguish law variants; an arbitrary mapping adjusted for each case cannot. These project arguments should not be treated as an additional paraphrase or endorsement of the literature anchor.

**Panpsychism (A-T03-003).** Motivation: starting with experiential constituents could avoid deriving experiential reality wholly from a nonexperiential base. **Objection, constitution:** many private micro-perspectives do not evidently constitute one unified macro-perspective; simply summing them specifies no subject. **Reply:** posit a constitutive relation with clear conditions for a unified subject, or test PA3's whole-to-local approach. **Remaining burden:** the new relation cannot just rename unity. PA3 exchanges a combination task for explaining private differentiation; PA2 must explain how proto-experience suffices. A precise rule that handles both integration and separation without arbitrary boundaries would improve comparison. Ubiquitous elementary experience by itself predicts neither human survival nor a loving cosmic agent. Access to elementary subjectivity remains an evidential limitation, not evidence against it.

**Dualism (A-T03-004).** Motivation: if unified subjectivity and first-person agency resist physical constitution, distinct subjects provide a direct bearer of those features. **Objection, causal fit:** reciprocal interaction must specify how mental influence fits a sufficiently complete physical account; merely calling the subject nonphysical does no explanatory work. **Reply:** the completeness of physical causation is a premise to assess, and explicit interaction laws could constrain any departure. DU2 avoids reciprocal intervention but must explain the reliable relation between experience and physical reports, including their epistemic significance. **Remaining burden:** neither causal closure nor its violation has been established here. Independently corroborated information acquisition under a model's explicitly excluded physical conditions could matter; unexplained testimony alone does not identify a mental substance. Persistence after bodily destruction requires a continued subject and an identity rule, not just separability in thought.

**Idealism (A-T03-005).** Motivation: if experience is our unavoidable starting datum, grounding the public world in conscious reality might explain both in one ontology. **Objection, explanatory scope:** dependence of our knowledge on experience does not establish dependence of the world on mind; idealism must also explain shared lawful regularities and private, apparently separate subjects. **Reply:** use an inference to the best overall explanation, rather than inferring ontology directly from how we know; specify common constraints and relations between perspectives. **Remaining burden:** prove explanatory gains beyond relabeling physical structure, with enough constraints to risk failure. Brain effects can be relations within a mentally grounded world, but any particular accommodation needs a model. A universal subject's persistence alone is neither continued personal identity nor a preserved relationship between persons.

**Theism (A-T03-006).** Motivation: if contingent reality calls for explanation and purposive agency is independently defensible, an intentional creator might unify existence, intelligibility and cosmic purpose. TH1 adds a proposed account of good ends and obligations. **Objection, premise and normative scope:** a demand for explanation does not identify mind; suffering and hiddenness challenge specified benevolent intentions; power or creation does not yield moral authority. **Reply:** offer separate arguments for agency and goodness, consider goods involving freedom or regularity, and articulate why a good nature supplies reasons rather than arbitrary commands. **Remaining burden:** these replies need independently justified intentions, proportionate explanatory costs and a noncircular normative bridge. Unspecified divine reasons make accommodation easy while weakening prediction. TH0 may avoid goodness-specific objections, but withdrawing goodness changes the assessed hypothesis and forfeits that moral explanation.

## T01 component mapping and overlap

R = required by this selected model, E = excluded by its stipulated ontology, O = open/not entailed. These are logical bookkeeping marks, not evidential confidence. Qualified cells identify the relevant variant or bearer.

| Model | H-F | H-U | H-B | H-S | H-T | H-P | H-I | H-G | H-A |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PH1/PH2 | E | O | O | O | O | O | O | O | O |
| PL1 | R | O | O | O | O | O | O | O | O |
| PA1 | R | O | O | O | O | O | O | O | O |
| DU1 | Variant | O | O | O | O | O | O | O | O |
| ID1 / ID2 | R | O / R | O | O | O | O | O | O | O |
| TH1 | R for creator | R for all created subjects | O for humans | O | O | O | R | R stipulated | R stipulated |

PH's open H-B permits other physical substrates; it does not promise experience without any realization. PH survival would require an admissible same-person continuation account. Its open purpose and moral columns permit additional agent or normative premises; physicalism alone establishes neither nihilism nor cosmic intention. PL1 can require a physical substrate despite fundamental experiential properties. DU1's H-F depends on whether its subjects are fundamental or irreducibly emergent. PA2's nonexperiential interpretation cannot simply inherit PA1's H-F mark.

TH1 stipulates an irreducible conscious creator grounding all created subjects, which satisfies T01 H-U for that domain even though creator and persons are distinct. The creator's brain independence does not prove any human H-B. ID2 likewise needs a conscious whole with the specified dependence relation; many minds sharing laws are insufficient. Open H-P means no general guarantee of enduring preservation; ordinary stored information can instantiate only a limited version. Any H-S addition inherits T01's identity-dependent H-B and narrow H-P consequences. H-T never follows merely from an enduring ground.

The families consequently overlap: PL1 is property dualist; PA3 can overlap ID2; a theistic model can employ dualist or physically realized created persons. ID2 need not be intentional, whereas TH1 is. A theistic idealist variant is possible if physical reality is grounded in mind while creator and created persons remain distinct. State the joint commitments instead of placing these labels into exclusive probability bins.
