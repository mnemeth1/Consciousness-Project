## Strongest case and countercase

The strongest common-foundation argument begins with experience as an explanatory target, proposes constrained mental grounding and individuation, then asks whether the same relations explain public lawfulness and concrete existence. If independently warranted, this could be an explanatory improvement. Adding worthwhile same-person continuation and defensible purposes could increase what the account explains about enduring significance. Normative authority requires another justified relation. This reconstruction preserves the proposal’s attraction without treating desired outcomes or target-restating assumptions as evidence.

The strongest countercase combines actual neural interventions, incomplete acquisition timing, rival explanatory stopping points and additional identity/normative burdens. Papineau directly addresses phenomenal concepts rather than simply ignoring experience; Chalmers explicitly disputes whether that strategy explains the epistemic situation. The synthesis fairly leaves that disagreement open. [Chalmers](https://consc.net/papers/pceg.pdf), [Papineau](https://www.davidpapineau.co.uk/uploads/1/8/5/5/18551740/perc_and_phen_concepts.pdf).

Neural stimulation supports local causal contribution under its controls and proxies. It does not establish a complete account of experience or metaphysical identity. Conversely, possibility of an interface model is not positive evidence for it without constraints. The single human stimulation case and two intervention macaques retain their real units; repeated trials and awake comparators do not become additional intervention subjects. [Human study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3517886/), [macaque study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8932660/).

H-F therefore retains contested philosophical motivation. H-U remains a conditional unification proposal, allowing one conscious subject or whole and, in a specified domain, creator-dependent persons. H-B remains unresolved beyond local neural contributions. H-S requires an additional identity and irreversible-death warrant. H-T needs a non-successive bearer and a relation to temporal contents. H-P distinguishes records from continuing subjects. H-I depends on independently motivated ends and comparative expectations. H-G objections target specified rich governance/love packages. H-A requires normative warrant beyond creation or unity. None of these judgments implies equal probabilities.
