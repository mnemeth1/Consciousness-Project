# P2T04 — Universal and plural idealism

This packet develops the previously thin idealist comparison without selecting a family winner. Kastrup’s account makes one cosmic consciousness the ontological primitive and explains finite perspectives through dissociation. Leibniz’s selected account gives created perceivers genuine substantial unity and individual laws, while coordinating them through God. The relevant contrast is one conscious medium versus plural created mental substances; it is **not** one deity versus atheism, or one ground versus several uncreated self-sufficient gods. Neither account establishes its actuality merely by accommodating experience and an orderly world. This is an author submission requiring independent review.

The strongest selected universal challenge concerns what makes private subjects exist, rather than merely restricting their access. The strongest selected plural challenge asks why coordinated bodies are needed at all if the soul’s own states already generate every appearance. The actual direct exchange read here is the 1695 *New System*, Foucher’s 1695 criticism and Leibniz’s 1696 *First Explanation*, in the explicitly abbreviated 1998 translations. The later *Monadology* is a developed comparator, not the object of that earlier exchange. No verified direct reply to the selected Chalmers idealism draft or its final chapter has been identified or inspected; Kastrup’s 2018 response to the broader constitution problem is compared argumentatively, without inventing a reply relation. That universal exchange gap remains open.

**The universal account and its strongest challenge.** Kastrup starts with the conditional economical appeal of reducing the relevant facts to cosmic consciousness, then posits experiences as its self-excitation patterns (§§7–8, pp.138–140). The dance and physical-field comparisons explain the intended relation between a bearer and its changing states. They do not supply an independently tested qualitative dynamics. The p.140 statement about an eternal or immutable medium is conditional; it is not a result establishing timeless consciousness.

In §9, pp.140–143, Kastrup models cognitive associations as edges between phenomenal contents and dissociation as disconnection of the graph. He explicitly calls alters different subjects with private qualitative fields (p.142), while grounding their consciousness and generic I-ness in a common medium and treating separation from that medium as illusory (pp.142–143). Both parts matter. It would be misleading to quote only common I-ness and erase his different-subject language, or to quote only different subjects and turn the account into independently existing substances.

Chalmers separates subject, quality and structure constitution (draft p.21; final p.365). His subject objection asks whether the cosmic subject with all its mental states could exist without further distinct macrosubjects. If that is conceivable, if the relevant conceivability entails possibility, and if grounding necessitates the grounded subjects, the proposed grounding fails (draft pp.21–22; final pp.365–366). Those modal links are substantive premises; the passage does not experimentally establish impossibility. It is nevertheless a stronger test than simply asking why one person cannot read another’s thoughts.

Chalmers considers identity cosmopsychism with mode-relative access a coherent candidate, but presses apparent interpersonal difference, different experiences, clinical/cosmic disanalogies and extensive introspective error (draft pp.23–24; final pp.366–367). Nonidentity constitution must explain why experiential parts are subjects and why their existence is necessitated, rather than labeling them parts or vortices (draft p.24; final p.367). His DID discussion explicitly cites Kastrup’s *An Ontological Solution to the Mind–Body Problem* (2017), not *The Universe in Consciousness* (2018). Kastrup2018 cites Chalmers2016a/b and develops its response to privacy/decombination in dialogue with Coleman and Shani; it does not cite the selected idealism draft. Thus the clinical analogy is an available substantive response schema, while a chronological Chalmers2018-critique/Kastrup-rebuttal chain is unsupported.

The strongest charitable assessment is conditional: if dissociation can form private subjects without adding an ontological kind, the account offers more than a bare declaration of one mind and many appearances. Its connected/disconnected representation makes the proposed loss of access intelligible. The remaining problem is whether that access structure is sufficient for the subject facts, their particular phenomenal qualities and cosmic instantiation. If the alters are numerically identical, apparent interpersonal difference requires the revision Chalmers describes; if they are distinct but grounded subjects, an account of necessitation remains due. This is not a demonstration that no fuller response could work.

The final chapter retains this challenge. It also adds the no-highest-level countercase to the appeal to holism: if there is no total cosmos but only indefinitely embedded universes, that cosmos cannot be the cosmic subject (note16, p.371). At pp.368–369 the draft’s “pure/impure Russellian cosmopsychism” terminology becomes “austere/enriched,” separating extra mental structure from the earlier issue of whether fundamental properties are all mental. The extra structure does not thereby establish intention, value or a divine agent. The simulation discussion still allows non-conscious realizers and a distinction between our universe and everything concrete (p.369).

**Illustrations and empirical limits.** Kastrup proposes living organisms as the boundaries of cosmic alters (§10, pp.143–146), using bodily perception/action, metabolism, plant/amoeba behavior and a consciousness-dependent reading of quantum measurement. He cites psychiatric descriptions, co-conscious alters and a sight/blindness case in §9, an fMRI study in §12, and no-report literature in pp.151–152. His §11 model adds cross-boundary impingement and coded perception, drawing on Hoffman and Friston. These underlying originals are not inspected in this packet. We verify what Kastrup argues from them, not their empirical adequacy, the mental status of every organism or the claimed cosmic scaling.

His figures distinguish absent cognitive associations from continuing impingement; treating the latter as the same edges would misread the model (§11, p.146; Figure2 p.147). The brain/body appearance account and common surrounding thoughts accommodate correlations and a shared world within the hypothesis (§§12–13, pp.150–152). They do not derive the required laws or establish a unique subject boundary. Missing reports cannot be converted into positive evidence of inaccessible experiences. Physical nonseparability is not by itself numerical subject identity. No new clinical case, independent event, sample, survival datum or physics result is entered.

**The plural account and actual exchange.** In *Monadology* §§1–18 (pp.268–270), composites require simple substances, and each monad has internal qualitative differences, perception and appetition rather than spatial parts. The mill argument rejects an explanation of perception by mechanical shapes and motions (§17, p.270). This is a substantive anti-mechanist premise and reasoning, not an observation that rules out every functional or identity account. Crucially, §14 distinguishes perception from apperception/consciousness, while §19 distinguishes bare perception from more distinct perception with memory. Calling all monads ordinary conscious persons would therefore overstate this text.

The created plurality is genuine within the account but remains dependent on God. God is the original simple substance and created monads are products (§47, p.274); their mutual influence is ideal and coordinated through divine ordering (§§51–52, pp.274–275). Different perspectives express one world (§§56–62, pp.275–277), without becoming experiences of a single numerically identical created subject. Soul and body follow their respective final/efficient laws in harmony (§§78–81, p.279). The selected plural-ground comparison is thus a metaphysical account of plural irreducible created perceivers and their world, with theological coordination. It is not established that Leibniz endorsed every requirement of Chalmers’s modern “all concrete facts grounded in mental facts” definition, nor that the1695 ontology and1714 ontology are identical.

The earlier *New System* argues that aggregates require true unities, introduces active substantial forms, and requires physical explanations to remain mechanical while metaphysical principles supply their grounding (§§2–4, pp.145–146; §§11–18, pp.148–152). The model is not an arbitrary collection of private perceptions: individual representational natures and divine coordination are the proposed constraints. Yet this explanatory structure carries commitments needing independent defense.

Foucher questions how unextended points could supply extension (§2, pp.180–181), and why organic unity would warrant a special animal principle of consciousness (§3, p.181). His strongest coordination objection concedes the possibility of synchronized soul/body sequences but asks why God would need bodies if every appearance could occur without them, as in dreaming; he then challenges a system built to preserve prior principles and offers effort/resistance as a possible interaction route (§§4–5, pp.181–183). This combines parsimony, epistemic underdetermination and causal objection. It is not an argument that idealism must be impossible because unusual.

Leibniz’s actual reply distinguishes foundations of corporeal mass from mathematical extension, and true animal unity from clock-like aggregation (*First Explanation* §2, pp.187–188). He rejects the inference from animal feeling to human rationality. In §§3–4 (p.188), he says harmony follows from his view of unities, explains correspondence through original natural laws without recurring special interventions, and answers the unnecessary-bodies objection through God’s preference for more substances and correspondence to an external domain. The torpid-body scenario is excluded by the harmony premise (§4, pp.188–189), rather than by a new observation discriminating it. In §§5–6 (pp.189–190), he argues that fitting appearances is not a defect and independent a priori reasons would improve a theory; forces are intrinsic, not transmitted between substances. Section7 (p.190) adds a conservation-of-force/direction argument against an interactionist alternative. The contemporary physics and physiology are source assertions here, not newly verified modern laws or a decisive causal-closure proof.

This reply corrects conflations between an aggregate and a substantial unity and articulates how the proposed system intends to preserve lawfulness. It does not establish the foundational unity premise, divine choice, every law-to-appearance correspondence or uniqueness of that explanation. The full French exchange, omitted translation passages, Leibniz’s separate1695 *Remarks*, later Bayle exchange and contemporary plural-idealist defenders remain outside inspection. Neither no-influx metaphysics nor metaphorical cosmic dissociation is certified merely because it can be stated consistently.
