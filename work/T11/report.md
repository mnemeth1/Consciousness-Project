# T11 — Spacetime, timelessness and physical ontology

Author submission, 12 September 2026. Protocol v2; publication cutoff 11 September 2026. Independent R11 review is required before integration.

## Finding and scope

The inspected physics permits descriptions of temporal histories without a privileged external clock. It does not establish that physical change is unreal or that a conscious foundation lacks intrinsic succession. A timeless conscious foundation is a substantive philosophical proposal. Its strongest form distinguishes the ordered history it knows or grounds from a succession of changes in its own awareness. That distinction avoids an immediate contradiction, but leaves disputed questions about knowledge, agency, concrete grounding and personal identity.

Five originals supply the decisive material: Einstein’s 1905 kinematics, Rovelli’s *Partial observables*, Boethius’s account of eternity and providence, and two Craig arguments. Nine single queries used the eight-query baseline plus the sole authorized one-query extension. Seven inspection units include a partial second representation of one Craig article and one unrelated repository landing page; they are not seven independent arguments. No new empirical case is proposed. Twenty claims and five reconstructed arguments remain unreviewed author proposals.

## What “time” and “timeless” mean

| Term | Bearer and meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinate time | A temporal coordinate assigned to events in a chosen description | A coordinate label is not automatically a measured clock or unique cosmic present. |
| Global time | An assignment across a specified model or domain | Selecting one does not establish that it is physically privileged; no theorem that every spacetime admits one is asserted here. |
| Proper time | Elapsed time along a physical clock’s worldline | Different elapsed readings do not mean that neither clock undergoes succession. |
| Relational time | A clock or changing variable used to locate another variable’s reading | Relational change remains even if no variable is privileged as external time. |
| Emergent time | Time recovered from a more basic description under stated assumptions | This packet examines a timeless formalism, not a demonstrated mechanism of spacetime emergence. |
| Temporal order | Earlier/later relations | An ordering need not provide a uniquely moving global present. |
| Experienced duration or succession | Apparent length or changing contents of a subject’s awareness | Reports do not by themselves identify the ontology of time. T08/T09 supply the reviewed report boundaries. |
| H-T | No intrinsic temporal succession in the specified bearer | Not endless duration, a different time dimension, or ignorance of elapsed time. |
| Eternity | Either timelessness or unending successive existence | These meanings must remain separate. |

Intrinsic change means the bearer itself differs in its states across succession. Relational change can mean that something else changes its relation to an unchanged bearer. Whether all causally or epistemically significant relations require intrinsic change is part of the dispute, not a conclusion settled by these definitions.

## The original physics and its limits

Einstein synchronizes clocks by light signals and derives transformations between uniformly moving frames. Distant simultaneity becomes frame dependent; moving clocks still record elapsed time. The inspected electronic edition is John Walker’s 1999 version based on the 1923 English translation, with notation changes and editorial corrections disclosed. Original German and a modern experimental test series were not inspected. **CL-T11-001–003.** [Einstein, introduction and §§1–4; edition notes](https://www.fourmilab.ch/etexts/einstein/specrel/www/).

In modern notation, our direct restatement of the displayed kinematic relations is

\[
t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2),\qquad \gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.
\]

For a clock following \(x=vt\), \(\Delta t'=\Delta t\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}>0\) when \(\Delta t>0\) and \(|v|<c\). This narrow deduction preserves clock order. It is not a derivation of a theory of subjective awareness. Einstein’s translated \(\tau\) denotes the moving frame’s coordinate time; on this clock’s worldline it supplies that clock’s elapsed reading. No claim about gravity or an actual light-speed observer follows.

A geometric block can represent different events and their temporal relations together. Describing the entire history without changing the description does not make its events simultaneous, or make its inhabitants experience everything together. Eternalism adds an ontological claim about the existence of events; a drawing or coordinate representation alone does not establish it. Conversely, the presence of ordered change inside a history does not logically refute an atemporal explanation of the history as a whole. These are our inference boundaries, not quotations from Einstein.

Rovelli distinguishes partial observables from predictive correlations. His constrained treatment expresses dynamics through relations across histories, without selecting privileged external time. Section VI’s detailed construction is explicitly restricted to finitely many degrees of freedom. Section V preserves relative clock evolution despite invariance under coordinate-time changes. This is a formal and interpretive proposal, not a new experiment establishing a complete quantum cosmology. The arXiv v3 header says 21 January 2002 while the title block says 23 October 2018; that discrepancy remains explicit. **CL-T11-004–006.** [Rovelli, §§II–VII, especially equations (4)–(11) and (21)–(22)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0110035).

The strongest positive lesson is that familiar external time need not appear in every adequate formulation. The strongest caution is supplied by the same construction: removing that role does not remove relations, clock readings or physical evolution. This packet neither endorses every claim Rovelli makes about observables nor establishes that spacetime is nonfundamental in our universe. It does not review competing quantum-gravity programs. **A-T11-001; CL-T11-018.**

## A strong timeless-mind account

In Boethius, Philosophy distinguishes eternity’s whole possession of life from indefinitely extended successive life. Divine priority concerns simplicity rather than an earlier date. Providence unifies what unfolds temporally; V.6 answers the foreknowledge challenge by distinguishing conditional necessity from simple necessity. The text is H. R. James’s 1897 translation, whose preface identifies Peiper’s 1874 Latin edition. The translator’s “single moment” must be read with the explicit contrast to temporal duration. The Latin and historical claims about Plato were not independently audited. **CL-T11-007–008.** [Boethius, IV.6 and V.3–6](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/14328/14328-h/14328-h.htm).

Craig separately defends possible timeless personhood by considering a God without temporal creation. He argues that self-awareness, rational knowledge and volition need not require discursive learning; not every intention concerns a future good. He also considers timeless existence without creation and temporal existence with creation. These are distinct options, and his personhood defense does not itself prove timeless interaction with our world. **CL-T11-011–013.** [Craig, scope paragraphs and the consciousness, intentionality and interpersonal sections](https://www.reasonablefaith.org/writings/scholarly-writings/divine-eternity/divine-timelessness-and-personhood/).

Our strongest M2 reconstruction, **A-T11-002**, has four substantive premises:

1. A conscious bearer can have non-discursive awareness of a complete, internally ordered history without acquiring successive states.
2. This bearer stands in an explanatory grounding relation to the concrete history, rather than merely representing an independently existing history.
3. Its intention concerns the history and independently specified ends within it. Having reasons for that intention need not require a process of deliberation.
4. Temporal individual subjects can depend on the foundation while retaining their own ordered perspectives.

If these premises hold, temporal manifestation does not require the foundation to accumulate experiences sequentially. The model could unify conscious reality, temporal order and intentional explanation. That is a positive explanatory proposal, not merely an assertion that physics has failed.

The cost is substantial: the premises are not secured by naming the bearer “foundation.” The model must explain why its relation grounds concrete existence, why its content is experiential, how individual perspectives arise, and why a conscious rather than impersonal ground explains the relevant facts better. One fixed act is not automatically rational agency; the defender owes an account of action for reasons. A representation of alternatives is not by itself a selection of one actual world. If necessary nature and a necessitating relation determine every detail, preserving contingency requires additional explanation, as already flagged in T10.

## The hardest knowledge and response problems

Craig’s original objection makes objective A-theoretic tense crucial: if complete knowledge includes what is presently occurring, and knowing that requires temporal location, divine timelessness is incompatible with it. He explicitly acknowledges that a B-theory denying irreducibly tensed facts escapes this particular inference. He also argues that replacing propositions with intuition does not resolve the problem if the same changing actuality still must be known. **CL-T11-009–010.** [Craig, opening numbered argument, discussion of intuition and conclusion](https://www.reasonablefaith.org/writings/scholarly-writings/divine-eternity/omniscience-tensed-facts-and-divine-eternity/).

The reconstructed dispute, **A-T11-003**, must distinguish three things. A dated fact says that an event occurs at a particular time. An indexical belief locates a person as “here” or “now.” An irreducibly tensed fact would concern which time is objectively present beyond those dated and contextual relations. A model that timelessly knows the event and knows the person’s indexical perspective need not claim that the knower is that person or acquires the person’s beliefs successively. But whether it thereby knows everything about objective presentness is exactly what is contested. **CL-T11-015.**

A strong atemporal reply denies that there is an additional moving present to track. It can grant the reality of ordered events and each subject’s situated experience while denying a further global fact of presentness. This is a substantive B-theoretic response, not ignorance relabeled omniscience. Its burden is explaining the force of tense and passage without omitting a genuine feature of reality. A different reply retains objective tense but posits a distinct non-successive access relation; that relation needs an account, not a stipulation that all times are one ordinary present. This is our conditional reconstruction; Wierenga’s and Leftow’s original replies have not been inspected, and Craig’s assessment of them does not settle their success.

For responsiveness, compare two proposals. On the first, a foundation newly notices an event, reconsiders, and changes its intention. That foundation has intrinsic succession under H-T. On the second, a complete reasons-sensitive intention grounds a history in which a response follows a request; had the request or reasons differed, the intended history would have differed. The latter counterfactual dependence is between possible histories, not a sequence of revisions within the actual foundation. It is intelligible as a proposed relation, but whether it amounts to genuine responsiveness remains disputed. Calling a relational change “merely extrinsic” does not answer an objection that new awareness is essential. Equally, defining every response as a later mental update excludes the competing account before evaluating it. **CL-T11-016; A-T11-004.**

A temporal conscious foundation is a serious alternative. It can know a changing present and respond through successive states while remaining enduring or metaphysically basic. It gives up H-T for that bearer, not necessarily H-F, H-I or survival. Craig’s timeless-without/temporal-with creation model still owes a noncontradictory account of the relation: “without creation” cannot mean an earlier temporal interval before time exists. No preferred cosmic clock or particular cosmology is established here.

Boethius’s necessity distinction also matters. In our logical reconstruction, necessarily, if an infallible knower knows that p, then p; it does not follow from that alone that p is necessary in every possible world. A critic can still question whether the content of the knowledge is modally fixed or how it depends on free choices. Thus the simple modal fallacy is blocked without pretending the entire foreknowledge/free-will debate is resolved. Neither timeless representation nor temporal succession alone settles determinism.

## Personal continuation and preservation

| T01 model | Where succession occurs | What inclusion or accumulation means | Survival boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1: temporal personal continuation | In the same person’s experiences after bodily death | The person may learn or remember sequentially | Needs a death condition, substrate/ground and identity relation; H-T is unnecessary. |
| M2: timeless foundation, temporal persons | In dependent persons; not intrinsically in the foundation | A history is known or grounded as a whole; additions occur within the person’s sequence | A timeless foundation does not by itself extend any person’s life. |
| M3: atemporal personal inclusion | Must distinguish ordered contents, the included perspective and the including bearer | Complete finite-history inclusion is not sequential acquisition by the whole | A finite record or represented life alone is H-P, not H-S. A richer model needs same-person subjectivity beyond the bodily-life boundary. |

These mappings use T01’s definitions; they are our conceptual analysis, **CL-T11-017; A-T11-005**. Even experiential knowledge of a person by another mind does not automatically make the knower numerically identical to that person. Conversely, a model promising an enlarged first-person history should be evaluated under its stated identity account rather than rejected merely because it is unusual. T13 must handle identity, interruption, branching, memory, the irreversible-death condition and the exact meaning of beyond-death subjectivity. This task does not repeat T08’s temporal-report review.

## Assessment, dependence and possible discrimination

All nine components remain **unresolved in this task**, for different reasons. H-T receives both a conditional positive model and a substantial conditional objection. H-F and H-U need an experiential ground and individuation account. H-B and H-S receive no new empirical demonstration here. H-P requires specifying what is preserved; H-I requires actual reasons-guided agency. H-G and H-A need normative premises that neither geometry nor eternity supplies. The companion component file lists individual IDs, assumptions, dependence, sensitivity and outstanding questions. These are not nine equally likely propositions, and unresolved alternatives need not have equal support.

The direct physics and the philosophical models play different roles. Boethius and the Craig discussions share commitments concerning knowledge, personhood and explanation; reusing them is not independent corroboration. Removing NDE reports leaves this packet’s formal and conceptual arguments intact, while removing objective tense disables the tensed-knowledge objection in its stated form. Requiring deliberative updating excludes the proposed timeless agency; accepting non-discursive intention preserves a candidate model without showing that it is actual. Changing the identity criterion changes M3’s survival assessment and must remain visible. **CL-T11-020.**

Discrimination requires more than compatibility. A specified physical theory can be tested through its clock or correlation predictions; those tests do not automatically test an added conscious-ground hypothesis. If two ontologies predict the same physical and reported observations, those observations alone cannot choose between them. A proposed mental intervention could become empirically relevant if it independently specifies distinct outcomes, timing and controls; an unspecified ability to produce any outcome supplies no such comparison. Even independently verified anomalous information would need another argument to identify timelessness over temporal alternatives. No such new verification is claimed. **CL-T11-019.**
