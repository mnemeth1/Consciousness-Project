## The inspected exchange

[O'Connor's 1994 defense](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/OConnor_Emergent-Properties-1994.pdf), *Emergent Properties*, American Philosophical Quarterly 31(2), 91–104, combines supervenience, nonstructurality and downward causal influence (pp. 97–98). Its explanation of novelty distinguishes immediate microphysical activity from further powers mediated through the emergent property; it discusses the alternative of hidden microproperties or dispositions (pp. 98–99). These are philosophical proposals, not measured neural effects. **CL-P2T01-001.**

[Kim's challenge](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1004563122154), *Making Sense of Emergence* (1999), is inspected in the explicitly credited 2008 MIT Press reprint, chapter 7, printed pp. 127–153. Kim permits ordinary downward causation, questions simultaneous reflexive dependence, and argues that a sufficient noncausal emergence base preempts the purported emergent cause (reprint §§7.6–7.8, pp. 143–150). His notes explicitly recognize microstructural causal powers and cite O'Connor's attempted counterargument (p. 153, notes 35–37). He also treats qualia as promising irreducibility candidates rather than claiming their reduction already established (pp. 138–139). **CL-P2T01-002–003.** The [institutional reprint](https://lps.elte.hu/gomori/reduction/readings/Emergence%20-%20Contemporary%20Readings%20in%20Philosophy%20and%20Science%20by%20Mark%20A.%20Bedau%2C%20Paul%20Humphreys%20%28z-lib.org%29.pdf) identifies the original at printed p. 150 and its reproduction provenance at preliminary p. xiii. PDF page = reprint printed page + 17. Original journal pagination must not be inferred by a constant offset.

[O'Connor and Wong's 2005 account](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OConnor_MetaphEmergence05.pdf), *The Metaphysics of Emergence*, Noûs 39(4), 658–678, replaces synchronic supervenience with causal generation over time; note 8 explicitly repudiates the earlier supervenience formulation (pp. 664–668, 675). Composite systems bear basic nonstructural properties, supported by organized microstructure. The reply to Kim makes emergents intermediate causes rather than independent redundant causes (pp. 669–670). Their illustrative dynamics separate sustaining configuration from changing physical and mental states (p. 666, diagram visually inspected). They acknowledge a contest over simplicity against microdispositional alternatives and rely on a disputed introspective motivation (pp. 671–674). **CL-P2T01-004–006.**

This is an inspected chronological exchange, not a set of independent evidential confirmations. The two defenses share an author and developing framework. Kim's reprint p. 149 contains the passage which the 2005 paper quotes as original p. 32; that is a passage-level cross-reference, not verification of every original page. The original typeset Kim PDF remains inaccessible through the attempted journal/university routes. The substantive challenge itself is accessible in the reprint.

[Shoemaker's 2002 reply](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1015708030227), *Kim on Emergence*, Philosophical Studies 108, 53–63, is now inspected in the user-supplied journal PDF. Printed pp. 53–63 map to PDF pages 1–11; PDF page 12 is visually blank. Shoemaker argues that powers manifest only in suitable combinations can belong to the micro-entities throughout: the complete microfacts still fix the macrofacts (pp. 54–57). His initially distinct-property version distinguishes necessitation from causal power and rejects Kim's preemption inference (pp. 58–60). His preferred account individuates properties by essential causal features, identifies the purportedly two properties and revises how supervenience is expressed (pp. 60–63). **CL-P2T01-012–013.** He acknowledges a causal-base option before rejecting it on his own individuation assumptions (p. 61, visually checked). Note 1 cites Kim with an inconsistent 1–34 extent; the verified journal extent is 3–36. The original-page quotations at Shoemaker pp. 58 and 60 independently point to Kim p. 32, matching the passage at reprint p. 149; this still does not supply a complete pagination map.

## What follows from the arguments

The following assessment is project reasoning, distinguished from the attributions above. Its premises remain contestable.

The strongest positive case is that a theory can acknowledge genuine experiential characteristics, their causal importance and their dependence on organized physical systems together. A new property need not be a separate substance inserted into an otherwise complete physical process. Its causal production can instead be part of that process's full explanation. If causal dependence were itself reduction, ordinary intermediate causes would become dispensable simply because earlier conditions produced them; that does not follow without an additional account of causal relevance. This supports a possible model, not the claim that introspection reveals every metaphysical constituent of an experience. **A-P2T01-001.**

The strongest challenge is not that all wholes influencing parts are contradictory. Grant a complete sufficient basal explanation, an irreducible emergent effect and causal exclusion: an added mental explanation needs a nonredundant role. If the base-to-mental link is simultaneous noncausal determination, describing a causal chain does not answer the problem. Moving the physical effect later removes one circularity worry but alone does not remove preemption. **A-P2T01-002.**

The developed response rejects that assumed dependence structure and the relevant completeness thesis. In a causal sequence from physical organization through experience to later effects, the earlier physical conditions act partly through the emergent. It is not a model with both an independently complete physical route and an additional mental route. Consequently Kim's selected preemption argument does not establish a contradiction in this revised model merely from its label. This is a restricted result: a conditional argument whose disputed premise is rejected cannot be used as a premise-free disproof; equally, rejecting the premise does not show that the revised ontology describes our world. **CL-P2T01-007; A-P2T01-003.**

The strongest remaining explanatory countercase asks why the extra property is better than an expanded microphysical disposition or law. Both descriptions must be specified before simplicity can decide between them. Counting one emergent property while ignoring its generation, maintenance, quality and subject-boundary rules would favor it unfairly. Counting all physical laws as costless while charging every experiential law would favor its rival unfairly. Moreover, successful prediction of behavior alone need not identify the intrinsic nature of its bearer. No comparison here derives a unique phenomenal quality or a complete subject boundary from an independently confirmed emergence law. **CL-P2T01-008.**

Shoemaker sharpens that countercase without functioning as a direct empirical rebuttal. A response to Kim can favor a microstructural account and conceptual redescription rather than the basic nonstructural properties of the 2005 defense. His property-identity conclusion depends on essential causal individuation and noncausal dependence; it does not follow just from observed co-occurrence. Conversely, the 2005 authors' simplicity comparison and history-dependent thought experiment do not independently defeat that rival: the stipulated histories, excluded physical probabilities and disputed ontology require justification. **CL-P2T01-014; A-P2T01-004.** The dispute is therefore partly over what properties and their causal powers are, and what would distinguish the resulting theories, not merely whether downward causation can receive a coherent description.

## C1–C6 assessment

- **C1, coherence:** the diachronic causal model avoids the specific simultaneous-base structure targeted in the challenge. Full metaphysical consistency is not proved, and the 1994 and 2005 dependence claims must remain distinguished.
- **C2, scope:** it addresses experiential properties, dependence and mental causal influence. It does not by itself explain why physical reality exists, supply a universal conscious subject, or establish survival and cosmic purpose.
- **C3, independent warrant and cost:** count the basic experiential properties, causal powers, emergence and maintenance conditions, and quality/subject assignment rules. First-person access motivates the proposal but does not independently prove absence of hidden physical constitution.
- **C4, evidential fit:** the packet contributes no new empirical evidence. The requirement to reproduce specific neural-dependence patterns inherited from v1 remains. Compatibility with dependence is not exclusive support.
- **C5, discrimination:** a specified theory should fix its onset conditions, temporal dependencies and predicted departures from a specified physical model before testing. An unrestricted appeal to complexity cannot earn a successful prediction. An apparent departure must also rule out misspecified physical dynamics; it cannot simply be renamed emergence.
- **C6, residual problems:** the now directly inspected microdispositional alternative, the basis for phenomenal irreducibility, causal measurement, and subject individuation remain. Shoemaker's preferred identity conclusion and the 2005 authors' nonstructural ontology disagree; neither attribution settles the premise truth. The wider later debate remains incompletely covered.

These are assessments of one selected account, not scores for an entire family. **CL-P2T01-008–010.**

## Component and v1 changes

**H-F:** do not place strong emergence and all fundamental experience in mutually exclusive boxes. The selected account posits basic experiential property types while making their instances causally dependent. If its nonstructural ontology is granted, that can satisfy a limited property-level fundamentality reading; it does not yield an independently existing conscious substance or consciousness grounding the physical universe. T01 distinguishes causal production from constitutive grounding. This conditional classification supplies no new actuality warrant. **CL-P2T01-009.**

**H-B:** causal influence on brain states is not independence from the configuration required to sustain experience. The selected maintenance assumption keeps that condition operative. This packet neither observes experience without it nor proves such independence impossible for every alternative. **CL-P2T01-010.**

**H-U/S/T/P/I/G/A:** no positive upgrade. A composite bearer is not a universal subject; causal evolution presupposes succession for the modeled experiences; a new property supplies neither same-person persistence after death nor complete preservation, intention, goodness or moral authority. These are separate questions, not equal-probability labels. **CL-P2T01-011.**
