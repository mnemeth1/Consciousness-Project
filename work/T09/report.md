# T09 — Neural dependence and consciousness theories

Author submission, 2026-09-12. Protocol v2; publication cutoff 2026-09-11. Independent R09 review is required before integration.

## Finding

Neural interventions can change reported perceptual content and behavioral arousal under controlled conditions. This goes beyond passive correlation. It supports causal contributions of neural systems to embodied conscious functions, while leaving the precise relation between neural processing, experience, access, reporting and memory partly unresolved. It does not establish physicalist identity or disprove every form of brain independence. Conversely, a limitation of a neural theory is not evidence for survival or a universal mind.

The bounded packet includes four empirical originals, a prior theory-test protocol, the original article’s supplementary adversarial discussion, a later GNWT critique, and two author-name corrections: nine inspection units. Thirteen single queries used twelve baseline queries plus one authorized correction-access query. Twenty claims and two arguments are proposed. The four study records contain related arms, trial repetitions and reused comparators; their denominators must not be added.

## Distinguishing the measurements

Wakefulness/arousal describes activation and behavior; responsiveness is performance on a specified output test. Connected experience concerns the environment, while disconnected experience can include internally generated dreams. Reportability and later memory are additional capacities. An absent response, absent recalled content or absent neural marker does not directly verify absence of experience. A later report is evidence of remembered experience with timing and memory uncertainty. A neural predictor of a report is not automatically its cause or the experience itself.

### Human perceptual content

The fusiform experiment aligned fMRI and ECoG face selectivity before stimulation. The subject reported pronounced distortion of real faces during stimulation, without the same effect in sham or nearby-pair trials. EEG monitoring found no after-discharges or seizures during stimulation. This supports causal involvement in the reported percept, rather than merely a neural correlate. **CL-T09-001–002.** [Original, Methods EBS and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3517886/).

The specificity is qualified: the subject described small nonspecific changes for a television and balloon text. Famous-face naming errors were 1/5 during real stimulation versus 2/5 during sham, and place errors were 2/5 versus 0/5. Those small tests differed in instructions and stimulation timing from real-face viewing; they neither establish equivalence nor prove that all recognition was intact. Current could affect distant connected sites, and the two fusiform regions were stimulated together. Trial order randomization and assessor masking were not established in the inspected text. The video was not separately reviewed. A 2013 correction changes Nathan Witthoft’s name only. [Correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3668436/).

### Human anesthesia and sleep

The anesthesia allocation was open and randomized: propofol 19 and dexmedetomidine 20 after one withdrawal. Thirty-seven members of the same cohort returned for sleep, on average 18 weeks later. They are not 76 independent participants. Unresponsiveness meant zero of ten instructed handle presses to sentences. Forced awakening used verbal and, when needed, mild tactile stimulation while infusion was maintained. **CL-T09-003–006.** [Original, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8115882/).

Thirteen of 19 propofol and 16 of 20 dexmedetomidine participants could be awakened, but only nine propofol responsive scans were usable: three participants had fluctuating behavior and one an intravenous-line malfunction. State-transition comparisons used 19 propofol, 14 dexmedetomidine and nine sleep participants for disconnection, and nine propofol and 16 dexmedetomidine participants for reconnection. Because only two propofol participants reached a second unresponsive state, its loss contrast used moderate sedation versus initial unresponsiveness at different drug levels. Therefore, not every comparison isolates state at an identical dose. PET integrated relative blood flow over 90 seconds; it was not a fine-grained clock for a specific dream.

The Results section reports experience in **80% of anesthesia interviews and 71% of sleep interviews**, including contentless impressions. Exact counts underlying these percentages are unavailable in the inspected main text. Later prose calls 80% a proportion of arousable subjects; that unit inconsistency is preserved. Initial anesthesia questioning about dreaming occurred immediately after awakening, followed by a PET scan and then the detailed interview. Sleep interviews followed awakening directly. Do not describe every detailed anesthesia interview as occurring before imaging.

Three states with reports of current environmental awareness were excluded from disconnected comparisons: one after propofol UR2, one after dexmedetomidine UR2, and one after N2 sleep. These need not represent three disjoint people across overlapping arms. Unarousable states lacked immediate interviews. The authors themselves acknowledge that absence of a report can reflect amnesia, and that no-response criteria depend on salience and motor requirements. Their PET findings associate thalamic, cingulate and angular regions with connectedness. The stronger author language of necessary and minimally sufficient mechanisms is not established by a focal necessity/sufficiency intervention in this design.

### Macaque state modulation

High CT stimulation raised the reported arousal score from 0/11 under anesthesia to 9/11 in both DBS macaques, compared with 11/11 awake, 3/11 under low CT stimulation, and 0/11 with either tested VL intensity. Stimulation was 3 V versus 5 V at 130.208 Hz. This is evidence for a causal change in behavior under the tested conditions. **CL-T09-007–009.** [Original, Figure 2 and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8932660/).

Behavior was assessed outside the scanner without paralysis. All anesthetic fMRI sessions used a muscle blocker. High CT also broadened the repertoire of fMRI states and enhanced responses to hierarchical auditory violations. The auditory task was passive, acquired only without stimulation and with high CT; **there were no auditory VL-control data**. Previously published awake data from other animals supplied the comparison. Awareness remains inferred through signatures rather than a trialwise experience report. Physiological changes, recruitment of several medial nuclei, and stimulation of afferent/efferent axons constrain localization. The intact anesthetized brain is not interchangeable with severe human structural injury. Run counts and detailed supplementary exclusions were not inspected; no population effect size is pooled from sessions or voxels.

## The contemporary adversarial theory test

The original Nature study and earlier [2023 protocol](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0268577) explicitly compare IIT and GNWT biological implementations. All main stimuli were suprathreshold, with four categories and 0.5-, 1.0- and 1.5-second durations. Task relevance varied; orientation was always irrelevant. The design did not directly contrast conscious and unconscious processing, and it did not perturb PFC or posterior cortex. A separate 39-person surprise-memory control supports encoding of irrelevant items, but cannot certify continuous awareness of every attribute or offset on every main-study trial. **CL-T09-010–012.** [Nature, Figure 1 and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1).

Recruitment was fMRI 120, MEG 102 and iEEG 34; retained samples were 108, 97 and 32. Noninvasive datasets split into optimization/held-out groups of 35/73 for fMRI and 32/65 for MEG. iEEG was not split and used different analysis samples: 31 for activation, 29 for decoding and 28 for RSA. The paper discloses exclusions and the retention of three iEEG participants marginally below its behavioral threshold. The published protocol had described a half-split, while the final study used one-third optimization for the noninvasive modalities. These version changes are recorded, not silently harmonized.

| Prediction | Original expectation | Observed qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Content decoding | Posterior emphasis for IIT; PFC role for GNWT | Category decoding in both; limited PFC identity/orientation evidence. MEG orientation results retain leakage uncertainty. IIT decoding was a noncritical prediction. |
| Maintenance | Sustained posterior activity/content versus PFC onset/offset ignition | Some sustained posterior representations; transient PFC onset signals; expected offset profile not established. Posterior orientation was not sustained. |
| Connectivity | Sustained posterior gamma versus transient long-range PFC synchrony | Preregistered phase-consistency analysis supported neither critical prediction. Exploratory amplitude-based DFC partly supported GNWT and still lacked sustained posterior connectivity. |

These are mixed outcomes. Sparse sampling matters: the cited comparison included 12 V1/V2 electrodes versus 472 PFC electrodes, and posterior pair analyses involved four patients. That does not turn nulls into positive findings. Nor can exploratory DFC become a successful preregistered phase-synchrony test. Main fMRI connectivity results pooled task conditions, with separate conditions reported elsewhere. **CL-T09-013–014,018.** [Nature, Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1).

### Serious challenges and replies

The later [GNWT critique](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147) argues that offset ignition requires consciously attending to offset, continuous awareness was not measured, and mixed neural selectivity can make PFC information hard to decode at macroscopic resolution. The original 2023 protocol already qualified offset ignition by conscious detection and elsewhere said it was possible; it also presented onset/offset as the operational expectation. Thus, the later objection is not wholly a new post-result invention. The conditional wording and the committed test both remain relevant. The critique contains a wording error about absent category decoding; the original reports category decoding. Its [2026 correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13134662/) changes only Xiao-Jing Wang’s name. **CL-T09-015–016.**

The [original supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-08888-1/MediaObjects/41586_2025_8888_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), sections 12–14, includes both adversaries’ interpretations and declared deviations. IIT proponents acknowledge failure of sustained gamma synchrony but emphasize coverage, frequency choice and relaxed ROI criteria. Their claim that posterior cortex is sufficient is an interpretation, not a direct inactivation finding. GNWT proponents emphasize onset activity and decoding, dispute uninterrupted awareness, and request causal tests. The theory-neutral consortium argues that high-quality suprathreshold signals, multiple modalities and prior agreement make the failures informative. Neither measurement caveats nor prior agreement alone settles the dispute. A full OSF amendment-history audit and a separate later consortium reply were not obtained. **CL-T09-017–018.**

## Argument and component assessment

**A-T09-001** is the strongest dependence case: controlled neural perturbations change embodied perceptual reports and arousal/access functions, so models denying all neural causal contribution face difficulty. The strongest objection is that an intervention may alter routing, reporting or an interface rather than constitute experience. The reply is that such a model must explain the content-specific, reversible pattern with prospective predictions.

**A-T09-002** limits metaphysical extrapolation: a causal contribution is not an identity relation, and failures of operational neural theories do not establish survival. This leaves ontological underdetermination; it does not make all alternatives equally supported. **CL-T09-019–020.**

All nine T01 components remain unresolved in this task. H-B is relevant but means more than absence of brain influence: causal dependence in current embodied functions constrains a model while leaving its proposed possibility of experience without specified neural processes untested here. H-F and H-U are not identified by neural localization. H-S is not tested beyond irreversible death under a specified identity criterion. H-T and H-P do not follow from neural reports or stored information. H-I, H-G and H-A require independent intentional or normative premises. The companion component file records claim IDs, assumptions, dependence, sensitivity and outstanding questions separately; no numerical posterior is assigned.

## What would distinguish the alternatives

1. Manipulate offset awareness and measure it with a preregistered method before evaluating conditional GNWT offset predictions. Compare attended/ignored offsets while retaining report-confound controls.
2. Predetermine ROI, frequency and phase/amplitude metrics, adequate coverage and sensitivity criteria, then independently replicate the revised IIT/GNWT predictions. A successful revision is new evidence, not retroactive confirmation.
3. Use reversible, spatially better-characterized interventions with matched sensory/motor/report controls to separate causal contribution to specific content from access and reporting. A null needs demonstrated engagement and power.
4. Validate animal markers against independent behavioral discrimination where possible; do not substitute a marker label for direct knowledge of phenomenology.
5. Specify a brain-independent model’s distinct predictions about experience, timing and information access. A generic compatibility claim cannot identify a likelihood ratio.

Removing the one human stimulation case weakens this packet’s direct human content evidence; removing the animal study removes its direct state-arousal intervention comparison. Removing report evidence leaves physiological and behavioral findings with weaker experiential interpretation. Removing the theory test does not erase causal intervention results. None of these sensitivity changes by itself establishes a cosmic component. This is a focused packet, not an exhaustive neural or lesion review; broader coverage remains for G2 and philosophical comparison for T12.
