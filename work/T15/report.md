# T15 — Moral grounding, suffering and hiddenness

Author submission, 12 September 2026. Protocol v2; independent R15 review required.

**An enduring conscious foundation could unite moral value, personal responsibility and eventual restoration. But each connection needs its own justification.** Conscious experience supplies possible beneficiaries and victims; it does not, by itself, supply impartial obligation. A good creator might have authority, but creation and power alone do not establish that authority. Endurance can make accountability possible without explaining wrongness. These distinctions preserve the strongest integrated proposal while identifying what would make it explanatory.

## Value, obligation and authority

Craig locates value in divine nature and obligation in authoritative commands. His reply permits commands that could differ while remaining consistent with essential goodness. A simple dilemma about an unconstrained divine will therefore misses his stated position. The remaining question is why the proposed being counts as a qualified authority. **CL-T15-001–002,019; A-T15-001.**

The strongest project reconstruction begins with facts about subjects and their welfare, adds an objective reason to care about that welfare, and then adds a relation extending claims across persons. It proposes that these relations depend on one necessarily good personal reality. This could offer more than a threat of punishment: a personal good might explain why persons are worthy of concern and why demands are legitimately addressed to agents. But an account must establish the proposed identity or grounding relation, its scope and our warrant for recognizing it. Defining goodness as whatever a powerful creator happens to do would not answer those questions.

Shared consciousness also needs care. If another’s pain will eventually be experienced as mine, I may acquire a prudential incentive to prevent it. That does not yet establish that the other matters independently of my future interests, that all interests count equally, or that some may be sacrificed for a larger total. Distinct persons and rights cannot disappear unnoticed into a claim of unity. Conversely, duties need not vanish merely because an agent lacks motivation to comply. **CL-T15-016–019.**

The grounding matrix separately tracks the origin of moral behavior, moral truth, justification and knowledge, motivation, value, obligation and accountability. Evolutionary cooperation can address the first question without answering the others. No conclusion about a person’s moral character follows from whether their metaethics is theistic or secular.

## A substantive secular countercase

Railton grounds informed-interest judgments in natural facts and proposes an impartial moral criterion. He explicitly allows obligation to apply without supplying every rational agent a categorical reason to comply. The missing categorical force is a limitation he acknowledges, not something to conceal when presenting his realism. **CL-T15-003–005; A-T15-002.**

This gives a physicalist an actual positive route: facts about lives can support criticism of desires and conventions rather than merely report what society approves. Its explanatory burden concerns the proposed natural-to-normative identification, impartiality, competing interests and the role of rights. Explaining why we believe a moral claim is not sufficient unless the account also warrants that claim or explains how it can be true. Equally, the mere fact that a moral term has a different meaning from a natural description does not by itself disprove property identity.

Rational or constructivist accounts could instead ground demands in defensible conditions of agency or reciprocal justification. Their burden is to show that the relevant conditions do not simply import the intended moral answer. Non-theistic non-natural realism could treat categorical normative reasons as irreducible. Its burden concerns their intelligibility, their connection to agents and epistemic access. These are substantive candidate alternatives in the matrix, **not independently inspected representative defenders**. Neither a divine nor a secular stopping point receives a free pass; the remaining original-literature gaps prevent a full four-family verdict.

## What suffering challenges

Rowe argues evidentially from suffering that appears preventable without the relevant loss. He grants uncertainty, distinguishes necessary from sufficient permission conditions, and recognizes independent theistic grounds as a possible counterweight. His hypothetical fawn is not a documented case. **CL-T15-006–008; A-T15-003.**

The target must specify knowledge, power, concern and feasible alternatives. A constrained designer, an indifferent origin or a foundation without personal intentions is not automatically subject to the same objection as an omniscient, omnipotent, perfectly good creator. Dropping attributes can change the argument’s force, but also changes the full hypothesis being assessed.

A freedom defense can propose that valuable free decisions cannot be guaranteed while remaining free in the relevant sense. A developmental defense can point to goods enabled by genuine difficulty and stable causal conditions. Neither proposal alone shows that every permitted degree or kind of harm is justified. The conceptual tests include suffering imposed on those who cannot choose, animal suffering, and conditions that prevent development altogether. There are no prevalence estimates in this task. Direct Plantinga and Hick originals remain uninspected; their broader traditions are not certified as defeated.

The project’s strongest pressure is comparative: what makes the harmful route permissible given the proposed agent’s options? Sometimes the defender proposes necessity; sometimes a route no worse than available alternatives. The critic must engage the stated criterion and the defender must count the victim’s costs. A merely conceivable reason may challenge a claim of logical impossibility without establishing the actual explanation or the best evidential fit.

## Restoration and the claims of victims

Adams requires good within each participant’s life and distinguishes mere outweighing from meaningful integration. Sutherland asks whether reassurance answers injustice and the claims of other victims. This exchange exposes a central distinction between what becomes of a harmed person and whether allowing the harm was justified. **CL-T15-009–012; A-T15-004.**

The strongest enduring-restoration account preserves the same victims, acknowledges the wrong, restores relationships and permits meaningful understanding without erasing the person’s agency. That could address losses a finite-life account cannot repair. But three questions remain separate: does later benefit compensate, does the event acquire a place within a worthwhile whole, and was its original permission justified? Neither compensation nor integration automatically settles the third.

This is not a reason to dismiss restorative goods. It is a reason to distinguish their jobs. Within an internal consistency argument, a theistic account may invoke its own specified goods; the critic cannot simply stipulate them away. In a comparison about actuality, however, those goods and the relevant continuity must be independently warranted. A successor with copied memories or a perfect record in another mind is not automatically the restored victim under T13’s identity conditions. A timeless foundation can ground temporal persons under T11’s M2; it need not itself improve through successive lessons. Adams’s later clarification/reply to Sutherland remains a coverage gap.

## Hiddenness: available relationship and reasons for delay

Schellenberg’s 2025 formulation requires capacity for conscious relationship and resistance that actually causes nonbelief. A merely unrecognized relation is not the relationship he specifies. His conclusion would not establish metaphysical naturalism or exclude every nonpersonal ultimate. **CL-T15-013–015; A-T15-005.**

Howard-Snyder’s 1996 paper responds to earlier Schellenberg. It is an original serious countercase, **not a direct reply to the 2025 formulation**. He grants inculpable nonbelief for argument and distinguishes wanting relationship now from having an all-things-considered reason to enable it now. His positive account includes already well-disposed seekers: a person might need to own a loving disposition or refine its depth and motivation. He does not claim that every nonbeliever secretly resists, nor demand perfect love before disclosure. His criterion allows a means no worse than alternatives; it does not require strict logical necessity. His epistemic aim is modest: challenge confidence that no suitable reason exists, rather than establish an actual divine reason. **CL-T15-023.**

His qualifications matter. The defense concerns delay, not permanent abandonment. He does not resolve cultural distribution. He accepts that an appropriately specified seeker can escape his objection while questioning our ability to identify such a person; note18 also limits his sketched reason where a seeker acknowledges defective love and strongly desires improvement. These concessions do not establish that actual seekers satisfy every condition, but they prevent an unrestricted defense. **CL-T15-024.**

Our comparison turns on a specific bridge: does a reason to delay a more fitting developed relationship also justify withholding the opportunity to enter one? Believing someone exists is different from loving or obeying them. Improving love may be possible within an available relationship; the defender may contest that comparison or our knowledge of it. A counterfactual disposition to respond poorly is not automatically resistance that causes present nonbelief. The later formulation sharpens what the earlier reply must address, but does not by wording alone defeat it. Exact earlier and later reply chains remain uninspected.

## Conditional assessment and limits

Unknown reasons may weaken an inference from an unrecognized purpose. Blanket skepticism may also weaken positive claims about recognizable divine goodness; local uncertainty need not. The relevant test is whether positive and negative judgments rely on the same inaccessible domain. **CL-T15-022; A-T15-006.**

H-G and H-A receive the most direct pressure and conditional support here. H-I is affected when the proposed purpose includes benevolent development; H-S and H-P matter to personal restoration. None of these arguments by itself establishes H-F, H-U, H-B or H-T. Shared value judgments and sources are dependencies, not repeated independent observations. Removing retrospective NDE claims leaves the conceptual distinctions intact but supplies no new evidence of survival.
