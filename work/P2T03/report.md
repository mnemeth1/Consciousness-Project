# P2T03 — Strawson’s experiential constitution and Goff’s combination objection

Submitted for independent review on 2026-09-12. This packet improves the original-source basis for the selected experiential-constitution variant, but establishes neither a constitutive solution nor its impossibility. Strawson’s actual reply recognizes the force of Goff’s argument against an exhaustively revealed, additive picture of experience. He answers with a different account of experiential subjects, their causal and constitutive roles, and the limits of acquaintance. He explicitly leaves the composition mechanism unknown. His argument for experiential fundamentals consequently remains conditional on disputed metaphysical premises; it is not an empirical demonstration of H-F.

## The positive position, with its assumptions visible

In the supplied 2008 chapter, Strawson assumes physicalism for argument and insists that experience belongs among concrete realities (pp. 53–57, §1). His use of physicalism is expressly broader than the thesis that the vocabulary of physics exhausts concrete nature. Thus his insistence that experience is physical is not acceptance of wholly nonexperiential role-realization. His descriptions of opponents as denying experience are disputed philosophical attributions, not findings that this packet adopts about all physicalists.

The developed argument adds several premises. Ultimate physical reality constitutes the rest; physics gives structural knowledge without exhausting intrinsic nature; experience is real; and wholly nonexperiential reality cannot constitute experience through radical emergence. The rejection of radical emergence requires more than a regular correlation: the base must supply what explains the dependent phenomenon in virtue of its nature. Strawson distinguishes metaphysical sufficiency from mere human intelligibility, but explicitly identifies the experiential/nonexperiential prohibition as an intuition he cannot further argue for (2008, pp. 60–70, especially 62–66 and 68; p. 74). His spatial and liquidity analogies express that intuition. They are not independent scientific tests of the metaphysical prohibition.

With these assumptions, at least some ultimates must involve experience: **micropsychism**. To reach panpsychism he adds a homogeneity assumption, rejecting fundamental heterogeneity as implausible (2008, pp. 70–71). The 2006 reply is especially explicit that no-radical-emergence and homogeneity are additional premises (p. 232 n. 101). Certainty that an experience occurs does not itself establish either premise. A framework might contain basic experiential property types whose instances depend on organization; that distinction, already accepted in P2T01, must not be erased by calling everything irreducible fundamental.

The constitution claim is substantial, even though incomplete. Macroexperience is to consist in organized experiential ultimates whose qualities can differ greatly from the resulting macroexperience. The 2008 chapter explicitly leaves subject combination and mental causation to be explained and says that this general framework supplies no scientific test (pp. 72–73, including nn. 49–51). It does not claim that stones and tables are subjects (p. 72).

The reply develops a thin subject—an experiencer that cannot exist without its current experience—and then proposes identity of subject, experience and experiential being. Thinness alone does not entail momentariness or immortality (2006 reply, pp. 189–193). His stronger identity thesis and rejection of a robust object/property split are further metaphysical commitments, not logical consequences of the definition of experience (pp. 193–198). In the preferred smallist pure-panpsychist model, ultimates are subjects/experiencings; some pluralities constitute numerically distinct larger subjects, others do not (pp. 246–249). No criterion is supplied for which pluralities succeed. Nor does calling the subject-experience unit a sesmet supply such a criterion.

## Goff’s strongest objection and Strawson’s actual reply

Goff first presses **emergence parity**: adding a billion small experiencing subjects to a brain does not by itself explain a further, distinct subject. If the motivation is rejection of unexplained emergence, why tolerate this new emergence? His James passage illustrates the issue, but the argument is Goff’s own developed challenge, not an independently inspected James original (2006, pp. 53–54, 59–60).

He then anticipates an ignorance reply. If we can be ignorant of the constitution of our own experience, perhaps its apparent separation from ordinary physical or functional properties also reflects an epistemic limitation. Goff argues that Strawson needs transparent knowledge of experiential nature to rule out the latter possibility, and cannot withdraw that transparency only when combination becomes difficult (pp. 54–57; p. 59 n. 2 continuing onto p. 60). His citation of Strawson 2003 actually contains the qualification that acquaintance reveals reality in certain respects (p. 55). Whether this weaker acquaintance nevertheless commits Strawson to the stronger transparency is disputed; the original 2003 work was not separately inspected.

Goff strengthens the challenge with phenomenal parts and wholes. If a large pain is wholly constituted by several small pains, and experiential being consists exhaustively in how it feels, then the whole should contain how those small pains feel. Yet the stipulated severe pain does not feel like many slight pains. Mere addition seems unable to produce a new experiential quality (pp. 57–59). The example is a philosophical stipulation, not a psychophysical measurement: no numerical pain scale or observed summation follows from it.

Strawson’s reply does not simply ignore this. It agrees that Goff’s argument is devastating against one natural full-revelation, smallist interpretation (p. 255). It distinguishes first-order acquaintance in having an experience from reflective introspection and, separately, partial access from exhaustive access to experiential nature (pp. 250–253). It allows that the phenomenal character of *my* experience can be fully present to me in one sense while the complete constituting event involves other numerically distinct subjects whose perspectives I do not share (pp. 253–256). That qualified concession is important: he does not deny every possible meaning of full revelation.

The positive elaboration treats experience as active substance, not passive content. Its causal and constitutive relations are aspects of the same experiential reality; a constituent’s contribution need not transfer its first-person character into another subject’s field of acquaintance (pp. 256–262). Strawson does not introduce absolutely nonexperiential substance to make this work. His relational language describes how one experiential reality functions for another, while preserving his pure-panpsychist supposition (p. 261).

This comes with a real cost. Strawson acknowledges that his schematic argument on pp. 258–259 goes wrong at premise 7, about things being wholly closed to one another. It must not be reproduced as a sound proof. He proposes that causal powers belong to the nature of experiential things, and at p. 262 expressly relinquishes his earlier characterization of experience as qualitative character considered independently of everything else. That is a revision/concession documented by this reply; it is not a independently verified comparison with all earlier works. He also acknowledges that his only argument for macroexperience emerging from microexperience is transcendental and assumes smallism, while conceding that he has no account of how it happens (p. 262). At p. 266 he recognizes the formal reversibility of the argument from antecedent commitments.

The bounded assessment therefore has two parts. **Goff has not demonstrated impossibility independently of the contested transparency and experiential-parthood premises. Strawson has not provided a positive law or model establishing composition.** Partial acquaintance can undermine Goff’s route from an absent experienced part to an impossible constituent. But that defense leaves the central burden of showing how distinct subjects yield the actual unified macroexperience, and why the standard is less demanding than for the emergentist rival. The distinction between not understanding and contradiction is legitimate; applying it asymmetrically without a further argument is not a comparative victory.

Both sides owe causal and individuation explanations under their own premises. An irreducible bearer, on either account, does not establish postmortem persistence.

## Components, remaining duties and stopping point

**H-F** receives a conditional argument for experiential ultimates, not an actuality upgrade from irreducibility or terminology. **H-U** is not entailed by all ultimates experiencing: a universe-wide subject is expressly left optional in the reply (p. 260). **H-B** is not established; Strawson accepts an experiential identity interpretation of relevant brain activity (2008 p. 56; reply pp. 267, 275–276). **H-S** and **H-P** receive no evidence of continued or preserved persons. **H-T** receives no timeless bearer: the supplied chapter even treats experience as requiring time (p. 58 n. 16), a philosophical premise rather than a proof about cosmology. **H-I**, **H-G** and **H-A** do not follow from experiential nature or causation; the reply expressly distinguishes activity from intentional agency (p. 257 n. 155). IR and the explanation-of-existence target remain separate from H-F. All twelve GATE-T16 conclusions remain withheld.
