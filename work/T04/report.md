## Strongest support, challenge and reply

**Support available here:** direct experiential testimony, the memoir excerpt's rescue linkage, and S009's more specific timing and reported witness/medical-check claims. The personal reply in S009 (44:11–50:16) appeals to medication history, distinctive stable memory, elapsed rescue time and neurological outcome. These are her reasons, not independent demonstrations. If inaccessible information were independently documented and timed to excluded physiological conditions, it could discriminate against a specified dependence model. That conditional possibility motivates further investigation; it does not make the present account a verified instance.

**Author's methodological challenge:** this packet cannot synchronize experience, information acquisition, reporting and physiology. The feeling of continuity does not alone distinguish brain-independent experience from an experience constructed or remembered around loss/recovery of responsiveness. Ordinary hearing, intermittently restored awareness, later discussion, reconstruction and altered time perception are possibilities to test, not demonstrated explanations of this case. The information-exposure history is presently unknown.

**Strongest defensible reply, authored for this audit:** retrospective testimony remains evidence even when charts are unavailable; precise independently documented correlations would matter, and naming a physiological possibility does not explain a particular event. S004 supplies an affirmative separable-mind argument, but its Neal passage does not independently close the timing and provenance gaps. Neither narrative confidence nor commercial/advocacy affiliation decides accuracy or sincerity.

**Published criticism/reply is partially recovered.** S008 is Mays and Mays's 2017 rejoinder, reached through their author bibliography. They acknowledge that the debate remains unsettled, criticize additional speculative explanations, and defend a common proximate cause. Their conclusion explicitly retracts the earlier attribution that Fischer and Mitchell-Yellin called NDEs hallucinations. This correction matters when representing the critics fairly. [Rejoinder, pp. 110–113 and 119; DOI 10.17514/JNDS-2017-36-2-p110-120](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329233248_Near-Death_Experiences_A_Critique_of_the_Fischer_and_Mitchell-Yellin_Physicalist_Interpretation). The wrapper title names the earlier critique, but its visible extracted text is the rejoinder. The critic's separate PDF link failed (404; corrected path also failed). Its position is therefore only indirectly represented here. No Neal-specific discussion was located in the extracted rejoinder. This is useful general argument context, not a completed Neal critic/reply audit.
