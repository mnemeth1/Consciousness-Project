# T10 — Existence, intelligibility and Lennox arguments

Author submission under protocol v2; cutoff 2026-09-11. Independent R10 review is required. This is a focused argument audit, not a completed cosmology review or a numerical assessment of theism.

## Finding

These arguments can have positive explanatory force without being deductive proofs. Their force depends on different premises: a demand for an explanation of contingent reality, an expectation that rational agency would produce discoverable order, or a comparative expectation of embodied life under specified hypotheses. The strongest versions do more than point to gaps in science. They attempt to explain why there is an ordered, knowable, life-permitting reality at all.

The main disputes concern whether those expectations are independently justified and whether impersonal alternatives perform worse on the same terms. A necessary foundation is not yet a conscious foundation; a designer is not yet a universal subject; and neither establishes personal survival. This packet leaves all nine project components unresolved, while recording conditional support for conscious-foundation and intention proposals. “Unresolved” does not mean disproved, equally probable, or devoid of evidence.

Lennox distinguishes an agent from the mechanism through which an artifact works, then advances a further positive claim: rationally accessible order and sophisticated laws favor a creator. He also challenges naturalism’s ability to justify reason and describes physical laws as regularities. **CL-T10-001–003.** His Newton/Ford illustration supports explanatory compatibility; the comparative conclusion requires additional premises. The article’s historical and quoted-author claims have not been independently corroborated here.

Leibniz’s PSR applies broadly to facts and truths. He argues that an indefinitely extended contingent sequence still needs an ultimate reason outside that sequence, identifies a necessary substance, and adds separate claims about unity, perfection, understanding and choice of the best. **CL-T10-004–006.** Those later steps must remain visible; necessity alone does not yield the richer conclusion.

In Hume’s dialogue, Cleanthes challenges Demea’s necessity argument through conceivability, a possible necessary material universe, and explanation of the parts. Philo challenges design analogies and the extra explanatory gain of a mental plan; Cleanthes replies that an explanation need not itself have a fully explained cause. Part XII complicates any simple identification of Philo with wholesale rejection of every analogy to intelligence. **CL-T10-007–008.** These are opposed arguments, not contradictory records of what was said.

Collins explicitly claims comparative support rather than proof or an all-things-considered verdict. His favorable expectation appeals to goodness and the value of conscious life. He uses epistemic probability, answers anthropic dismissal by considering our existence as evidence, and argues that a designer’s unexplained complexity need not cancel a favorable likelihood comparison. **CL-T10-009–012.** These replies do not supply the missing numerical distributions. His physical examples, entropy claims and assessments of cosmological models are not adopted as established physics.

Weisberg distinguishes learning that life exists from later learning that life requires stringent conditions. His argument uses divine intent plus matched indifference distributions; under them, life screens off the additional stringency evidence. He acknowledges that life itself may initially favor design and examines difficulties with uniformity and parameterization. **CL-T10-013–015.** His result is conditional on those assumptions, not a theorem against every design hypothesis.

## Argument maps and the strongest exchanges

The following are our reconstructions, with author provenance above. The full premise and bridge records are in argument_extraction.json and proposed_records.json.

### A-T10-001: mechanism and agency

An account of how a process works can coexist with an agent’s reasons for arranging it. Therefore a successful mechanism, by itself, does not exclude agency. This is a valid compatibility point when the two explanations concern different relations and make no conflicting causal claims.

The objection is that familiar human designers are independently observed, while a cosmic designer is what is being inferred. The reply is that lack of direct observation does not invalidate every inference to an unobserved cause. The remaining requirement is a positive discriminatory reason to infer this cause. If a proposed intervention contradicts a specific empirically supported closure claim, compatibility must be assessed for that model; it cannot be established merely by calling the explanations different levels. **CL-T10-016.**

### A-T10-002: intelligibility as positive evidence

Assume a rational creator has reason to enable finite inquirers to discover stable, general mathematical order, and that this outcome is less expected under a specified impersonal alternative. If those assumptions are independently motivated, intelligibility favors that creator proposal. This is an abductive comparison, stronger than mere compatibility.

The strongest objection is that the contrast class and agent preferences are underspecified. Impersonal structured reality might itself explain regularity; a proposed selection-and-learning account could explain some cognitive fit. Merely originating through nonintentional processes does not logically make a belief false or unjustified. Conversely, merely invoking adaptation does not yet explain reliable abstract mathematics or its successful reach. This packet does not adjudicate the empirical adequacy of an evolutionary account.

The strongest reply is that local learning and observer selection may leave the broad reach of discoverable order unexplained, and one rational source might unify that order with reason’s capacity to grasp it. That could be an explanatory advantage if it yields expectations independent of the observed successes. It weakens if the agent’s aims are chosen only after observing the world or if an independently motivated impersonal account predicts the same pattern. **CL-T10-016,020.**

A mathematical description is not, just as a description, a physical producer. But a realist may take mathematically represented laws to describe actual structure, powers or constraints. That ontology is not refuted by noting that symbols do not act. Conversely, a successful mathematical model does not by itself explain why its structure is instantiated. Both sides owe an explicit account of what “explanation” and “law” mean.

### A-T10-003: contingent reality and a necessary ground

1. There is contingent concrete reality, and its obtaining as a totality is a legitimate contingent explanandum.
2. Every such contingent fact has an adequate explanation: the relevant restricted PSR.
3. A complete explanation of that totality cannot be wholly internal, circular, or merely another contingent item in the same totality.
4. Explaining concrete reality ultimately requires a concrete ground; abstract truths alone are insufficient.
5. Therefore the ultimate explanatory basis includes necessary concrete reality.

This conditional deduction makes the disputed assumptions explicit. It does not infer the contingency of a whole simply because its parts are contingent. Nor does it assume that an infinite sequence must have a first temporal member. Its proposed priority is explanatory, not necessarily chronological. An external explanation means external to the contingent explanandum, not spatially outside the universe. **CL-T10-017.**

The strongest countercase accepts local explanations but denies a further explanation of the totality, or proposes a necessary impersonal basis. A brute fact need not be something that caused itself or came from nothing: it is a proposed explanatory stopping point. The PSR defender replies that local dependency leaves unanswered why this entire arrangement obtains and that selectively exempting the whole needs justification. This dispute concerns PSR scope and explanatory sufficiency; neither side wins by calling its stopping point a fact or a foundation.

Even granting the conclusion, reaching mind needs a further premise: for example, that alternatives are represented and selected for reasons, and that only an intellect can perform the required selection. Reaching one mind, goodness and moral authority needs still more. Leibniz supplies additional metaphysical commitments, not a cost-free renaming of necessary reality as God.

A further test concerns contingency preservation. If the necessary ground plus an equally necessary, necessitating explanatory relation forces every outcome, the claimed contingency disappears. A defender may propose nonnecessitating rational choice, but must explain why its reasons are sufficient without requiring that exact outcome. An impersonal probabilistic proposal faces a parallel question about explanation of occurrence versus distribution. This is our sensitivity challenge, not an attributed contradiction in the inspected text.

### A-T10-004: fine-tuning as comparative evidence

Let E specify the relevant life-permitting observation, D an independently specified life-directed design hypothesis, N a specified impersonal single-universe hypothesis, and B their common background. If E is appreciably more expected given D and B than given N and B, E comparatively favors D. This can be a meaningful qualitative argument even if precise numbers are unavailable. It does not automatically compare D against all impersonal alternatives.

For positive priors, positive likelihood denominators and defined conditional probabilities, our standard mathematical reconstruction is:

\[
\frac{P(D\mid E,B)}{P(N\mid E,B)}=
\frac{P(E\mid D,B)}{P(E\mid N,B)}
\frac{P(D\mid B)}{P(N\mid B)}.
\]

This is not a quotation of Collins’s displayed formula. His excerpt’s p.17 endnote 2 visually prints adjacent likelihood terms without the needed division; the text’s verbal comparison remains clear. The missing appendix and the excerpt’s production history limit diagnosis of that error. No numerical likelihood ratio or posterior is identified here. **CL-T10-018.**

A parameter-sensitivity calculation alone is not a probability distribution. Required choices include the possibility space, parameterization, measure, correlations, life criterion, background evidence and agent preferences. A brute-fact hypothesis does not automatically sample uniformly. “Chance” needs a model. Epistemic uncertainty about a necessary truth differs from physical randomness; proving an impersonal necessity could change the epistemic comparison.

The strongest positive case grants a robust, independently investigated constraint on embodied life and independently motivated reasons for a creator to value such life. If plausible alternatives make that outcome substantially less expected, design can gain support without supplying a complete explanation of the designer. The strongest objection attacks those comparative expectations, rather than simply announcing that only one universe is observed. A designer able to choose entirely different laws, create nonembodied life or refrain from creation needs a specified preference account.

Observer selection must be modeled symmetrically. That observers can only observe compatible conditions does not, by itself, prove that their existence supplies no evidence. Conversely, one cannot reuse life first as evidence and then again as new background-independent evidence of the same fact. Our observation that life exists, our discovery of its sensitivity to parameters, and the process that selected the observation are distinct items.

### A-T10-005: the stringency challenge

Weisberg’s model addresses the incremental contribution of stringent laws once life is known. If design and nondesign induce the same distribution over life-permitting possibilities after conditioning on life, then learning stringency makes no further difference. The equality follows from the matching assumptions. It does not follow from ignorance alone.

The strongest reply denies that a designer and an impersonal process deserve the same measure: values such as elegant laws, discoverability or embodied development might motivate different preferences. That is a possible substantive response, but requires independent support. A rival cannot simply stipulate its preferred distribution; equally, a critic cannot impose uniformity on every design account. The challenge weakens when a defensible asymmetric measure is supplied. The objection remains significant for an argument that relies on indifference for one side while rejecting its comparable use for the other. **CL-T10-013–015,018.**

## Alternatives, dependence and consequences

| Alternative | Strongest relevant role | Remaining obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Brute contingent reality | Stops explanation without an added agent or global PSR | Explain why global explanatory stopping is acceptable; it supplies no probability measure by itself. |
| Necessary impersonal foundation | Can satisfy a demand for a necessary basis without mentality | Establish the claimed necessity and how concrete contingent outcomes depend on it. |
| Deeper physical constraints | Could reduce apparently free parameters and predict order | Derive and test the constraints; unexplained laws do not automatically become evidence for design. |
| Multiverse with observer selection | Can change expectations when many differently configured regions arise | Supply a generator, measure, selection rule and relevant empirical motivation; multiplicity alone is insufficient. |
| Rational agency | Can unify existence, intelligibility and life-directed explanation | Defend agency, aims and explanatory relation without fitting every outcome after the fact. |

The multiverse row is a model family for comparison, not an assertion that a contemporary cosmological mechanism has been verified. A generator might leave a further explanatory question, but explaining one level can still be an advance. The same standard applies to design.

The arguments share premises about order, explanatory adequacy, rationality and agent preferences. Their conclusions must not be multiplied as independent evidence. Fine-tuning support based on an assumed good creator cannot subsequently be counted as an independent derivation of goodness. **CL-T10-019–020.**
