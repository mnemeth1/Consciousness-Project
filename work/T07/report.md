# T07 — Prospective cardiac-arrest experience studies

**Submitted for independent R07 review, 12 September 2026.** This bounded audit covers the AWARE I and AWARE II originals, one early Dutch prospective study, and a direct methodological critique. It establishes what those sources report and where their inference depends on missing measurements. It does not establish experience independent of the brain, survival after irreversible death, or a specific ordinary explanation of every recollection.

The AWARE II full journal PDF arrived from the user after web retrieval had failed and the query budget had been exhausted. Its methods, results, tables, figures and references now support the extraction. The earlier abstract-only assessment is preserved in `versions/pre_user_pdf`; it is superseded, not an accepted finding. The teaching excerpt and supplied original are two access records of the same article. The supplied PDF does not contain the supplementary files.

## AWARE II: the decisive distinctions

**Attrition is material.** Sustained ROSC means at least 20 minutes. Of 213 with sustained ROSC, 160 died in hospital. Of 53 discharged, 44 had GOS 4–5, eight GOS 3 and one GOS 2. Twenty-five were not interviewed: four subject/family refusals, seven labelled disabled, three other, eleven reason not reported. Interviews required AMTS >6 and occurred approximately 2–4 weeks after arrest. Thus 11/28 and 6/28 describe interviewed survivors, not all people undergoing cardiac arrest. Initial screening, separate approach/eligibility totals and exact repeat-event handling are not fully reconciled in the inspected main text. (S-T07-007 pp2,6–7; CL-T07-011–012.)

**Deployment is not confirmed exposure.** The tablet displayed one of ten images one minute after activation; headphones delivered three fruit names starting five minutes after activation, repeated over five minutes. The reported 365/567 equipment deployments do not establish that each stimulus was delivered before ROSC or termination, functioned correctly, or reached someone subsequently interviewed. Those intersections remain null in the extraction. The 28-person interview group is not silently converted to 28 valid exposed trials. (S-T07-007 pp3,7; CL-T07-013.)

**Explicit and implicit results differ.** The abstract's auditory-identification wording loses an important distinction preserved in the body: none explicitly recalled the experimental image or sounds. None selected the displayed image in the implicit task, while one chose the three fruit names. The paper prints 1/28 as 3.5%; this is retained as reported, not a project estimate. The implicit choice does not establish conscious hearing or anomalous acquisition. Without a defensible response probability model, no significance calculation is warranted. Conversely, unknown exposure cannot be treated as demonstrated recognition failure. (S-T07-007 p7; CL-T07-014.)

**EEG people, images and reported contents are separate.** There were 851 captured images from 85 subjects, of which 466 images from 53 subjects were interpretable; 49 of those subjects also had cerebral rSO2. Four-lead frontotemporal EEG was captured as screenshots during 3–5-second pulse-check pauses, reducing compression artifact but leaving limited spatial and temporal sampling. Electrical interference, electrode malfunction and movement caused losses. Median staff arrival was four minutes and setup took approximately five minutes; adding these summaries does not establish a nine-minute start time for any individual. (S-T07-007 pp3–4,7; CL-T07-015.)

Fig. 2 explicitly says that only two of the 28 interviewees had EEG data and neither was among those with explicit cognitive recall. The exact wording does not prove absence of every kind of experience or physiological association. It does prevent linking the reported RED contents to a particular late EEG rhythm from the aggregate graph. Authors report near-normal/physiological activity up to 35–60 minutes into CPR and acknowledge that alpha coma could not be distinguished from physiological alpha. A low rSO2 mean is not evidence of zero perfusion or absent brain activity. (S-T07-007 p7 Fig. 2 footnote, p9; CL-T07-016–017.)

The actual EEG figure is Fig. 1 on p4, although results refer to Fig. 2 A/B/C. Its later points and >60-minute bin extend beyond the main-text timing summary. This audit preserves that mismatch and does not reconstruct an exact maximum from the plot. The categories' aggregate reported EEG percentages are not turned into exhaustive person-level classes.

## Corroboration, uncertainty and dependence

AWARE I reports chart corroboration for one account and infers approximately three minutes from the AED sequence. Its body and footnote use different upper/lower timing formulations; there is no directly synchronized neural recording. AWARE II reports chart verification of a shock recollection, with no further information supplied for independent corroboration, and acknowledges unmeasured prior knowledge of resuscitation. This project did not inspect clinical charts. Author-reported corroboration supports some correspondence between a recollection and a medical event; it does not by itself establish when experience formed or how information was acquired. (S-T07-001 pp4–5, Table 2/footnote 2; S-T07-007 pp6,9; CL-T07-004,019.)

AWARE I's printed interview counts are inconsistent: 52 in-hospital plus 90 later interviews sum to 142, while the main text says 140; a Table 1 footnote also uses 142. AWARE II's introduction gives 143 for the earlier study, which does not resolve the original discrepancy. The Dutch paper prints 248 interviews within five days as 74%, although the overall sample is 344; the applicable denominator or correction is unresolved. These are reporting/version issues, not grounds for inventing replacement values. The Dutch erratum is listed at Lancet 2002;359(9313):1254 in the [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11755611/), but its content was not obtained. (CL-T07-001,005,010.)

The Dutch two-year follow-up initially interviewed 37 previous experiencers and 37 selected controls, then reclassified them to 35/39; eight-year retention was 23/15. These are follow-up observations of the same cohort, not replications. AWARE II p9 footnote 3 likewise identifies additional manuscripts analyzing the same EEG/rSO2 datasets. Exact companion-publication overlap has not been checked. Similar sites or authors alone do not establish duplicate participants, and different publication dates do not establish disjoint samples. AWARE I's main dates precede AWARE II's feasibility phase, but person-level independence across studies was not verified. (CL-T07-006,020.)

## Strongest argument and countercase

The strongest conditional argument for H-B starts with detailed reports and limited medical-event corroboration. It would become discriminating evidence if content were independently timed to an interval excluding the neural processes required under a specified dependence model, with ordinary information routes adequately controlled. Neither aggregate EEG nor missing neural measurement supplies those premises. The concealed-target opportunity gaps are especially consequential. This argument remains conditional. (A-T07-001.)

The strongest countercase is that timing, memory reconstruction, selection, ordinary knowledge and surviving or returning neural activity remain available explanations. The [Martial and colleagues author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Charlotte-Martial-2/publication/377188274_Exploring_awareness_in_cardiac_arrest_studies_Methodological_challenges/links/65991b3d3c472d2e8eb71a64/Exploring-awareness-in-cardiac-arrest-studies-Methodological-challenges.pdf) directly challenges EEG interpretation, missingness, terminology, fruit-cue interpretation and exclusion of pre-arrest/recovery timing (S-T07-003 p2 lines21–58). Those possibilities do not demonstrate a mechanism for each account. AWARE II's own discussion acknowledges alpha ambiguity, prior-knowledge and recall limitations, and that association does not establish causation. Its speculative disinhibition or alternative ontological interpretation is an author proposal, not a measured mechanism. The direct later author reply, DOI 10.1016/j.resuscitation.2023.110080, was not accessible; the original discussion is not presented as that reply. (A-T07-002.)
