# T12 — Emergence, panpsychism, dualism and idealism

Author submission, 12 September 2026. Protocol v2; independent R12 review required. This is a bounded comparison of selected models, not a literature-wide verdict.

**The main result is a comparison of explanatory commitments.** Physical identity seeks one property under different modes of access; property dualism introduces experiential properties and linking laws; constitutive panpsychism seeks a micro-to-macro experiential relation; universal idealism seeks a whole-to-local relation. Each supplies a positive explanatory proposal and each owes a different missing account. An unresolved burden neither refutes a family nor gives all families equal support. No numerical metaphysical posterior is identified.

## Physical identity and emergence — A-T12-001

Papineau argues that identities can be evidenced without a priori derivation and that a genuine identity does not leave two items needing another bridge. He attributes the felt gap to resistance to identity, while leaving its possible psychological explanations open to empirical inquiry. These are his philosophical claims, not this project’s certification that science has established a particular mind–brain identity. **CL-T12-001–003.**

Our reconstruction gives this response real force: two ways of thinking need not refer to two properties. However, an identity that is merely stipulated has not earned the explanatory benefits of one independently warranted. The strongest objection asks whether the proposed identification retains the phenomenal target or only its functional neighborhood. The strongest reply rejects the demand that understanding every true identity must deliver another first-person mode of access. Resolving that dispute needs a defensible account of reference, justification and the intended explanatory achievement; neither a neural correlation nor an intuition alone settles it.

Under T01/T03, weak emergence is complete physical grounding without additional basic experiential laws. Strong emergence introduces an irreducible property or law, even if its appearance depends on complex physical organization. “More complexity” is therefore not a self-contained explanation: specify what changes, why this organization realizes this experience, and which systems have a unified subject. Computational unpredictability is an epistemic limitation, not by itself ontological novelty. **CL-T12-004.**

## Property dualism and causation — A-T12-003

Chalmers proposes basic experience and supplementary psychophysical principles, while acknowledging that a basic experiential primitive does not explain why experience exists at all. The proposal can respect physical laws without identifying experience with their usual properties. **CL-T12-005–006.**

Our strongest positive reconstruction is a lawful theory of instances rather than a special exception for every experience. The same stopping-point standard must apply to physical and experiential primitives. Its cost lies in independently constraining the mapping, not simply in using the word “dualism.”

The exclusion challenge is conditional. Suppose physical effects already have sufficient physical causes, experience also causally contributes, and systematic independent overdetermination is rejected. A distinct additional mental cause then requires explanation. This package is stronger than energy conservation alone. Interaction laws could in principle challenge completeness or modify causal structure, but naming an immaterial cause does not supply such laws. Property dualism does not itself entail detachable substances or human survival. **CL-T12-008.**

Papineau additionally asks how epiphenomenal experience could explain physical beliefs in dualism. **CL-T12-007.** A strong proposed reply distinguishes physical report production from justification: psychophysical laws might secure reliable tracking, and acquaintance might ground knowledge without an extra causal push on the brain. This is our reconstruction, not a newly inspected original epiphenomenalist defense. It still owes an account of truth and justification rather than merely matching reports. Allowing mental interaction avoids that particular absence of influence but reopens causal compatibility. No option is dismissed through a conservation-law slogan.

## Constitutive panpsychism — A-T12-002

Chalmers’s constructive account combines experiential realizers of physical roles with constitution of ordinary experience. His criticism separates subject summing, qualitative palette, and structural organization; his discussion of phenomenal bonding asks what relation could yield appropriately bounded subjects. He does not establish either a complete solution or impossibility. **CL-T12-009–011.**

Our positive reconstruction is that experiential properties might occupy causal roles rather than enter as additional outside causes. This can preserve the insight behind both phenomenal irreducibility and causal integration. But an unknown intrinsic nature is not thereby experiential, and one microscopic subject’s existence does not by definition yield a larger subject. All allowed relations must be included in the proposed base before testing its sufficiency.

The subject challenge concerns a bearer of a unified perspective; the quality challenge concerns the contents’ character; the structure challenge concerns their organization. Solving an information-processing binding problem does not automatically solve all three. A bonding relation is a substantive candidate if it specifies when unity obtains. A definition that simply labels whatever produces our subject “bonding” supplies no independent criterion.

The modal burden must be symmetrical. If conceivability is used against physicalism, its use against experiential constitution deserves the same scrutiny; conversely, rejecting that bridge against panpsychism cannot silently retain it against physicalism. A constitutive response may reject the critic’s account of the complete base or the assumed primitiveness of subjects. A strong-emergence response changes the model and inherits additional law/causation costs.

Russellian experiential bases are not T03’s wholly nonexperiential physicalism under another name. Proto-experiential bases are also not automatically experiential under H-F. Basic experience everywhere does not imply a single subject for every aggregate, and a cosmic subject is an additional thesis. **CL-T12-012.**

## Universal and plural idealism — A-T12-004–005

Kastrup proposes cosmic consciousness with finite dissociated perspectives, living organisms as their appearances, and common surrounding mentation as the basis of a shared world. His dissociation model distinguishes cognitive access from causal impingement; private perspectives need not be causally isolated. **CL-T12-013–014.**

The strongest positive reconstruction starts from a known experiential reality and seeks one law-governed explanation of subjectivity and public regularities. It does not say each person can wish the world into any form. But access through experience alone cannot demonstrate that everything is constituted by experience. Nor does one substance automatically mean fewer independently adjustable assumptions: dynamics, perspective boundaries, manifestation and interaction rules must be counted too. **CL-T12-018.**

Chalmers distinguishes generic cosmopsychism from the stronger mental-grounding thesis and considers cognitive fragmentation a candidate response to constitution, while retaining serious individuation and introspective costs. **CL-T12-016–017.** This is a related original philosophical challenge, not a verified2018 critic/reply exchange.

Our assessment distinguishes two models. If finite subjects are numerically identical to the cosmic subject, ordinary transitivity of identity puts pressure on the claim that they are mutually distinct. Relativizing cognitive access to modes is a candidate reply, but a mode’s limited access does not by itself settle whose experience it is. If finite subjects are distinct and grounded in the whole, a sufficient subject-generating relation is still needed. Describing regions of one experience is not yet deriving new bearers. Plural idealism avoids a compulsory cosmic subject but owes common-law coordination; micro-idealist versions can retain the combination problem.

Kastrup’s clinical, plant-consciousness, quantum-measurement and free-energy premises were not independently inspected. They remain his argumentative supports, not accepted empirical findings. In particular, a clinical dissociation analogy does not demonstrate universe-scale subject formation; a statistical or cognitive boundary is not automatically a phenomenal boundary. No graph topology, clinical diagnosis or quantum formalism establishes that inference here. **CL-T12-015.**

## Common constraints and discriminators

The reviewed T09 dependence evidence must constrain all models: an adequate account should specify why particular manipulations produce particular experiential changes, preserving report and measurement limits. Physical realization, psychophysical mapping, experiential constitution and mental appearance can each propose such a relation. Compatibility alone is less than predictive success, and importing the same physical predictions into another ontology is not an independent exclusive success. **CL-T12-019.**

Useful next tests concern specified models: a fixed realization rule, a psychophysical threshold, a composition boundary, or an interaction law can rule out outcomes in advance. Such rules should face the same held-out intervention and cross-system constraints. An independently verified outcome excluded by one specified model could weaken it without proving an entire rival family. Family labels that allow every mapping supply little discrimination. These are research designs, not experiments performed in T12.

[component_assessment.json](component_assessment.json) separately records H-F/U/B/S/T/P/I/G/A with IDs, assumptions, dependence, sensitivity and questions. All remain unresolved here, for different reasons. Granting a basic or cosmic consciousness does not establish continued human subjectivity. A dynamic experiential ground is not strictly timeless merely because its underlying kind is stable; T11’s bearer-specific succession criterion remains binding. Persistence of a ground does not ensure preservation of a finite life. Goodness and moral authority require separate normative arguments; physicalism does not logically imply nihilism. **CL-T12-020.**
