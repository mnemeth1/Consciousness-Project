# T13 — Personal identity, endurance and temporal reports

Author submission, 12 September 2026. Protocol v 2. Independent R13 review required; no author acceptance.

**The identity relation and the evidence that it holds are different questions.** A psychological account, an organism account and an irreducible-subject account give distinct conditions for survival. None makes a remembered experience, a faithful copy, or a universal mind's knowledge of a life automatically the original person's continued subjectivity. Conversely, an enlarged first-person history is a live conditional proposal when it specifies actual subjectivity beyond the bodily-life boundary and a defensible relation unifying the person across it.

## What would make the same person persist?

Locke separates person, human animal and substance. His positive proposal centers on consciousness that appropriates past acts, allowing changes in matter. He explicitly addresses sleep, forgetfulness and the prince/cobbler example; this is not a simplistic requirement that every memory always be present. Nevertheless, complete disconnection creates difficult consequences for the person/man distinction. **CL-T13-001–002; A-T13-001.**

Olson's positive case starts with the embodied thinker: if the organism thinks and there is one relevant thinker, that thinker is the person. It avoids multiplying coincident subjects and accommodates psychological change. The most serious competing intuition follows a transplanted cerebrum. His reply distinguishes recognition and concern from what constitutes identity. Importantly, animalism alone does not settle every question about an animal's persistence. **CL-T13-003–004; A-T13-002.**

Swinburne's strongest contribution is a definite first-person bearer through qualitative change. His hypothetical split leaves equally supported successor descriptions; he argues for an immaterial identity-bearing part. The crucial challenge is the move from unknowable identity to further identity facts and then to a soul. A charitable defense must justify those steps. Even a simple soul is not thereby immortal or always experiencing. **CL-T13-005–006; A-T13-003.**

The following are project-authored tests, not unseen historical attributions. Genuine memory defined as memory of one's own experiences can presuppose identity. A repaired criterion needs independently specified causal links; overlapping psychological continuity can survive loss of direct memories, but branching still matters. If A and B are distinct successors, both cannot be strictly identical to their one predecessor. Both might preserve valued relationships or warrant prudential concern under a different relation. Neither concern nor narrative/social recognition decides numerical identity. **CL-T13-007–009; identity_matrix.json.**

An interruption of awareness is therefore not by itself personal extinction. Nor does survival of sleep establish survival of irreversible death: the relevant organism, causal continuity or bearer may persist in one situation and fail in the other. No absent response or missing recollection is treated as a measurement of total absence of experience.

## Enduring or enlarged histories

| Model | What must be supplied |
|---|---|
| M1: temporal personal continuation | A subject actually experiences after irreversible bodily death, with a defended identity relation and substrate/ground. Timelessness is unnecessary. |
| M2: timeless foundation, temporal persons | A grounding and individuation relation; persons can learn successively while the foundation does not acquire states. Their histories must actually extend beyond bodily life for H-S. |
| M3: atemporal personal inclusion | A finite history alone preserves embodied subjectivity as part of the history. An enlarged history can qualify as survival if it includes actual first-person subjectivity beyond the bodily-life boundary and explains its ownership by the same person. |
| M4: preservation only | Identify retained information, represented relationships, memories or value. Fidelity and the consciousness of a record holder alone do not preserve the original subject. |

The strongest enlarged-history proposal is concrete first-person reality grounded as a whole, with ordered content that need not imply change in the foundation. It need not be dismissed because “beyond” is relational rather than a later foundational instant. But it owes both an actual extension and a noncircular ownership relation. Under strict organism criteria, a disembodied extension needs an additional same-organism account; under psychological criteria, it needs continuity and a branching rule; under a simple-subject criterion, it needs the same bearer's persistence and activity. The proposal's compatibility with some criteria does not establish those criteria or the proposal. **CL-T13-016–018; A-T13-004.**

## Temporal reports and the comparison state

Greyson 1990's highest time response combines apparent simultaneity, stopped time and loss of time meaning. The modified wording differs from the earlier instrument. Rounded group percentages in the extraction are not exact category counts and cannot yield a prevalence of literal timelessness. The same existing sample supplies both the original project result and this reinspection. **CL-T13-010; C-T08-008.**

Timmermann's study used 13 previously psychedelic-experienced volunteers, placebo first and DMT one week later, with single blinding and varying doses. All 13 reached the total NDE-scale threshold after DMT; one reached it after placebo. A separate 13-person retrospectively selected NDE group supplied the comparison. Table 1's time-item means are scores, not counts of stopped time; no response-category distribution was inspected. The article's described age/gender matching needs qualification because its reported sex counts differ between groups. Small samples, expectancy, order, selection and reporting delay constrain inference. A non-significant difference is not an equivalence result. **CL-T13-011–013; C-T13-001.**

The ordinary interpretation gains an actual example of scale resemblance in a drug context. It gains no established explanation of a particular NDE and no proof that endogenous DMT caused one. The strongest survival interpretation can say that different conditions enable access to a wider reality; it must then supply discriminating evidence beyond their shared phenomenology. Neither interpretation can substitute a total score for a finding about absence of succession. **A-T13-005.**

The inherited Alexander account concerns altered duration, not a new independently verified event or necessarily succession-free awareness. AWARE II's timing and monitoring limitations remain those in reviewed T07; resuscitation is not demonstrated subjectivity beyond irreversible bodily death. Narrating an experience in sequence neither proves that the original awareness was successive nor proves it was timeless. **CL-T13-014–015,020.**

## What would discriminate, and what remains open?

First separate reports of elapsed duration, simultaneous content, no time awareness, no succession and retrospective ordering. Then seek independently timed and scored information with controlled ordinary access and physiological limits. Finally ask what that evidence establishes under each identity criterion. Even a strong anomalous-information result would still require the irreversible-death and identity bridges for H-S.

The nine-component assessment remains qualitative and task-scoped. Changing identity assumptions can change whether a proposed continuation qualifies as H-S without changing the underlying report. Removing retrospective NDE cases leaves the drug-context resemblance and conceptual models, but no established brain independence or post-death subjectivity. H-P can remain while H-S fails. No selected criterion automatically supplies H-F/U/T/I/G/A; no numerical posterior or equal-support conclusion is proposed.
